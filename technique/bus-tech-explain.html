Business / Technique cards / Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks)

Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Business · 4BS1

Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks)

Give one fact about a business idea and then add two further points that develop it; no case is needed.

Command word: ExplainUsually 3 marksAbout 3 minutes.

What Pearson asks

Give one fact about a business idea and then add two further points that develop it; no case is needed.

How the marks are earned

One mark for a correct point (a benefit, way, advantage or reason), then up to two more marks for developing that same point with linked steps that show why or how it matters. Listing three separate points with no development only earns one mark, and a definition alone earns nothing.

Method

  1. Choose ONE point that answers the question exactly (one benefit, one way, one advantage).

    Why: Only one point is credited; the other two marks are for developing it.

  2. State the point in a short sentence.

    Why: This earns the first mark.

  3. Add a first development using 'This means that...'.

    Why: It shows how the point works and earns the second mark.

  4. Add a second development using 'As a result...' or 'which leads to...'.

    Why: It shows the final effect and earns the third mark.

  5. Keep it general; you do not need a business name.

    Why: Explain questions have no context, so time spent on a case is wasted.

Answer frame

One [benefit/way/advantage] is... This means that... As a result,...

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Listing two or three different points instead of developing one.
  • Starting with a definition of the term, which earns no marks.
  • Repeating the same idea in different words instead of adding a new step.
  • Giving a disadvantage when the question asks for an advantage (or the reverse).

Worked illustration

November 2024 · Paper 2 · Q2(e)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (e).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One advantage is that firms in the country can sell to other member countries without paying tariffs on their exports. This means it costs them less to sell abroad, so they can offer lower prices in those markets. As a result their exports are likely to rise, which creates more jobs and income in the country.

Why it earns the marks

The first sentence gives one clear advantage (no tariffs inside the bloc). The second sentence develops it by explaining lower costs and prices. The third adds a further linked effect on exports, jobs and income. One point plus two developments matches the three-mark pattern.
