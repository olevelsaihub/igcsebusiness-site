Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Business · 4BS1

Answer a multiple-choice question

Choose the correct answer from the options given; some test what you know and some need a quick calculation.

Command word: multiple-choiceUsually 1 markAbout 1 minute.

What Pearson asks

Choose the correct answer from the options given; some test what you know and some need a quick calculation.

How the marks are earned

One mark for selecting the single correct option. The wrong options are designed to look sensible, often matching a common misunderstanding or a calculation mistake, so careful reading and checking matter more than speed.

Method

  1. Read the question stem twice and underline key words (non-financial, external, total).

    Why: One small word often decides the answer.

  2. Decide what the answer should be before looking at the options.

    Why: It stops tempting wrong options from leading you astray.

  3. Cross out options you know are wrong and say why to yourself.

    Why: Removing wrong options raises your chance of the right one.

  4. For calculations, work it out on paper and match your answer exactly.

    Why: Wrong options are often the results of common slips.

  5. Mark only one box, clearly; never leave it blank.

    Why: Two boxes or a blank earns no mark, while a guess might.

Answer frame

Key word: ... My answer before looking: ... Ruled out: ... because ... Final choice: ...

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Missing a key word such as 'non-financial', 'external' or 'total'.
  • Choosing an option that is true but does not answer the question asked.
  • In calculations, dividing instead of multiplying or forgetting the quantity.
  • Leaving the question blank or marking two boxes.

Worked illustration

June 2023 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q1(a)(ii)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (a)(ii).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

D Venture capital. Personal savings, retained profit and selling assets all come from inside the business or its owners, so they are internal. Venture capital is money from investors outside the business, so it is external.

Why it earns the marks

The key word 'external' is checked against each option. The three internal sources are ruled out with a reason, leaving only venture capital, which is the one source that comes from outside the business.
