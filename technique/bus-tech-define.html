Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Business · 4BS1

Define a business term (1 mark)

Give the exact meaning of a business term from the course.

Command word: DefineUsually 1 markAbout 1 minute.

What Pearson asks

Give the exact meaning of a business term from the course.

How the marks are earned

One mark for a clear, accurate meaning of the term. The meaning must be precise enough that it could not describe a different term. You do not need the case, an example or a long explanation.

Method

  1. Learn a short, exact definition for every key term in the course.

    Why: Define questions only test recall, so preparation is everything.

  2. Write one sentence that says what the term is, not just what it does for a firm.

    Why: A benefit or example is not a meaning.

  3. Do not use the term itself in your answer (do not explain 'secondary sector' as 'the secondary part').

    Why: Repeating the word does not show understanding.

  4. Check it would not also fit a similar term (for example primary versus secondary sector).

    Why: Vague answers that fit two terms do not get the mark.

Answer frame

[Term] is/means ... (one precise sentence).

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Giving an example instead of a meaning (for example 'a car factory').
  • Repeating the words of the term in the answer.
  • Writing an advantage or disadvantage instead of what the term is.
  • Mixing up similar terms such as primary and secondary sector, or public corporation and public limited company.

Worked illustration

June 2024 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Q3(a)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (a).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The secondary sector is the part of the economy where businesses manufacture or build products by processing raw materials.

Why it earns the marks

It says exactly what the sector is: the making or building stage that changes raw materials into products. This separates it from the primary sector (getting raw materials) and the tertiary sector (services), so it is precise enough for the mark.
