Set out a calculation with working (2 marks)

Use maths on the data you are given to work out an answer, showing your working (or fill in missing values in a table).

Command words: Calculate, Complete the tableUsually 2 marksAbout 2 minutes.

What Pearson asks

Use maths on the data you are given to work out an answer, showing your working (or fill in missing values in a table).

How the marks are earned

Usually one mark for putting the right numbers into the right formula and one for the correct final answer. A correct answer on its own normally gets full marks, but if the answer is wrong, clear working can still save a mark. Follow any instruction about decimal places or units.

Method

  1. Write the formula in words first (for example: selling price = cost + mark-up).

    Why: It shows the examiner your method and helps you choose the right numbers.

  2. Copy the numbers carefully from the case or table into the formula.

    Why: Correct substitution is usually worth a mark even if you slip later.

  3. Work it out step by step with a calculator, writing each line.

    Why: Each written line can earn credit if the final answer goes wrong.

  4. Round only at the end, to the number of decimal places asked for.

    Why: Early rounding can change the final answer.

  5. Add the unit or sign: $, AED, %, :1 or 'times'.

    Why: An answer with the right unit is clear and complete.

Answer frame

Formula: ... = ... ; Working: ... = ... ; Answer: ... (unit, rounded as asked)

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Giving only a final number, so a small slip loses both marks.
  • Using the wrong year's figures or the wrong line of a table.
  • Finding the percentage but forgetting to add it to (or take it from) the original amount.
  • Rounding too early or to the wrong number of decimal places.
  • Leaving out the unit, currency or ratio form.

Worked illustration

June 2019 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q1(e)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (e).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Selling price = cost + mark-up Mark-up = 17% of 252 = 0.17 × 252 = 42.84 AED Selling price = 252 + 42.84 = 294.84 AED (Shortcut: 252 × 1.17 = 294.84 AED)

Why it earns the marks

The formula is written first and the correct figures (252 and 17%) are placed into it, which earns the method mark. The mark-up is then added back to the cost to give the correct selling price with its currency, which earns the answer mark.
