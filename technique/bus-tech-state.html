Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Business · 4BS1

State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Give a short answer, no more than a sentence, using a piece of information about the business in the case (Give tests recall; Identify means reading the answer from a graph or table).

Command words: State, Give, IdentifyUsually 1 markAbout 1 minute.

What Pearson asks

Give a short answer, no more than a sentence, using a piece of information about the business in the case (Give tests recall; Identify means reading the answer from a graph or table).

How the marks are earned

One mark for a correct point that fits the business in the case. For Identify, the mark is for reading the correct value or item from the data. Generic answers that ignore the case usually do not earn the mark when context is asked for.

Method

  1. Underline what is asked (one task, one reason, one benefit) and the business name.

    Why: You need exactly one point about this business.

  2. Look back at the case or data for a matching detail.

    Why: Context is what makes the answer creditworthy.

  3. Write one short sentence that includes the business or its product.

    Why: It shows the point applies to this firm.

  4. For Identify, read the graph or table carefully, including units and the right year.

    Why: A misread value earns nothing.

Answer frame

[Business] ... so that/to ... [case detail].

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Giving a general answer with no link to the business.
  • Writing several points when only one is credited.
  • Misreading a graph or table (wrong year, wrong row, missing units).
  • Answering a different question, for example a drawback instead of a benefit.

Worked illustration

June 2024 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q2(b)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (b).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

To reduce the risk of injury to staff who use machines in Jimmy Choo's shoe factories.

Why it earns the marks

It gives one valid reason in a single sentence and links it to Jimmy Choo's own setting (workers using machines in shoe factories), so it meets both the reason and the context requirement.
