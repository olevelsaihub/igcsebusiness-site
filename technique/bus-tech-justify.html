Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Business · 4BS1

Justify a choice between two options (9 marks)

Write a longer answer that uses the information given to recommend which one of two options a business should choose.

Command word: JustifyUsually 9 marksAbout 9-10 minutes, including a minute to plan.

What Pearson asks

Write a longer answer that uses the information given to recommend which one of two options a business should choose.

How the marks are earned

Marks are split evenly across three skills: using case details for both options (application), building linked chains of reasoning about each option (analysis), and making a clear choice that is supported by weighing up the options (evaluation). Higher levels need both options covered with case facts and a firm recommendation with reasons.

Method

  1. Read both options and the case; note facts that help or hurt each one.

    Why: Application marks come from using the business's own details.

  2. Paragraph on Option 1: one advantage developed in a chain, plus a 'However...' drawback.

    Why: Both sides of each option give material to weigh up.

  3. Paragraph on Option 2: the same pattern.

    Why: Covering both options is needed for a balanced judgement.

  4. Recommend ONE option clearly, giving the main reason it is better for this business.

    Why: Justify asks for a choice; no choice limits the evaluation marks.

  5. Say why the other option is weaker and what your choice depends on.

    Why: Comparing the options is what makes the judgement supported.

Answer frame

Option 1 would let [business]... This means... However,... Option 2 would... This means... However,... I recommend Option [X] because... Option [Y] is less suitable because... This depends on...

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Writing about only the chosen option and ignoring the other.
  • Not making a choice, or choosing both.
  • Making a choice that does not match the reasons given earlier.
  • Using general points that could fit any business instead of case facts.
  • Giving advantages only, with no drawbacks to weigh up.

Worked illustration

June 2022 · Paper 2 · Q2(f)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (f).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Option 1, cost-plus pricing, means LEGO adds a fixed percentage to the cost of making each Princess Leia toy. This makes sure every toy sold covers its production costs, so LEGO earns a profit on each sale and pricing is simple to work out. However, the price ignores what customers are willing to pay, so LEGO might charge less than fans would happily pay and miss out on extra profit.

Option 2, price skimming, means setting a high price when the toy is first launched. Star Wars toys are very popular, so keen fans and collectors are likely to buy early even at a high price. This gives LEGO a large profit margin on each toy, so profit can be high even if fewer toys are sold at first. However, some customers may think the price is too high and wait for it to fall, or buy a cheaper rival toy, so early sales could be lower.

I recommend Option 2, price skimming. The Star Wars brand creates strong demand from loyal fans, so LEGO can earn high profit per toy at launch and can lower the price later to reach more families. Cost-plus pricing is safer but wastes this chance to earn more from a new, popular product. This choice depends on the toy being seen as special enough for fans to pay the higher price.

Why it earns the marks

Both options are covered with LEGO and Star Wars facts (application). Each has a developed chain to profit or sales plus a drawback (analysis). The answer makes one clear choice, explains why the other option is weaker and states what the choice depends on (evaluation).
