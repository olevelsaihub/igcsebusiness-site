Business / Technique cards / Evaluate and reach a supported conclusion (12 marks)

Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Business · 4BS1

Evaluate and reach a supported conclusion (12 marks)

Write a longer answer that uses your business knowledge and the case to reach a judgement that is backed up with reasons.

Command word: EvaluateUsually 12 marksAbout 12-14 minutes, including 1-2 minutes to plan.

What Pearson asks

Write a longer answer that uses your business knowledge and the case to reach a judgement that is backed up with reasons.

How the marks are earned

Marks are split evenly across four skills: knowing and using business terms (knowledge), using case details (application), building linked chains of reasoning (analysis), and weighing up both sides to reach a supported judgement (evaluation). Higher levels need all four done well together, with a balanced conclusion that says what it depends on.

Method

  1. Read the question and note the business, the issue and what you must judge (importance, benefits, factors).

    Why: The conclusion must answer exactly this question.

  2. Paragraph 1: one side, using a key term, a case detail and a full chain of reasoning.

    Why: This one paragraph shows knowledge, application and analysis together.

  3. Paragraph 2: a second point or the other side, built the same way.

    Why: Two developed points give enough depth for the higher levels.

  4. Add 'However...' points that show limits or risks for this business.

    Why: Weighing up the other side is the start of evaluation.

  5. Write a conclusion that gives a clear judgement, the main reason for it, and what it depends on.

    Why: A supported judgement is needed for the top evaluation marks.

  6. Use case facts in every paragraph, including the conclusion.

    Why: A conclusion that could fit any firm is weak evaluation.

Answer frame

[Term] means... For [business], the case shows... This means... which leads to... However,... Overall, I think... because... but this depends on...

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Giving only one side, so there is nothing to weigh up.
  • Repeating earlier points as the conclusion without a reason or a 'depends on'.
  • Sitting on the fence with no clear judgement.
  • Writing general theory with no case facts.
  • Listing many short points instead of developing a few in depth.

Worked illustration

June 2022 · Paper 1 · Q4(c)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (c).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Internal communication is the passing of information between people inside a business, such as the owners and staff. External communication is with people outside it, such as customers and suppliers.

Good internal communication matters to TBTS because staff need to know about the toys it sells, including any new range for young children, and what the business is trying to achieve. If staff understand the products, they can give customers accurate advice in the shop. This means customers are more likely to buy and come back, which raises sales. Staff who are kept informed also feel valued, so they are more likely to stay, which saves TBTS the cost of recruiting and training new workers.

Good external communication with suppliers of toys and wood is also important. If TBTS tells suppliers early about orders and changes in demand, materials and stock are more likely to arrive on time. This means TBTS can keep its shelves full in busy periods, so it does not lose sales to rival toy shops, and a good relationship may help it agree better payment terms.

However, communication only helps if it reaches everyone. If some employees are left out, they may feel ignored, which can cause conflict and lower motivation. Also, good communication cannot fix every problem: if a supplier is unreliable or the toys are too expensive, sales may still fall.

Overall, I think good communication is very important to TBTS, and external communication with suppliers is slightly more important because a small toy business cannot sell what it does not have in stock. However, this depends on how many staff TBTS employs: if it is very small, informal internal communication may already be good enough, so the main focus should be on its suppliers.

Why it earns the marks

Key terms are defined (knowledge) and case details such as toys, wooden materials and suppliers are used throughout (application). Each point is a full chain to sales, costs or stock (analysis). 'However' points and a conclusion with a reason and a 'depends on' give a supported judgement (evaluation).
