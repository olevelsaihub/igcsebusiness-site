Net cash flow and closing balance

Using cash-flow forecast relationships: net cash flow = inflows - outflows; closing = opening + net cash flow; or working back to a missing outflow.

Type 12 Calculations BUS-CALC-07

★★★★☆ · 11 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Gives a cash-flow forecast table (opening balance, inflow, outflow) and asks for net cash flow, closing balance or a missing line.

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Cash inflows are money coming into the business (sales, other income). Cash outflows are money going out (wages, suppliers, rent).
  • Net cash flow = total cash inflows − total cash outflows. It can be negative; show this with a minus sign.
  • Closing balance = opening balance + net cash flow.
  • The closing balance of one month becomes the opening balance of the next month.
  • To find a missing figure, rearrange: cash outflow = total inflows − net cash flow.
  • A cash-flow forecast is a prediction of future cash in and out. Give answers in the currency shown, as whole numbers when the figures are whole.

Before you begin

  • Meaning of cash inflow and outflow
  • Adding and subtracting with negative numbers
  • Reading a table of figures

Learn these types first: Cash-flow forecasts: terms, uses and limits

Specification: 3.2.2

Answer technique: Set out a calculation with working (2 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Add up all inflows and all outflows if there is more than one line of each. Why: Net cash flow uses the totals.
  2. Net cash flow = inflows − outflows; keep the minus sign if outflows are bigger. Why: A negative net flow reduces the balance.
  3. If the closing balance is asked for, add net cash flow to the opening balance. Why: The opening balance is the starting cash.
  4. If a line is missing, rearrange the same relationships to find it. Why: Some questions give the net cash flow and ask for an outflow.
  5. Check which figure the question wants: net cash flow or closing balance. Why: Giving the closing balance when net was asked loses the mark.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice5Options add inflow and outflow, add the opening balance to the outflow, or stop at net cash flow. Work out the requested line first. Use the Multiple-choice card.
Calculate6Use the Calculate card: write net cash flow = inflow − outflow, then closing = opening + net, with the sign and currency.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Adding inflows and outflows together instead of subtracting.
  • Adding the opening balance to the outflow, or forgetting the opening balance.
  • Subtracting the wrong way so the sign is wrong.
  • Giving the closing balance when only net cash flow was asked, or the reverse.

Worked explanation

  1. From Figure 3: opening balance 16 000, cash inflow 22 000, cash outflow 20 000.
  2. Net cash flow = 22 000 − 20 000 = 2 000.
  3. Closing balance = 16 000 + 2 000 = 18 000.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Net cash flow = cash inflow − cash outflow = 22 000 − 20 000 = 2 000. Closing balance = opening balance + net cash flow = 16 000 + 2 000 = €18 000.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1: the net cash flow of 2 000 (or the correct set-up). Mark 2: the closing balance of 18 000. The correct closing balance alone gets both marks.

Calculation check

  • 22000-20000 = 2000
  • 16000+(22000-20000) = 18000
