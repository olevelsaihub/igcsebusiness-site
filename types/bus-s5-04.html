Capital-intensive and labour-intensive production

Production that relies mainly on machinery versus mainly on people.

Type 1 5 Business operations BUS-S5-04

★☆☆☆☆ · 5 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

The words 'capital intensive' or 'labour intensive', or a question asking which job or process relies more on machines or on people.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Main plan: A-Grade Plan · Production methods and scale

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Capital in production means machines, equipment and technology.
  • Labour means the people (workers) who do the work.
  • Capital-intensive production uses more machinery than labour, e.g. a car assembly plant with robots.
  • Labour-intensive production uses more workers than machinery, e.g. a hairdresser, chef or fruit picker.
  • Job production is usually labour intensive; flow production is usually capital intensive.
  • Kaizen and just-in-time are lean production methods, not types of capital or labour intensity.

Before you begin

  • The factors of production: land, labour, capital and enterprise.
  • Everyday examples of jobs done mainly by hand and jobs done mainly by machines.

Specification: 5.2.1 · 5.3.1

Answer technique: Define a business term (1 mark) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Ask: who or what does most of the work here, machines or people? Why: That single question decides the classification.
  2. For a Define, write one sentence that compares the two: more machinery than labour, or more labour than machinery. Why: The mark needs the comparison, not just 'uses machines'.
  3. For multiple choice, rule out options that are lean methods or production types rather than intensity. Why: Distractors often name related but different ideas such as Kaizen or JIT.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Define3Say which input the method relies on more than the other: capital (machinery) versus labour (workers).
multiple-choice2Pick the role or process where machines do most of the work; hand skills like hairdressing are labour intensive.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Saying capital-intensive means 'needs a lot of money' rather than relying on machinery.
  • Writing 'uses machines' with no comparison to the amount of labour used.
  • Reversing the two terms.
  • Choosing Kaizen or JIT in multiple choice because they sound like production processes.

Labour-intensive production is a way of producing that relies more on workers than on machinery and equipment.

How the marks are earned
