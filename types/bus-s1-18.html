Effects of exchange rate changes

How a rise or fall in a currency affects importers, exporters, competitiveness, costs and prices.

Type 14 1 Business activity and influences on business BUS-S1-18

★☆☆☆☆ · 5 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

The question mentions an exchange rate change, appreciation, depreciation, a stronger or weaker currency, or an importer or exporter.

Knowledge

  • An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another, for example how many euros one pound buys.
  • An importer buys goods or materials from another country; an exporter sells goods or services to another country.
  • Appreciation: the home currency gets stronger, so it buys more foreign currency.
  • Depreciation: the home currency gets weaker, so it buys less foreign currency.
  • A stronger currency makes imports cheaper and exports dearer for foreign buyers.
  • A weaker currency makes imports dearer and exports cheaper for foreign buyers, so exporters may sell more.

  • What imports and exports are
  • What costs, prices and profit are

Specification: 1.5.4

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Decide whether the home currency has become stronger or weaker. Why: The direction decides every effect that follows.
  2. Work out what happens to the cost of imports for the business. Why: Importers' costs move with the exchange rate.
  3. Work out what happens to the price foreign customers pay for its exports. Why: Exporters' competitiveness moves with the exchange rate.
  4. Follow each effect to sales, costs, profit or cash flow for this business. Why: Analysis marks come from linked consequences in context.
  5. For Analyse, follow the Analyse card: two developed points using the case's imports and exports. Why: Higher levels need detailed application throughout.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice3Fix the direction first; a stronger home currency means cheaper imports and lower cash outflows for importers.

Analyse1Trace a weaker or stronger currency through both import costs and export prices for the named business, linking each to profit or sales.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Getting the direction wrong, for example saying a weaker currency makes imports cheaper.
  • Only discussing imports or only exports when the business does both.
  • Describing the exchange rate change without linking it to the named business's costs or sales.
  • Mixing up importer and exporter.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Analyse, 6 marks; needs two applied, connected points.
  2. Fix the direction: depreciation means the krona is weaker.
  3. Point 1: imports of materials cost AWG more, so costs and possibly prices rise.
  4. Point 2: exports become cheaper abroad, so overseas demand may rise.
  5. Link each to AWG's profit or sales and note they partly offset each other.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A depreciation means the Icelandic krona (ISK) falls in value, so each krona buys less foreign currency.

First, AWG imports the materials it uses to make its jumpers. After the depreciation, AWG must pay more krona for the same amount of imported material. This raises its cost of making each jumper, so AWG may have to raise its prices, which could put off some customers, or accept a smaller profit on each item it sells.

Second, AWG exports clothing to customers in other countries through its website. With a weaker krona, overseas customers need less of their own currency to buy an AWG jumper, so its clothes become cheaper for them. This can increase demand from abroad, raising AWG's sales revenue from exports.

Therefore the overall impact on AWG depends on which effect is larger: higher import costs or extra export sales. Because AWG both imports and exports, some of its higher material costs may be offset by stronger overseas demand.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): both points are applied to AWG's imports of materials and exports via its website (AO2, 3 marks), and each has a logical chain to costs, prices, demand or revenue (AO3, 3 marks). Total 6.
