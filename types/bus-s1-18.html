Effects of exchange rate changes

A stronger home currency makes imports cheaper and exports dearer to foreign buyers. A weaker home currency makes imports dearer and exports cheaper. Decide the direction first.

4

The method

  1. Stronger or weaker?

    Decide which way the home currency moved.

  2. Imports

    Stronger: cheaper imports. Weaker: dearer imports.

  3. Exports

    Stronger: dearer for foreigners. Weaker: cheaper.

  4. Follow on

    Take it to sales, costs, profit or cash flow of this business.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong direction

    A weaker currency makes imports dearer, not cheaper.

  • ✗
    Half the story

    If it imports and exports, cover both.

  • ✗
    No link

    Name the effect on this business's costs or sales.

  • ✗
    Mixed roles

    An importer buys; an exporter sells.

Written-answer check
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Analyse, 6 marks; needs two applied, connected points.
  2. Fix the direction: depreciation means the krona is weaker.
  3. Point 1: imports of materials cost AWG more, so costs and possibly prices rise.
  4. Point 2: exports become cheaper abroad, so overseas demand may rise.
  5. Link each to AWG's profit or sales and note they partly offset each other.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A depreciation means the Icelandic krona (ISK) falls in value, so each krona buys less foreign currency.

First, AWG imports the materials it uses to make its jumpers. After the depreciation, AWG must pay more krona for the same amount of imported material. This raises its cost of making each jumper, so AWG may have to raise its prices, which could put off some customers, or accept a smaller profit on each item it sells.

Second, AWG exports clothing to customers in other countries through its website. With a weaker krona, overseas customers need less of their own currency to buy an AWG jumper, so its clothes become cheaper for them. This can increase demand from abroad, raising AWG's sales revenue from exports.

Therefore the overall impact on AWG depends on which effect is larger: higher import costs or extra export sales. Because AWG both imports and exports, some of its higher material costs may be offset by stronger overseas demand.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): both points are applied to AWG's imports of materials and exports via its website (AO2, 3 marks), and each has a logical chain to costs, prices, demand or revenue (AO3, 3 marks). Total 6.
