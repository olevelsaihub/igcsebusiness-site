Mass marketing and niche marketing

Selling one product to the whole market versus serving a small specialised segment, and the benefits of each.

Type 1 4 Marketing BUS-S4-24

★☆☆☆☆ · 4 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

The question names mass marketing or niche marketing, asks about targeting a specialised segment, or asks why a business sells to all demographics.

Knowledge

  • Mass marketing means selling the same product to the whole market, aiming at as many customers as possible.
  • Niche marketing means targeting a small, specialised segment of the market with a product made for it.
  • Mass marketing can bring very high sales and lower average costs through large-scale production (economies of scale).
  • Mass marketing usually needs large promotion budgets and faces many competitors.
  • Niche marketing can allow higher prices and strong loyalty, with fewer competitors.
  • Niche markets are small, so total sales are limited and the business depends on one group.

Before you begin

  • Market segments
  • Economies of scale in simple terms
  • Revenue and profit

Learn these types first: Market segmentation and targeting

Specification: 4.2.1 · 4.2.3

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Decide whether the approach is broad (mass) or specialised (niche). Why: Multiple-choice and Define parts test this distinction.
  2. Pick one benefit: for mass, large sales and lower unit costs; for niche, higher prices and loyalty. Why: The first mark is for a valid advantage.
  3. Link the benefit to customers: how many people are reached, or how well the product fits them. Why: This is the first development link.
  4. End with the result: revenue, profit or spread of risk. Why: The last mark needs a business outcome.
  5. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: apply each point to the business's range of customers. Why: Level 3 needs applied chains.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice1Niche = small specialised segment; mass = the whole market. Market share and market analysis are distractors.
Define1Niche marketing: targeting a small, specialised segment of a market.
Explain1One advantage of mass (or niche) marketing, then how it reaches customers, then the effect on sales or profit.
Analyse1Two applied reasons, e.g. more customers and risk spread across groups, each chained to sales stability.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Swapping the meanings of mass and niche marketing.
  • Choosing market share or market analysis as a form of targeting.
  • Giving a definition in the Explain answer instead of an advantage.
  • Saying mass marketing means 'lots of advertising' without the whole-market idea.
  • Not following the advantage through to sales or profit.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one advantage, 3 marks; no definition needed.
  2. Advantage: more sales can be generated.
  3. Expand 1: the product is promoted to a very large number of people.
  4. Expand 2: higher sales raise revenue and can lower unit costs, improving profit.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Mass marketing can generate a very high volume of sales. Because the same product is promoted to the whole market rather than to one small group, a very large number of people become aware of it and may buy it. Selling in such large quantities increases revenue and allows the business to produce on a large scale, which can lower its cost per unit and improve its profit.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the advantage (high sales). 1 mark for the link (promoted to a very large number of people). 1 mark for the result (more revenue and lower unit costs, so better profit).
