Place: distribution channels and e-commerce

Methods of distribution through retailers and e-tailers, selling or taking bookings online through websites and apps, their advantages and concerns, and why place matters.

Type 7 4 Marketing BUS-S4-12

★☆☆☆☆ · 5 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question mentions place, distribution, retailers, e-tailers, e-commerce, selling online, a website or an app for orders or bookings.

Knowledge

  • Place is the part of the marketing mix about how and where customers can buy the product.
  • A distribution channel is the route a product takes from the producer to the customer, e.g. producer to retailer to customer.
  • A retailer is a shop that sells to the final customer; using other retailers lets a producer reach more customers.
  • An e-tailer sells online; e-commerce means buying and selling using the internet, websites or apps.
  • E-commerce can lower costs (fewer shops) and reach customers anywhere, at any time.
  • Concerns about e-commerce or booking apps: set-up and maintenance cost, customers unwilling to download apps, and needing technical skills.

Before you begin

  • The four Ps
  • Fixed costs such as rent
  • What a website and an app are

Specification: 1.4.1 · 1.7.1 · 4.3.3

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Name the channel the case describes: own shop, other retailers, website or app. Why: Answers must start from how this business actually sells.
  2. Pick one advantage or concern: reach, cost, convenience, or set-up costs and customer reluctance. Why: The first mark is for a valid point.
  3. Link it to customers or costs: who can now buy, or which costs rise or fall. Why: This is the first development link.
  4. End with the result: revenue, profit or market share. Why: The final mark needs a business outcome.
  5. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: two points tied to the case's shop, products and customers. Why: Level 3 needs detailed application.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Define1E-tailers: businesses that sell goods or services to customers using electronic systems such as websites.
State1One reason tied to the business, e.g. other retailers let it sell its produce to a wider market.
Explain1One advantage of e-commerce, then why (no shop costs, more customers online), then profit or revenue.
Analyse3Two applied points about the business's shop, website or app, each chained to sales, costs or customer behaviour.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Writing about online advertising instead of selling online.
  • Giving a definition of e-commerce in the Explain answer, which earns nothing there.
  • Treating place as only the shop's physical location and ignoring online channels.
  • Ignoring the cost and customer reluctance concerns when asked about an app.
  • Generic Analyse answers that never mention the business's products.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one advantage, 3 marks; no definition.
  2. Advantage: lower costs.
  3. Expand 1: fewer shops or offices are needed because customers order online.
  4. Expand 2: lower overheads mean more profit from each sale.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Selling through e-commerce can reduce a business's costs. Because customers can order and pay on a website or app, the business does not need to rent and staff as many shops or offices. With lower running costs, more of the money from each sale is kept as profit, so the business's profit can increase.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the advantage (lower costs). 1 mark for why (fewer premises and staff needed). 1 mark for the result (higher profit).
