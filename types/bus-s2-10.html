Written-answer check
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Analyse card: two chains, each applied to Four Fitness and the Marketing Manager job.
  2. Spot context: 14 gym instructors (fitness, not marketing, experts), a third gym opening, a website that cannot take bookings.
  3. Chain 1: wider pool of experienced marketers, so Gianni can choose a specialist for the new gym.
  4. Chain 2: an outsider brings fresh ideas, for example online booking, giving a competitive edge and more members.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Recruiting externally gives Four Fitness a much wider pool of applicants than its own staff. Its 14 employees are highly qualified gym instructors rather than marketing specialists, so advertising outside the business means Gianni can choose someone with real experience of marketing gyms in Italy. This should help the third gym attract members quickly after it opens, so it starts earning revenue sooner.

An external Marketing Manager can also bring new ideas from other businesses. For example, they might develop Four Fitness's website, which currently only gives information, so that members can book activities and personal training online. This could give Four Fitness a competitive edge over other gyms, increasing membership and improving the performance of all three gyms. Although advertising and interviewing will cost Gianni time and money, these benefits could outweigh that cost.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked: AO2 (3) for applying external recruitment to the Marketing Manager role, the instructors and the website; AO3 (3) for two connected chains from wider choice and new ideas to members, revenue and competitiveness. Both chains detailed and in context: Level 3 (5-6).

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Justify · November 2024 · Paper 2 · Q3(e)

Answer plan

  1. Option 1 internal: staff from 820+ hotels already know how Premier Inn hotels and restaurants run, and their skills are known.
  2. Chain: faster, cheaper, less induction, and promotion motivates staff so good service keeps guests returning.
  3. Option 1 drawback: fewer candidates and a second vacancy to fill.
  4. Option 2 agency: wide choice and shortlisting done for Premier Inn; outsiders bring new skills useful for expansion such as Germany; but fees, induction and possible poor fit.
  5. Judgement: choose one; depends on whether the vacancy needs skills that existing employees lack.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Premier Inn should choose internal recruitment (Option 1) for most vacancies, but this depends on the type of job.

Option 1 means filling the vacancy with someone already working in one of Premier Inn's 820-plus hotels in the UK, UAE, India or Germany. Premier Inn already knows this person's skills, attitude and work record, so there is less risk of appointing the wrong person. The employee also understands how a Premier Inn hotel runs, such as how rooms are prepared and how the restaurants serve guests, so they need little induction. This makes recruitment quicker and cheaper, and the post is filled sooner, so the service to guests stays high. Promotion also motivates staff because they can see a career path, which helps Premier Inn keep good employees; good service is what encourages guests to return. However, fewer candidates are considered, and moving one employee leaves another vacancy that must also be filled, which adds cost.

Option 2 means using an employment agency to find someone from outside. The agency advertises and shortlists candidates, so Premier Inn gets a much wider choice and saves its managers' time. An outsider may bring new skills and ideas, for example from other hotel chains, which could help as Premier Inn opens hotels in new countries such as Germany. However, agencies charge a fee, the new person needs a full induction, and they may not fit in with the existing team. Staff who were passed over may feel demotivated, which could lower the service guests receive.

On balance, Option 1 is better. With over 83,000 rooms in several countries, Premier Inn has a large pool of experienced employees, so someone suitable is very likely to work for it already. Internal recruitment is cheaper and quicker, and it supports the good service that brings guests back. However, this depends on the vacancy: if the job needs skills no current employee has, such as knowledge of a new country Premier Inn is entering, an agency may find a better candidate.

Why it reaches the top level: Applies both options to Premier Inn (820 hotels, 83,000 rooms, Germany expansion, service bringing guests back), develops chains for each (known skills to fast, cheap filling and motivation; agency to wider choice and new skills), weighs drawbacks of both and justifies a choice with a 'depends on' condition. Fits the top level for AO2, AO3 and AO4.

Justify · November 2023 · Paper 2 · Q3(e)

  1. Option 1 promote existing staff: they already know how Kraft Heinz production works, so new production lines can be managed quickly.
  2. Chain: less time and cost on advertising and interviews, little training, and promotion motivates staff towards environmental and robotics targets.
  3. Option 1 drawback: smaller choice, fewer new ideas, and the promoted person's old job creates another vacancy.
  4. Option 2 external: wider choice of managers, possibly with robotic production or waste-reduction skills the business needs for its 2025 targets.
  5. Option 2 drawback: CV checks, interviews and induction cost time and money, and the new manager may not know the products or culture.
  6. Judgement: choose one; depends on whether existing staff have the skills for the new technology-based production lines.
