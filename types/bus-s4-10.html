Why marketing matters: needs, loyalty and share

Why marketing matters: satisfying needs, customer relationships and loyalty, market and product orientation, and market share.

Type 12 4 Marketing BUS-S4-10

★★★★★ · 15 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question asks why satisfying customers, loyalty, customer satisfaction, marketing or market share matters, or about market or product orientation.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Stars describe how often this type appeared in past papers; they are not a prediction. 19 past-paper parts belong to this type.

Main plan: A-Grade Plan · Marketing and market research

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Marketing is identifying what customers want and making sure the business provides it at a profit.
  • Satisfying customer needs leads to repeat purchases and word-of-mouth recommendations.
  • Customer loyalty means customers keep buying from the same business instead of rivals; loyalty cards and offers encourage it.
  • Market orientation: the business finds out what customers want first, then makes it. Product orientation: the business makes what it is good at, then tries to sell it.
  • Market share is the percentage of all sales in a market made by one business; high share often brings loyal customers and lower costs per unit.
  • Poor customer service damages the brand and drives customers to competitors.

Before you begin

  • Customer needs and wants
  • Competitors
  • Revenue and market share

Specification: 1.8.1 · 2.5.3 · 4.1.1 · 4.2.1 · 4.2.2 · 4.3.1 · 4.3.4

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Evaluate and reach a supported conclusion (12 marks) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Name the benefit: repeat custom, recommendations, loyalty or a larger market share. Why: The first mark is for a valid benefit.
  2. Explain customer behaviour: satisfied or loyal customers come back and tell others. Why: This first link shows how the benefit arises.
  3. End with the business result: higher revenue, more market share, lower promotion costs, survival against rivals. Why: The final development mark needs a result.
  4. Apply it to the case: name the product the customer would return to buy. Why: State, Outline and Analyse parts must be in context.
  5. For Analyse use the Analyse card; for 12-mark Evaluate use the Evaluate card and weigh cost of marketing against benefits. Why: Longer answers need chains, and Evaluate needs a conclusion.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State7One reason tied to this business, e.g. loyal customers return to book another of its holidays.
Explain4One advantage (repeat custom, market share), then why customers behave that way, then effect on revenue or costs.
Outline2One reason or way applied to the business plus one linked effect, such as returning customers raising revenue.
Analyse4Two applied points about customers in the case, each chained to sales, reputation or competitiveness.
Evaluate2Weigh the gains of marketing or loyalty against its costs and limits for this business, then conclude.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Writing 'customers will be happy' without carrying the chain to sales or share.
  • Mixing up market orientation and product orientation.
  • Defining market share in an Explain answer instead of giving an advantage.
  • Giving a general reason in State or Outline without the business's product.
  • Listing several advantages briefly instead of developing one.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one advantage, 3 marks.
  2. Advantage: repeat custom.
  3. Expand 1: loyal customers keep buying from this business rather than rivals.
  4. Expand 2: steady sales raise revenue and market share.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One advantage of having loyal customers is repeat custom. Because loyal customers trust the business, they keep coming back to buy from it again and again rather than shopping with its competitors. This gives the business a steady flow of sales, which increases its revenue and can increase its market share.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the advantage (repeat custom). 1 mark for the link (customers return instead of going to rivals). 1 mark for the result (higher revenue and market share).

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Evaluate · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define marketing and market share; note PPS faces a rival pet hotel for the first time.
  2. Chain 1: promoting its four pet specialists and all-in-one hotel, grooming and shop builds awareness and loyalty, protecting market share.
  3. Chain 2: questionnaires reveal customer needs so PPS can improve before the rival opens, leading to repeat bookings and word of mouth in the village.
  4. Use Figure 4: each dog treat contributes €1.25, so 800 a month cover €1,000 fixed costs; marketing to existing customers helps reach this.
  5. Limits: small budget and only seven rooms; a cheaper rival may still win customers; marketing fails if care quality slips.
  6. Judgement: very important but depends on the rival's prices and on PPS keeping its care standards high.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Marketing is identifying what customers want and making sure the business provides it at a profit. It includes market research, promotion, pricing and building relationships with customers. Market share is the percentage of all sales in a market made by one business.

Marketing is important to PPS because it is about to face competition for the first time. Until now, pet owners in Alhaurin el Grande had no other local pet hotel, so PPS had the whole local market. When the rival opens, customers will have a choice, so PPS must give them reasons to stay. By promoting what makes it different, such as its team of four pet specialists and having overnight stays, grooming and a pet shop in one place, PPS can build brand awareness. Customers who understand these benefits are more likely to stay loyal, which protects its market share and revenue.

Marketing also helps PPS find out what customers need. Rachel could ask existing customers for feedback with short questionnaires. If they want, for example, longer grooming hours, PPS can change its service before the rival opens. Meeting needs better than the competitor leads to repeat bookings and word-of-mouth recommendations, which matter a lot in a village. Marketing could also support the new dog treats, sold at €4.50 with a variable cost of €3.25. Each treat contributes €1.25, so PPS must sell 800 a month to cover fixed costs of €1,000. Promoting the treats to existing hotel and grooming customers would help reach this level.

However, marketing has limits. It costs time and money, and PPS is small, with room for only seven dogs or cats, so its budget is limited. Marketing cannot solve everything: if the rival charges lower prices or has newer facilities, some customers may switch anyway. Marketing also only works if the service is good; if a pet is poorly cared for, bad word of mouth will undo any promotion. And with only seven rooms, PPS may often be full, so attracting many new hotel customers adds little; keeping current customers matters more.

Overall, marketing is very important for PPS because, for the first time, customers have a choice, and keeping them loyal is the main way to protect its market share. However, how important it is depends on what the rival offers, especially its prices, and on PPS continuing to give excellent care. PPS should focus its marketing on keeping existing customers rather than on expensive promotion.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines marketing and market share (AO1), uses first-time competition, four specialists, seven rooms, village setting and Figure 4 (break-even of 800 treats) (AO2), develops chains to loyalty, needs-based changes and word of mouth (AO3), weighs cost, rival pricing and service quality, then gives a conditional judgement (AO4).

Evaluate · June 2022 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q4(c)

  1. Define marketing (identifying and meeting customer needs profitably), including market research and promotion.
  2. Chain 1: market research finds what chocolate buyers want (e.g. a new flavour or ice cream), so the new product fits demand and sales rise.
  3. Chain 2: marketing builds on loyalty to well-known brands such as Dairy Milk, so loyal customers are more willing to try new products and recommend them.
  4. Use case facts: founded 1842, sold worldwide, part of Mondelez (gross profit $10.3m on revenue $25.9m) so it can fund campaigns.
  5. Counter-points: strong brand may sell the product with little marketing; bad publicity or a poor product harms the brand; offers and free gifts add cost.
  6. Judgement: importance depends on how new or different the product is and how strong competition is in that market.
