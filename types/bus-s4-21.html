The method

  1. Name the tool

    Targeted ads, social media, e-newsletter or website.

  2. Say why useful

    Targeting, low cost, speed or reach.

  3. Link to sales

    Repeat purchase, loyalty or new customers.

  4. Both sides

    Reviews can spread good or harmful news.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Just "good"

    Always give a reason such as targeting.

  • ✗
    Ignoring harm

    Social media risks include false reviews.

  • ✗
    Defining

    Explain a benefit, not what it is.

Written-answer check
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one advantage, 3 marks.
  2. Advantage: adverts reach people who are likely to be interested.
  3. Expand 1: less money is wasted, making it cheaper than many traditional methods.
  4. Expand 2: more viewers go on to buy, increasing sales.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Targeted advertising online lets a business show its adverts to the people most likely to be interested, for example those who have recently searched for or bought similar products. Because fewer adverts are wasted on people who would never buy, the business gets more value for the money it spends than with many traditional methods. This means a larger share of the people who see the advert go on to buy, which increases sales.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the advantage (reaches likely buyers). 1 mark for the link (less waste, better value than traditional methods). 1 mark for the result (more viewers buy, sales rise).

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Evaluate · June 2019 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Name technologies beyond the existing website: social media, email newsletters, apps, targeted online ads.
  2. Benefit 1: targeted ads match each brand to its customers (L.table organic fine dining, L.café cakes, L.bistro Asian food) -> less wasted spend, more sales.
  3. Benefit 2: newsletters/apps -> repeat purchases and cross-selling between bakery, café, bistro and restaurant; customer photos give free promotion and feedback.
  4. Counter: digital skills and staff cost; ads ignored; negative comments spread fast; formal L.table customers may prefer personal service.
  5. Judge: significant benefits, social media most useful for visual food; depends on skilled staff and customers' social media use; combine with website and in-store promotion.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Lavender already promotes its products on a website. Other forms of technology include social media such as Facebook or Instagram, email newsletters, mobile apps and online adverts that target people by their interests and location.

The first benefit is reaching a wider and more targeted audience at low cost. Lavender runs several different businesses: bakery, patisserie and takeaway shops; L.café, a self-serve café; L.bistro, casual Asian dining; and L.table, a formal restaurant using high-quality organic ingredients. Each attracts a different type of customer. Social media adverts can be aimed at people by age, location and interests, so Lavender can show L.table's organic menu to people interested in fine dining and L.café's cakes to people who enjoy snacks. Targeted promotion wastes less money than general adverts, so more of the people who see it are likely to buy, raising sales.

The second benefit is loyalty and repeat purchases. With an email newsletter or app, Lavender can send customers news of new cakes or special offers. A customer of the bakery could be encouraged to try L.bistro or L.table. Because Lavender owns several outlets, moving customers between them increases the total spent by each customer. Social media also lets customers share photos of food and leave comments, giving free word-of-mouth promotion and quick feedback on new products.

However, there are drawbacks. Lavender would need staff with digital skills to create content and reply to comments, which adds costs. Many people ignore emails and online adverts, so results are not guaranteed. Negative comments spread quickly online and could harm the brand. Also, L.table is a formal restaurant whose customers may respond better to personal service and traditional promotion than to online adverts.

Overall, the benefits are significant because Lavender has several outlets aimed at different customers, and technology lets it target each group cheaply and link them together to increase repeat visits. Social media is likely to be the most useful, because it is low cost and visual, which suits cakes and food. The size of the benefit depends on Lavender having skilled staff to manage the accounts and on how many of its customers actually use social media. Lavender should combine these methods with its website and in-store promotion rather than replacing them.

Why it reaches the top level: Names and explains technology methods beyond the existing website; applies to Lavender's bakery, L.café, L.bistro and L.table and their different customers; chains to targeted reach, cross-selling, repeat sales and feedback; weighs skills, cost and reputation risks; judgement with a 'depends on' condition. Meets Level 3 on AO1-AO4.
