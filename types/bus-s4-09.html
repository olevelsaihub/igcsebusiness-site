Branding and why it matters

What a brand is and why a strong brand matters for recognition, loyalty and pricing.

Type 3 4 Marketing BUS-S4-09

★★★☆☆ · 9 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question uses the word brand or branding, or asks why a business's name or logo matters.

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • A brand is a name, logo, symbol or design that identifies a business's products and makes them different from competitors' products.
  • Branding helps customers recognise the product quickly.
  • A strong brand builds customer loyalty: customers keep choosing it.
  • A strong brand can allow a higher price because customers trust its quality.
  • A well-known brand makes promoting new products easier and more likely to succeed.
  • A brand can become very valuable to a business over time.

Before you begin

  • What a competitor is
  • Customer loyalty
  • Price and profit

Specification: 4.3.4

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Say what makes the brand recognisable: its name, logo or image. Why: This shows you understand what branding is.
  2. Choose one reason it matters: stands out from rivals, builds loyalty, supports a higher price. Why: The first mark is for a valid reason.
  3. Chain the reason to customer behaviour and then to sales, price or survival. Why: Development marks need two linked steps.
  4. Use case facts: the business's products, rivals nearby, how long it has traded. Why: State, Outline and Analyse parts need context.
  5. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: two applied benefits, each with a logical chain. Why: Level 3 needs detailed application throughout.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Define2A name, logo or design that identifies a product and distinguishes it from competitors' products.
State3One reason for this business, e.g. it makes its shop stand out from the other flower shops nearby.
Explain1One reason, then how customers respond, then the result such as loyalty or a higher price.
Outline1One reason applied to the business plus one linked effect, e.g. customers recognise the logo so it stands out from rivals.
Analyse2Two benefits applied to the business's own products and rivals, each chained to sales, price or long-term success.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Defining branding as just advertising rather than an identifying name, logo or design.
  • Giving a definition in an Explain answer, which earns no marks there.
  • Saying branding 'makes it popular' without a chain to sales or price.
  • Not naming the business or its products in State and Outline answers.
  • Confusing branding with public relations or general promotion.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one reason, 3 marks; do not write a definition.
  2. Reason: branding makes products stand out from competitors.
  3. Expand 1: customers can recognise and pick out the brand they want.
  4. Expand 2: they keep choosing it, so loyalty and repeat sales grow.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Branding is important because it makes a business's products stand out from those of its competitors. When customers can quickly recognise the name or logo and know what quality to expect, they can easily find and choose the brand they want. Over time they keep choosing it rather than trying rivals, which builds customer loyalty and gives the business more repeat sales.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (differentiates the products). 1 mark for the link (customers recognise and pick out the brand). 1 mark for the result (loyalty and repeat sales).
