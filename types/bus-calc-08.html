Change between two values, absolute or percentage

Finding the change between an earlier and a later figure, either as an absolute difference in original units or as a percentage increase or decrease of the original.

Type 10 Calculations BUS-CALC-08

★★★★★ · 16 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Gives two figures for different times (years, before and after an offer) and asks for the increase, decrease or percentage change.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Main plan: A-Grade Plan · Percentages and change

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Absolute change = new value − old value, in the original unit (tonnes, dollars, customers).
  • Percentage change = (new value − old value) ÷ old value × 100.
  • Always divide by the ORIGINAL (earlier) value, not the newer one.
  • A negative result is a decrease. If the question says 'percentage decrease', give the size of the fall as a positive percentage.
  • Questions usually ask for 2 decimal places. Keep full calculator figures until the last step.
  • Units in thousands or millions do not matter for a percentage, as long as both figures use the same unit.

Before you begin

  • Subtracting large numbers
  • Converting a fraction to a percentage
  • Rounding to 2 decimal places

Answer technique: Set out a calculation with working (2 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Identify the old (earlier) value and the new (later) value from the right row and the right years. Why: Tables often show more rows and years than you need.
  2. Subtract: new − old. Why: This is the absolute change.
  3. If a percentage is needed, divide the change by the OLD value and multiply by 100. Why: A percentage change is measured against the starting point.
  4. Round to the places asked and say increase or decrease. Why: Marks need the exact rounding.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice10Options divide by the new value, divide new by old without subtracting, or give the difference as a percentage. Use the Multiple-choice card.
Calculate8Use the Calculate card: show (new − old) ÷ old × 100 with the numbers, then the percentage to 2 decimal places, or the absolute change with units.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Dividing the change by the new value instead of the original value.
  • Dividing the new value by the old value without subtracting first.
  • Giving the simple difference as if it were a percentage.
  • Using the wrong row or years from the table.

Worked explanation

  1. Old value (2020–2021) = 85 083 thousand rupees. New value (2021–2022) = 92 322 thousand rupees.
  2. Change = 92 322 − 85 083 = 7 239.
  3. Percentage change = 7 239 ÷ 85 083 × 100 = 8.508…%.
  4. To two decimal places: 8.51%. It is an increase.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Increase = 92 322 − 85 083 = 7 239. Percentage increase = 7 239 ÷ 85 083 × 100 = 8.51%.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1: the difference 7 239 (or putting it over the original value). Mark 2: 8.51% to two decimal places. The correct percentage alone earns both.

Calculation check

  • 92322-85083 = 7239
  • (92322-85083) ÷ 85083 × 100 = 8.51
