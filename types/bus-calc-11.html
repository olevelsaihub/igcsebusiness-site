Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Business · 4BS1

Increase or decrease a value by a percentage

Raising or reducing a base figure by a stated percentage: growth, wage rises, bonus or interest added, and promotional, group or employee discounts or percentage cuts taken off.

Type 16 Calculations BUS-CALC-11

★★★★★ · 24 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Gives a starting figure and a percentage rise, growth, tax, interest, discount or cut, and asks for the new price, wage, total or amount.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Stars describe how often this type appeared in past papers; they are not a prediction. 32 past-paper parts belong to this type.

Main plan: A-Grade Plan · Percentages and change

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Increase by x%: new value = old value × (1 + x/100). A 3.5% rise means × 1.035.
  • Decrease by x%: new value = old value × (1 − x/100). A 12.5% discount means × 0.875.
  • Or in two steps: find x% of the value, then add it (increase) or subtract it (decrease).
  • A discount is a reduction in price. A tax added to cost, a pay rise, interest added to a loan and growth are increases.
  • If several items or people are bought, find the full total first, then apply the percentage once.
  • Money answers are usually to 2 decimal places. Counts of things (vehicles, litres) are whole numbers.

Before you begin

  • Finding a percentage of an amount (BUS-CALC-10)
  • Converting percentages to decimals
  • Rounding to 2 decimal places

Learn these types first: Find a percentage of an amount

Answer technique: Set out a calculation with working (2 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Find the base: multiply price × number of items or people if needed. Why: Discounts and rises apply to the total.
  2. Decide if it is an increase or a decrease. Why: This decides add or subtract.
  3. Find the percentage amount: base × rate ÷ 100. Why: This is the size of the change.
  4. Add it for an increase or subtract it for a decrease (or multiply by 1 ± rate). Why: The question wants the new total.
  5. Round as asked and add the unit. Why: Rounding is part of the final mark.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice6Options stop at the percentage amount, add the percentage as a plain number, double the discount, or apply it to only one item. Use the Multiple-choice card.
Calculate26Use the Calculate card: show the percentage amount (or the multiplier), then the new total, with currency and rounding.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Stopping at the percentage amount and not adding or subtracting it.
  • Adding the percentage as a number (325 + 25) instead of the percentage amount.
  • Applying the discount to only one item, or forgetting to multiply by the number of items.
  • Adding when it should be a decrease, or using the wrong percentage.

(e) Calculate the new hourly minimum wage after the increase of 3.5%. You are advised to show your working. (2)   

Your answer

Work it out on paper, then type your final answer. Give it to 2 decimal places.

Final-value check
€
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Old wage = €7.45 per hour. Increase = 3.5%.
  2. 3.5% of 7.45 = 7.45 × 0.035 = 0.26075, about €0.26.
  3. New wage = 7.45 + 0.26 = €7.71.
  4. Check with the multiplier: 7.45 × 1.035 = 7.71075, which is €7.71 to 2 decimal places.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Increase = 7.45 × 0.035 = €0.26. New hourly minimum wage = 7.45 + 0.26 = €7.71.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1: the increase of €0.26 (or 7.45 × 1.035 set up). Mark 2: the new wage of €7.71. The correct wage alone earns both.

Calculation check

  • 7.45 × 0.035 = 0.26
  • 7.45+round(7.45 × 0.035,2) = 7.71
  • 7.45 × 1.035 = 7.71
