Training: induction, on-the-job and off-the-job

Written

Training teaches staff the skills for their job. Induction welcomes new staff. On-the-job happens while working. Off-the-job happens away from the workstation.

  2. Give a point

    One benefit or drawback that fits that type.

  3. Link on

    Skills, or cost and lost time, to output or service.

    Which job, and where does training happen?

    Compare both methods and choose.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Explain card: one disadvantage plus two linked expansions.
  2. Disadvantage: training costs the business money.
  3. Expansion 1: course or trainer fees, plus wages paid while staff are learning rather than working.
  4. Expansion 2: higher costs and lower output while training, so profit can fall in the short term.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One disadvantage is that training costs the business money. The business may have to pay for an outside course or an experienced employee's time to run the training, and it still pays the trainees' wages while they are learning instead of working. This raises costs and, because less work gets done during the training, output or service levels can fall, which may reduce profit in the short term.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the disadvantage (cost). 1 mark for explaining where the cost comes from (fees, trainer time, wages while not working). 1 mark for the consequence: reduced output and lower short-term profit.

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Justify · June 2023 · Paper 1 · Q3(e)

Answer plan

  1. Option 1 on-the-job: learns Irsi's handmade chocolate and jelly fruit methods while producing -> meets higher demand from longer hours and deliveries, low cost.
  2. Limit 1: only 3 employees and 4 directors -> trainer's output falls; early mistakes waste ingredients.
  3. Option 2 off-the-job: college course -> new techniques and ideas, efficiency; no staff time lost.
  4. Limit 2: fees and time away from the shop; not specific to Irsi recipes.
  5. Choose on-the-job for a small shop needing quick output; depends on someone having time to train.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

On-the-job training means the new employee learns while working in the shop, shown by an experienced worker. At Irsi Chocolatier the new employee could watch and copy how the business makes its handmade chocolates and jelly fruit sweets. Because the employee produces while learning, output rises straight away. This matters because Irsi is expanding its delivery service and opening hours and expects demand to increase. Training in the shop is also cheap, with no course fees or travel, and the employee learns Irsi's own recipes and standards, which protects quality in a city where many other chocolate shops are nearby.

However, on-the-job training needs someone to teach. Irsi has only three employees and four directors. While an experienced worker trains the newcomer, that worker produces less, and mistakes made while learning could waste ingredients.

Off-the-job training means learning away from the workplace, for example on a college course in chocolate making. The employee could learn new techniques from expert trainers and bring back fresh ideas, such as faster production methods, which could raise efficiency over time. Existing staff would not lose working time to teaching.

However, off-the-job training costs more, because Irsi must pay course fees and the employee is away from the shop rather than producing. It is also less specific: a general course will not teach Irsi's own handmade recipes, so some in-shop training would still be needed.

I recommend Option 1, on-the-job training. Irsi is a small family business that needs the new worker to start producing quickly to meet the extra demand from longer opening hours and more deliveries. On-the-job training is cheaper and teaches the exact methods that give Irsi its handmade quality. This depends on one of the experienced employees or directors having enough time to train the newcomer properly. If the expansion leaves everyone too busy, a short off-the-job course followed by in-shop practice would be the better choice.

Why it reaches the top level: Both training methods applied to Irsi facts (handmade chocolates, 3 employees + 4 directors, expansion of delivery and hours, nearby rivals); chains to output, cost and quality; limitation for each; justified recommendation with a 'depends on' condition. Meets Level 3 for AO2, AO3 and AO4.

Justify · June 2022 · Paper 2 · Q3(e)

  1. Option 1 on-the-job: staff learn in one of LEGO's 15 factories from colleagues -> keep producing, low cost, learn LEGO's exact methods.
  2. Limit 1: trainers are taken away from their own work; may pass on bad habits; few new ideas.
  3. Option 2 off-the-job: courses with external experts -> new skills and methods across 18,000 staff -> fewer errors, better products for brands like Star Wars and Harry Potter.
  4. Limit 2: course fees and lost working time; general courses may not teach LEGO-specific skills.
  5. Judge for a large, skilled global manufacturer that values staff development; depends on the type of skill: firm-specific -> on-the-job, new technology/management -> off-the-job.

Justify · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q3(e)

  1. Option 1 induction: new staff in many countries learn Jimmy Choo's rules, brand values (JC logo, award-winning designs) and workplace -> feel part of the firm -> supports the aim of fewer leavers.
  2. Limit 1: short and general; does not teach the skills to make shoes, handbags or perfume.
  3. Option 2 off-the-job: experts teach specialist production skills -> fewer errors, quality fit for a luxury brand bought by celebrities.
  4. Limit 2: expensive across many countries; staff away from work; may not match the exact job.
  5. Judge: induction first, because the stated aim is motivation and retention of new staff; depends on whether new staff already have the craft skills.
