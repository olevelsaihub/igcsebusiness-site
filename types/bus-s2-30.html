Span of control, chain of command and delegation

Roles and responsibilities: span of control, chain of command and delegation, including judging how well a wide or narrow span works, with effects on supervision, decisions and motivation.

Type 11 2 People in business BUS-S2-30

★★★☆☆ · 10 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question mentions span of control (wide, narrow or short), chain of command, layers between managers and workers, a manager delegating tasks, or how well a manager's span works.

Knowledge

  • Span of control is the number of workers a manager is directly responsible for.
  • A narrow (short) span means few workers per manager: close supervision but more managers and more layers.
  • A wide span means many workers per manager: less supervision, more trust and fewer managers.
  • Chain of command is the line of authority that instructions pass down, from the top of the business to the workers.
  • A short chain of command means fewer levels, so messages pass faster and are less likely to be distorted.
  • Delegation means a manager passes authority to carry out a task to a subordinate (someone lower in the structure).
  • Delegation frees the manager's time and can motivate staff who feel trusted.
  • A wide span can empower staff and speed up decisions, but a manager with too many staff may not supervise everyone well, so productivity can fall.

Specification: 2.1.1 · 2.5.1 · 2.5.2

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Define the term accurately in your own words. Why: Define questions test the precise meaning.
  2. Decide whether the case shows a wide or narrow span, or a long or short chain. Why: Effects depend on which version applies.
  3. State one benefit or reason. Why: This earns the identification mark.
  4. Link it to supervision, speed and accuracy of communication, decision-making or motivation. Why: These are the consequences examiners credit.
  5. Use the case's managers and jobs in State and Outline answers. Why: Answers must be in context.
  6. For Analyse, follow the Analyse card: use the case numbers (staff per manager, number of sites) to give one gain and one problem of that span. Why: Judging effectiveness needs both effects applied to this manager.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State3One reason or benefit linked to the business, for example a Premier Inn manager gaining time to greet guests by delegating restocking.
Define4Span of control: number of staff one manager directly controls. Delegation: passing authority for a task down to a subordinate.
Explain3One benefit or reason, then two linked steps through fewer levels, faster or clearer messages, and fewer errors or quicker decisions.
Outline1One reason plus one context link, for example messages about recycling reach staff quickly because they pass through few levels.
Analyse1Judge the named manager's span with case facts: direct contact and quicker decisions versus too many staff to supervise, each traced to motivation or productivity.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Confusing span of control (how many people one manager controls) with chain of command (how many levels).
  • Writing a definition when the question asks for a benefit; definitions earn no marks in Explain.
  • Describing delegation as giving work without any authority or trust.
  • Stopping at 'communication is better' without showing the effect on errors or decision speed.
  • Calling a wide span simply good or bad without using the case facts, such as how many staff or sites one manager runs.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain card: one benefit plus two linked expansions; no definition marks.
  2. Benefit: messages pass through fewer levels of management.
  3. Expansion 1: instructions go more directly, so they are less likely to be changed or misunderstood.
  4. Expansion 2: problems are sorted and decisions acted on faster, with fewer errors.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

With a short chain of command, messages pass through fewer levels of management. Because instructions travel more directly from senior managers to workers, they are less likely to be changed or misunderstood along the way. This means problems can be sorted out and decisions put into action more quickly, so fewer mistakes are made.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the benefit (fewer levels for messages). 1 mark for explaining that messages are less likely to be distorted. 1 mark for the consequence: quicker problem-solving and decisions, fewer mistakes.
