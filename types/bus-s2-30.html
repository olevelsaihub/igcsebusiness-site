Knowledge card

Span of control, chain of command and delegation

Written

Span of control is how many workers a manager directly manages. Chain of command is the line of authority down the levels. Delegation passes authority for a task to someone lower down.

The method

  1. Define it

    Say precisely what the term means.

  2. Wide or narrow?

    Decide which version the case shows.

  3. State one effect

    Give one benefit or problem.

  4. Link it

    Connect to supervision, speed, accuracy or motivation.

  5. Use the case

    Quote the staff per manager or the number of levels.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Span or chain

    Span is people per manager. Chain is the number of levels.

  • ✗
    Definition only

    A definition alone scores nothing in Explain.

  • ✗
    Delegation without trust

    Delegation passes authority, not just extra work.

  • ✗
    Wide is not always good

    Judge it with the case facts, not by habit.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain card: one benefit plus two linked expansions; no definition marks.
  2. Benefit: messages pass through fewer levels of management.
  3. Expansion 1: instructions go more directly, so they are less likely to be changed or misunderstood.
  4. Expansion 2: problems are sorted and decisions acted on faster, with fewer errors.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

With a short chain of command, messages pass through fewer levels of management. Because instructions travel more directly from senior managers to workers, they are less likely to be changed or misunderstood along the way. This means problems can be sorted out and decisions put into action more quickly, so fewer mistakes are made.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the benefit (fewer levels for messages). 1 mark for explaining that messages are less likely to be distorted. 1 mark for the consequence: quicker problem-solving and decisions, fewer mistakes.
