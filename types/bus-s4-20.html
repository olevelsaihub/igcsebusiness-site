Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Business · 4BS1

Promotion methods: above and below the line

Advertising, sponsorship, product trials and special offers; above- and below-the-line techniques; and choosing promotion for a segment.

Type 8 4 Marketing BUS-S4-20

★★★★★ · 19 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question names a promotion method (advertising, sponsorship, special offers, product trials, leaflets) or above/below the line, or offers two promotion options.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Stars describe how often this type appeared in past papers; they are not a prediction. 22 past-paper parts belong to this type.

Main plan: A-Grade Plan · Product life cycle, pricing, promotion and mix choices

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Promotion means communicating with customers to make them aware of a product and persuade them to buy it.
  • Above the line promotion uses mass media to reach a large audience: television, radio, newspapers, posters and billboards.
  • Below the line promotion is more direct and targeted: special offers, coupons, product trials and free samples, sponsorship, leaflets, loyalty schemes.
  • Sponsorship means paying towards an event or organisation so that the business's name is shown, raising brand awareness.
  • Product trials let customers try a product before buying, and give the business feedback to fix problems before a wider launch.
  • Promotion costs money; the right method depends on the target customers, the budget and the message.

Before you begin

  • Market segments and target customers
  • Branding
  • Revenue, costs and profit

Specification: 4.2.3 · 4.3.1 · 4.3.4

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Evaluate and reach a supported conclusion (12 marks) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Justify a choice between two options (9 marks) · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks)

Method

  1. Classify the method: above the line (mass media) or below the line (direct, targeted). Why: Definitions and Explain questions often specify the type.
  2. Match the method's reach and cost to the target customers in the case. Why: Application marks need the business's own customers.
  3. Link it to awareness or persuasion, then to sales or repeat purchase. Why: This is the chain that earns the development marks.
  4. Give a limitation: cost, short-term effect, or no guarantee people will buy. Why: Justify and Evaluate answers need a drawback for each option.
  5. For a 9-mark Justify, follow the Justify card: develop both methods, add a 'however' to each, then choose using case facts. Why: The top marks need a reasoned choice.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Define1Above the line: promotion through mass media, such as TV or billboards, reaching a very large audience.
Explain7One method or reason, then how it reaches or persuades customers, then the result for sales or loyalty.
Outline2One method for the business plus one linked effect, e.g. a poster that encourages visitors to book tours.
Analyse1Two applied methods or benefits, each chained to new or returning customers and revenue.
Justify10Develop both promotion options with case facts and a drawback each, then choose and say why it fits better.
Evaluate1Weigh the reach and impact of several methods against cost for the target customers; reach a judgement.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Classing special offers or sponsorship as above the line, or TV adverts as below the line.
  • Defining sponsorship in an Explain answer instead of explaining a benefit.
  • Saying a method 'increases sales' with no step showing how customers are reached.
  • In Justify, ignoring the cost of the method or the business's budget.
  • Recommending online methods when the type asks about traditional promotion (online belongs to the technology type).

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one advantage, 3 marks.
  2. Advantage: increases brand awareness.
  3. Expand 1: the name and logo are seen by everyone at or watching the event.
  4. Expand 2: followers may choose the brand, raising sales revenue.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One advantage of sponsorship is that it increases awareness of the business's brand. When a business pays towards an event, such as a sports tournament or a local show, its name and logo are displayed to everyone attending or watching, which may be a local, national or even global audience. People who follow the event become familiar with the brand and may choose to buy from that business, which increases its sales revenue.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the advantage (brand awareness). 1 mark for the link (name and logo seen by the event's audience). 1 mark for the result (followers buy, raising revenue).

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Justify · November 2025 · Paper 2 · Q2(f)

Answer plan

  1. Option 1 celebrity sponsorship: fans copy idols, so demand rises; Samsung can use different celebrities across its 74 countries.
  2. Chain: builds a fashionable brand image that helps win market share from Apple and Xiaomi.
  3. Option 1 drawback: very expensive and a celebrity scandal can damage the brand.
  4. Option 2 free game app: added value at purchase may make young buyers choose Samsung; cheap to give away.
  5. Option 2 drawback: only affects people already buying, many care more about camera or battery, and rivals can copy it.
  6. Judgement: choose one; depends on choosing celebrities with a clean reputation who match target customers.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Samsung should choose to sponsor celebrities (Option 1), but this depends on choosing the right celebrities.

Option 1 means paying well-known people to use and be seen with Samsung products. Samsung competes with Apple and Xiaomi for market share, and celebrities have huge numbers of fans. When fans see their idols using a Samsung smartphone, many will want the same phone, so demand rises. Because Samsung operates in 74 countries, it can choose celebrities who are popular in each region, so the message reaches different target markets. This builds a brand image of Samsung as fashionable, not just technical. That matters because Samsung invests heavily in new technology and needs high sales to recover that cost. However, celebrity fees are very expensive, and if the celebrity is involved in a scandal, Samsung's reputation could be damaged and sales could fall.

Option 2 means giving customers a free game app when they buy a product. This is a below-the-line special offer that adds value at the point of sale. It may persuade young people who enjoy gaming to choose Samsung instead of Apple or Xiaomi, and it is cheaper than sponsorship because an app costs little to give away once it is made. However, it only influences people who are already about to buy a device; it does little to raise awareness among new customers. Many buyers care more about the camera, battery life or price than about a free game, and rivals could easily copy the offer.

On balance, Option 1 is better. Samsung wants to increase market share against strong rivals, and celebrity sponsorship reaches a far larger audience and builds a long-term brand image, while a free game is a small extra that is easy to copy. However, this depends on Samsung choosing celebrities with a clean reputation who match its target customers; if the risk of scandal is high, the free game app is the safer, lower-cost choice.

Why it reaches the top level: Applies both options to Samsung (rivals Apple and Xiaomi, 74 countries, heavy technology investment, aim of higher market share), develops chains for each (fans copying idols to higher demand; added value to switching brands), weighs scandal risk, cost and copying, and justifies a choice with a 'depends on' condition. Meets top-level AO2-AO4.

Evaluate · November 2023 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define advertising; list Kraft Heinz methods: television, posters, billboards, supermarket leaflets.
  2. Chain 1: Ed Sheeran TV advert attracts young fans who copy their idol, bringing new customers to Heinz tomato sauce.
  3. Chain 2: posters, billboards and in-store leaflets reach older shoppers and repeat a short message at the point of purchase across its 24+ brands.
  4. Use Figure 3: gross profit $1,984m on revenue $6,554m (about 30%) shows it can afford many methods.
  5. Counter-points: high cost, no guarantee a celebrity advert raises sales, hard to know which method worked, Heinz already well known.
  6. Judgement: important because customers are varied, but depends on extra sales outweighing extra cost; track results.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Advertising is a form of promotion that uses media to tell customers about products and persuade them to buy. Kraft Heinz uses a variety of methods, including television, posters, billboards and leaflets in supermarkets.

Using different methods is important because Kraft Heinz's target customers are very varied. Its products are bought by young and old people, and it has over 24 brands, from tomato sauce and baked beans to Mac&Cheese. One method cannot reach everyone. The 2019 television advert with Ed Sheeran was likely to attract younger people who follow his music. They may try Heinz tomato sauce because they want to copy someone they admire, which increases sales and brings new young customers to the brand. Older customers may be reached by posters and billboards as they travel. Posters repeat a short, simple message many times, which keeps the brand in people's minds. Leaflets in supermarkets reach shoppers at the moment they choose what to buy, which may persuade them to pick Heinz over a rival brand.

A mix of methods also spreads risk: if one method works poorly, others still support sales. As the fifth largest food and drinks business in the world, Kraft Heinz can afford this. Figure 3 shows gross profit of $1,984 million on revenue of $6,554 million in three months, a gross margin of about 30%, so it earns enough to fund large campaigns.

However, using many methods is expensive. An advert with a star such as Ed Sheeran costs a lot, and there is no certainty it increases sales; people may enjoy the advert without changing what they buy. It is also hard to measure which method worked. If sales rise, Kraft Heinz may not know whether it was the television advert, the posters or the leaflets, so some money may be wasted. Posters can be ignored or damaged. Also, Heinz is already very well known, so extra advertising may add less than it would for a new brand.

Overall, using a variety of methods is important for Kraft Heinz because its customers range from young to old across many brands, and each method reaches a different group. However, its importance depends on whether the extra sales are greater than the extra cost. Kraft Heinz should track sales after each campaign and spend more on the methods that work best for each target group.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines advertising (AO1), uses the Ed Sheeran advert, young and old buyers, 24 brands, posters, billboards, leaflets and Figure 3 (30% gross margin) (AO2), develops chains from each method to its target group and sales (AO3), weighs cost, uncertain effect and difficulty measuring results, and judges with a clear 'depends on' (AO4).

Justify · November 2020 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Q2(f)

  1. Option 1 free bite-sized samples: passers-by taste Loaf's bread and cakes, so they may come in to buy or book a cookery course.
  2. Chain: staff can talk about Loaf's social aims (Waste Not, recycling hub, restoring the high street), building goodwill and loyal local customers.
  3. Option 1 drawback: free food and staff time cost money for a social enterprise with nine staff.
  4. Option 2 posters in charity shops: cheap or free display that stays up for a long time and links with Loaf's charity work.
  5. Option 2 drawback: may reach only charity-shop visitors, not the customers most likely to pay for courses.
  6. Judgement: choose one; depends on how much Loaf can afford and whether its target customers pass the shop.

Justify · June 2021 · Paper 2 · Q2(f)

  1. Option 1 targeted online advertising: adverts go to people who have shown interest (e.g. visited or signed up to NEXT's website), so spending is not wasted.
  2. Chain: NEXT can track clicks and purchases to see which adverts work and adjust them.
  3. Option 1 drawback: does not reach customers who have not shown interest online.
  4. Option 2 e-newsletters: very low cost once set up, can include pictures and special offers for all subscribers.
  5. Option 2 drawback: no guarantee customers open or read them; may be treated as junk mail.
  6. Judgement: choose one; depends on whether NEXT mainly wants new customers (targeted ads) or repeat buying from existing ones (newsletters).
