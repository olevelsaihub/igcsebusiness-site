Franchising: franchisor and franchisee

What a franchise is; the roles of franchisor and franchisee; benefits and drawbacks to each side, including support, an established brand, fees and royalties, rules and loss of control, and franchising as a way to expand.

Type 6 1 Business activity and influences on business BUS-S1-36

★★☆☆☆ · 7 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question mentions a franchise, franchisor or franchisee, buying the right to use a brand, or a business expanding by letting others run outlets under its name.

Knowledge

  • A franchise is when one business lets another person or business trade under its name, sell its products and use its way of working.
  • The franchisor owns the brand and sells the right to use it; the franchisee buys that right and runs the outlet.
  • The franchisee pays a set-up fee at the start and usually ongoing royalties, often a percentage of sales or profit.
  • Benefits to a franchisee: a brand customers already know, training and support, a business idea that is already proven, so less risk of failure.
  • Drawbacks to a franchisee: fees reduce profit, there is no guaranteed wage, and it must follow the franchisor's rules and buy from approved suppliers.
  • Benefits to a franchisor: fast growth using the franchisees' money, fee and royalty income, and its brand spreading to new areas.
  • Drawbacks to a franchisor: less control over quality, and one badly run outlet can damage the whole brand.

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Evaluate and reach a supported conclusion (12 marks) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Decide whose side the question takes: the franchisor (brand owner) or the franchisee (buyer). Why: Benefits differ for each side, and mixing them up loses marks.
  2. Pick one clear feature or benefit, such as support and training, an established brand, or fee income. Why: An accurate feature earns the first mark.
  3. Chain it to an outcome: lower risk of failure, more customers, more revenue or faster growth. Why: Explain and Analyse marks come from linked consequences.
  4. For Analyse, follow the Analyse card: two benefits applied to the named business using case facts. Why: Higher levels need context throughout.
  5. For Evaluate, follow the Evaluate card: weigh benefits against fees, rules, lost control or brand risk, then judge for this business. Why: Judgement needs both sides and a supported conclusion.

How each command word is answered

multiple-choice4Franchisees get support and an established brand; they cannot choose any supplier, are not paid a set wage, and do not have complete control.
Define1Franchise: an arrangement where a business lets another operator trade using its name and methods.
Explain1One advantage plus two linked steps, for example support from the franchisor, fewer mistakes, lower risk of failure. No marks for a definition.
Analyse1Answer from the brand owner's side when the case is the franchisor: fee income and wider reach, each applied to its products.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Mixing up franchisor (who owns the brand) and franchisee (who buys the right to use it).
  • Thinking a franchisee has complete control, a set wage or a free choice of suppliers.
  • Giving franchisee benefits when the case asks about benefits to the brand owner, such as IKEA or Ocean Vibes.
  • Listing benefits without linking them to risk, revenue or growth.
  • In Evaluate, giving only benefits with no drawback or conclusion.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Explain one advantage of being a franchisee, 3 marks; no definition needed.
  2. Pick one advantage: buying into a business that is already proven.
  3. Link it: the franchisor gives a known brand, training and advice.
  4. Add the consequence: the risk that the new business fails is lower.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One advantage of being a franchisee is that you run a business idea that has already been shown to work. The franchisor provides a well-known brand along with training and advice on how to run the outlet, so the franchisee does not have to learn everything by trial and error. As a result, the new business is less likely to fail, because customers already recognise the brand and costly mistakes are avoided.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the advantage (a proven business idea). 1 mark for the link (training, advice and a known brand from the franchisor). 1 mark for the consequence (lower risk of failure). Total 3.

Evaluate · November 2025 · Paper 1 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define franchise: franchisor lets franchisees use the Ocean Vibes name and methods for an initial fee plus royalties.
  2. Benefit 1: expand into other Portuguese resorts without buying boats or hiring staff itself (wage rose 3.6% from €820) -> lower costs -> faster growth, profit protected.
  3. Benefit 2: fee income + wider brand awareness in a crowded boat-tour market -> more bookings and money for wildlife research (goal: tourism + science).
  4. Counter: loss of control; a careless franchisee harms wildlife or service -> bad website reviews hurt all resorts; only fees received; may struggle to find franchisees.
  5. Judge: beneficial overall for quick low-cost growth; depends on careful franchisee selection, strict environmental and service rules.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A franchise is an arrangement where the franchisor, here Ocean Vibes, lets other people or businesses (franchisees) run a business using its brand name and methods. In return the franchisee pays an initial fee and usually a regular royalty, often a percentage of sales.

The first benefit is faster growth at low cost. At the moment Ocean Vibes runs tours in only two resorts. Franchisees would provide the boats, staff and premises in other Portuguese holiday resorts, so Ocean Vibes can expand without buying new boats or hiring more tour guides and sales teams itself. This matters because the minimum wage in Portugal rose by 3.6% in 2024 from €820 a month, so employing more staff directly is becoming more expensive. Because the franchisees carry most of these costs and risks, Ocean Vibes can increase its number of locations quickly while keeping its own costs low, which protects its profit.

The second benefit is a new income stream and a stronger brand. Each franchisee pays fees, so Ocean Vibes earns revenue from resorts it does not run itself. This money could be spent on its wildlife research, helping it achieve its main goal of linking tourism and science. More boats carrying the Ocean Vibes name across Portugal also makes the brand better known. In a market with many competing boat tour firms, a familiar brand can help tourists choose Ocean Vibes, increasing bookings. More franchise tours could also collect more research data.

However, there are drawbacks. Alfredo and Pedro would lose some control. Ocean Vibes' reputation depends on guides who are knowledgeable, passionate and respectful of the environment. If a franchisee cuts corners, for example by disturbing the dolphins or giving poor service, customers may post bad reviews on the Ocean Vibes website. That damage would hit every location, including the two original resorts. Ocean Vibes also receives only fees, not the full profit from franchise tours. And because competition is strong, it may be hard to find franchisees willing to pay the fees.

On balance, franchising is beneficial for Ocean Vibes because it allows quick, low-cost expansion into new resorts and brings fee income that can fund its research goal at a time when wage costs are rising. However, the benefit depends on Ocean Vibes choosing franchisees carefully and setting strict rules and training on environmental care and service quality. If it cannot control standards, the risk to its reputation could outweigh the extra revenue.

Why it reaches the top level: Accurate franchise terms (franchisor, fee, royalty); applied throughout to two resorts, Portugal, 3.6% wage rise, reviews, research goal; two developed chains to cost, revenue, brand and profit; counter-arguments on control and reputation; balanced judgement with a clear 'depends on' condition. Meets Level 3 on AO1-AO4.
