Market segmentation and targeting

Dividing a market by location, demographics, lifestyle, income or age, and using segments to target customers.

Type 6 4 Marketing BUS-S4-11

★☆☆☆☆ · 5 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Look for segment, segmentation, target market, demographics, or dividing customers by age, income, location or lifestyle.

Knowledge

  • A market segment is a group of customers who share similar characteristics.
  • Market segmentation means dividing a market into these groups.
  • Common bases: location, age, income, lifestyle and demographics (characteristics of the population such as age, gender and family type).
  • Targeting means choosing which segment(s) to aim products and promotion at.
  • Segmentation lets a business design a product, price and promotion that fit one group's needs.
  • It avoids wasting money promoting to people who are unlikely to buy.

Before you begin

  • Customer needs
  • The marketing mix
  • Income and age as features of customers

Specification: 4.2.3

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Evaluate and reach a supported conclusion (12 marks) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Pick the basis that splits this business's customers in a useful way (age, income, location, lifestyle). Why: A sensible basis is the starting point for State and Analyse parts.
  2. Describe the needs of one segment, using case facts. Why: Application marks need the business's real customers.
  3. Link targeting to a better-fitting product, price or promotion. Why: This is the first development link.
  4. End with the result: more sales, more loyalty or less wasted spending. Why: The final mark rewards a business outcome.
  5. For a 12-mark Evaluate, follow the Evaluate card: weigh benefits against the cost of serving many segments or missing others. Why: Evaluation needs both sides and a conclusion.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State2One reason for this business, e.g. it can aim different toys at different age groups.
Define1Demographics: the characteristics of a section of the population, such as age or family type.
Explain1One benefit, then how the business fits its mix to the segment, then more sales or loyalty.
Analyse1Two ways the business could segment its real customers, each chained to a decision about price or offer.
Evaluate1Weigh the gains of targeting chosen groups against costs and the risk of ignoring others; conclude for this business.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Confusing segmentation with market research.
  • Naming a basis such as age but not saying how the business would use it.
  • Writing about niche marketing when the question asks about segmenting.
  • Generic answers that ignore the business's actual customers in State and Analyse.
  • Evaluate answers that list segments with no judgement.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one benefit, 3 marks.
  2. Benefit: the business can find groups of customers with similar characteristics.
  3. Expand 1: it can design a product or offer that meets that group's needs.
  4. Expand 2: those customers are more likely to buy and return, raising sales and loyalty.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Market segmentation allows a business to identify groups of customers who share similar characteristics, such as the same age group or income level. The business can then design a product, set a price and choose promotion that match exactly what that group wants, instead of trying to please everybody at once. Because the product suits their needs better, those customers are more likely to buy it and to come back again, so sales and customer loyalty increase.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the benefit (identifies groups with similar characteristics). 1 mark for the link (product and mix designed for their needs). 1 mark for the result (more sales and loyalty).

Evaluate · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define demographic segmentation (age, family stage, income) and targeting.
  2. Life stage: designs for couples setting up home vs parents refurnishing children's rooms -> closer fit -> sales and repeat buying.
  3. Income: affordable flat packs (Kamprad's aim) for price-sensitive groups; ranges adjusted by country through franchised stores -> volume.
  4. Stock: better targeting helps control inventory (€4.3bn 2019 -> €3.7bn 2020, about 14% fall) -> lower storage cost.
  5. Counter and judge: tastes vary within groups, wide brand appeal; very important but best combined with lifestyle/geographic segmentation; depends on how different groups' needs are by country.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Market segmentation means dividing a market into groups of customers with similar needs. Demographic segmentation uses features such as age, family size, stage of life and income. A business then targets each group with suitable products, prices and promotion.

Demographic segmentation is important to IKEA because furniture needs change with family stage. The case shows IKEA changing bedroom furniture designs for a couple setting up home and for parents refurnishing their children's bedroom. A young couple may want affordable, simple furniture for a first home, while parents need safe, hard-wearing beds and storage for children. By designing for each group, IKEA meets their needs more closely, so customers are more likely to buy and to return as their family grows. This supports IKEA's aim of knowing the tastes and demands of its customers.

Segmenting by income also matters. Ingvar Kamprad wanted to make furniture people could afford, and flat packs keep transport costs and damage low. By targeting price-sensitive groups such as young families, IKEA can keep prices low and attract large numbers of buyers, giving high sales volume. Because incomes differ between countries, its franchised stores can choose ranges and prices that suit local buyers.

Demographic segmentation can also help IKEA manage stock. If it knows which groups buy which products, it can order the right amounts. IKEA's inventory fell from €4.3 billion in 2019 to €3.7 billion in 2020, a fall of about 14%. Better targeting can keep unsold stock low, which reduces storage costs and frees cash.

However, demographic segmentation has limits. People of the same age or income do not all share the same taste; lifestyle and personal style also matter. A high-income couple may still choose low-cost flat packs. IKEA's tutorials and workshops are open to everyone, and its brand appeals to a very wide market, so focusing too narrowly could miss customers. Customers also may not buy what their group is expected to buy.

Overall, demographic segmentation is very important to IKEA because its products are closely linked to life stage and budget, and it helps IKEA offer the right furniture at the right price in each country. However, it works best alongside other methods, such as lifestyle and geographic segmentation. Its importance depends on how different the needs of each group really are in each country; where tastes are similar, one low-price range for everyone may be enough.

Why it reaches the top level: Accurate segmentation terms; applied to IKEA's couple and children's bedroom designs, affordable flat packs, franchised stores, tutorials, and inventory €4.3bn to €3.7bn (about 14% fall); chains to sales, loyalty and stock costs; balanced limits; judgement with a 'depends on' condition. Meets Level 3 on AO1-AO4.
