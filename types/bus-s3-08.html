Why cash matters and how to improve cash flow

Written

Cash pays suppliers, overheads and wages. A business can make a profit but still run out of cash if customers pay late, and then risk insolvency. Bring cash in sooner or send it out later.

3 cash facts to find

Find why a profit can still mean a cash shortage.

  1. Costs paid now
  2. Profit is 3 000
  3. Customers pay late

Costs paid now

Use
Suppliers, rent and wages must be paid in cash.

Profit is 3 000

Working
12 000 − 9 000 = 3 000
Warning
Profit is not cash in the bank.

Customers pay late

Result
Only 4 000 arrived but 9 000 went out: a 5 000 shortage.
Risk
Unpaid wages or suppliers can lead to insolvency.

4 cash facts to find

Decide which actions improve cash flow.

  1. Pay later: helps
  2. Longer credit: hurts
  3. Less stock: helps
  4. Insolvency

Pay later: helps

Why
Cash stays in the bank longer.
Bonus
Paying on time builds trust.

Longer credit: hurts

Why
Customers pay later, so cash comes in later.
Trap
A common wrong option.

Less stock: helps

Why
Less money is tied up in unsold goods.

Insolvency

Meaning
Unable to pay debts when due; may close.

The method

  1. Name a payment

    Suppliers, overheads or wages.

  2. Use the case

    Say what that payment is for this business.

  3. Trace the chain

    What if it cannot be paid? Lost supplies, unhappy staff, insolvency.

  4. Test any action

    Does it bring cash in sooner or send it out later?

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Cash is not profit

    Delaying payments helps cash, not profit.

  • ✗
    Customers pay later

    This slows cash coming in.

  • ✗
    A definition

    Say why cash matters, not what it is.

  • ✗
    No development

    Three bare reasons earn one mark.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one reason, 3 marks: one reason plus two linked steps.
  2. Choose the reason: paying on time builds a trusting relationship with suppliers.
  3. Link 1: a trusted customer is more likely to get help, e.g. fast deliveries or credit.
  4. Link 2: the result: the business keeps stock available and does not lose sales.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Paying suppliers on time helps a business build a good, trusting relationship with them. Because the supplier knows it will be paid, it is more willing to help when the business needs extra stock quickly or wants to buy on credit. This means the business is less likely to run out of the goods it sells, so it does not lose sales to competitors.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (a trusting relationship with suppliers). 1 mark for the first link (supplier more willing to help with urgent deliveries or credit). 1 mark for the second link (stock stays available, so sales are not lost).
