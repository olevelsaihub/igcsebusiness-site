Why cash matters and how to improve cash flow

Why cash is needed to pay suppliers, overheads and staff, how shortages lead to insolvency, and ways to improve cash flow.

Type 4 3 Business finance BUS-S3-08

★★★☆☆ · 9 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question asks why cash is important, what insolvency means, what happens if bills cannot be paid, or which action would improve cash flow.

Knowledge

  • Cash is the money a business actually has available to spend now, in the bank or in hand.
  • Cash flow is the movement of money into the business (inflows, e.g. from customers) and out of it (outflows, e.g. to suppliers).
  • A business needs cash to pay suppliers, overheads (regular running costs such as rent and electricity) and employees' wages.
  • Insolvency is when a business cannot pay its debts when they are due; it can lead to the business closing.
  • Cash and profit are different: a profitable business can still run out of cash if customers pay late.
  • Cash flow can be improved quickly by paying suppliers later, getting customers to pay sooner, or cutting stock levels.
  • Paying suppliers on time builds trust, which helps the business get credit and good service in future.

Before you begin

  • What suppliers, employees and overheads are
  • The idea of a debt (money owed)
  • What profit means

Specification: 1.8.2 · 3.2.1 · 3.2.2

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks)

Method

  1. Name a payment the business must make in cash: suppliers, overheads or staff. Why: Every credited answer starts from a real use of cash.
  2. Use the case to say what that payment is for this business, e.g. the named staff or supplies. Why: Analyse and Outline answers need the business's own context.
  3. Trace what happens if the payment cannot be made: lost supplies, unhappy staff, legal action, insolvency. Why: The chain of consequences earns the development marks.
  4. For improving cash flow, ask whether the action brings cash in sooner or sends it out later. Why: This test removes wrong multiple-choice options such as allowing customers to pay later.
  5. For 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: two applied points, each developed into a chain about survival or reputation. Why: Top level needs detailed, connected chains in context.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Define1Insolvency: being unable to pay debts when they fall due. Keep it to one clear sentence.
multiple-choice3Pick the option that brings cash in sooner or delays cash out; profit, interest or relationships are distractors.
Outline1Give one reason cash matters and link it to a payment named in the case.
Explain1One reason, then two linked steps showing the effect on the business, e.g. supplier trust leading to reliable or cheaper supply.
Analyse3Apply to the business's own costs (staff, rent, supplies) and build chains to what happens if cash runs out, ending in survival or failure.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Confusing cash with profit, e.g. saying that delaying payments increases profit.
  • Choosing 'allow customers to pay later', which slows cash coming in.
  • Giving a definition of cash instead of a reason it matters, which the schemes do not reward.
  • Listing three reasons with no development, capped at one mark.
  • Writing general points in Analyse without using the named business's costs.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one reason, 3 marks: one reason plus two linked steps.
  2. Choose the reason: paying on time builds a trusting relationship with suppliers.
  3. Link 1: a trusted customer is more likely to get help, e.g. fast deliveries or credit.
  4. Link 2: the result: the business keeps stock available and does not lose sales.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Paying suppliers on time helps a business build a good, trusting relationship with them. Because the supplier knows it will be paid, it is more willing to help when the business needs extra stock quickly or wants to buy on credit. This means the business is less likely to run out of the goods it sells, so it does not lose sales to competitors.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (a trusting relationship with suppliers). 1 mark for the first link (supplier more willing to help with urgent deliveries or credit). 1 mark for the second link (stock stays available, so sales are not lost).
