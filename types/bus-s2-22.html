Why motivating and keeping staff matters

The importance of motivation: attracting and retaining employees, productivity, and reasons for paying above the minimum wage.

Type 7 2 People in business BUS-S2-22

★★★★☆ · 14 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question asks why a business motivates or retains staff, the importance of motivated employees, or why it pays above the minimum wage.

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Motivated employees work harder, make fewer mistakes and give better customer service.
  • Retaining staff means keeping employees so they do not leave the business.
  • Staff turnover is the rate at which employees leave and have to be replaced; high turnover is costly.
  • Replacing staff costs time and money for advertising, interviewing and training.
  • Experienced staff who stay build up product knowledge and skills they can pass on to customers and colleagues.
  • Paying above the minimum wage can attract better applicants, reward highly skilled staff and stop staff moving to competitors.
  • Higher productivity and better service can lead to more sales and higher profit.

Before you begin

  • What motivation and productivity mean
  • What the minimum wage is
  • Recruitment and training costs

Specification: 2.2.4 · 2.4.1 · 2.4.2

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Give one reason: higher productivity, better service, lower staff turnover or attracting skilled staff. Why: This earns the identification mark.
  2. Explain how motivation or retention leads to that effect. Why: Development marks need a clear link.
  3. Carry the chain to costs, sales or profit for the business. Why: The business consequence completes the chain.
  4. In State and Analyse questions, use the jobs and products in the case. Why: Answers must be in the context of the named business.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State7One reason using the business, for example Starbucks avoiding the need to train new staff to make its coffee.
Explain3One benefit or reason, then two linked steps through turnover, productivity or service to costs or sales.
Analyse4Two chains in context, such as motivated waiters giving better service so customers return, and staff staying so recruitment costs fall.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Explaining how to motivate (bonus, job rotation) when the question asks why motivation matters.
  • Writing 'staff work harder' and stopping without a business effect.
  • Giving a reason in a State question that is not about the named business.
  • Listing several benefits with no explanation, which earns only one mark.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain card: one benefit plus two linked expansions.
  2. Benefit: motivated staff are more likely to stay, so staff turnover is lower.
  3. Expansion 1: the business avoids repeated advertising, interviewing and training.
  4. Expansion 2: money saved can be used elsewhere, and experienced staff give better service.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Motivated employees are more likely to stay with the business, so its staff turnover is lower. This means the business does not have to keep advertising vacancies, interviewing applicants and training replacements. As a result it saves time and money that can be used elsewhere, and experienced staff who stay can give customers a better service, helping to increase sales.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the benefit (staff stay, lower turnover). 1 mark for explaining the recruitment and training avoided. 1 mark for the consequence: cost and time saved, used elsewhere, with better service.
