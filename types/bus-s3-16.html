Revenue and profit: meaning and how to raise them

Meaning of revenue and profit, why profit is important, and what drives revenue, profit and margins.

Type 2 3 Business finance BUS-S3-16

★★★★☆ · 12 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question asks what revenue or profit means, why profit matters, why revenue differs, or how a business could raise profit or its margin.

Knowledge

  • Revenue is the money a business receives from selling its goods or services over a period: price multiplied by the number sold.
  • Profit is what is left when total costs are taken away from total revenue; if costs are bigger, the business makes a loss.
  • Gross profit is revenue minus cost of sales (the cost of the goods sold); the gross profit margin is gross profit as a percentage of revenue.
  • Profit can rise by increasing revenue (sell more, or raise the price if customers keep buying) or by cutting costs.
  • Each method has a trade-off: a higher price may lose customers; cutting costs may lower quality.
  • Profit matters because it rewards owners and shareholders, can be reinvested to grow, and shows lenders the business is doing well.

Before you begin

  • Fixed and variable costs (BUS-S3-15)
  • Price multiplied by quantity
  • Basic percentages

Specification: 3.3.1 · 3.4.1 · 3.5.1

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks)

Method

  1. Separate the three levers: price, number sold, and costs. Why: Every method of raising revenue or profit works through one of these.
  2. Pick one lever and say exactly what the business does, e.g. find a cheaper supplier. Why: The identification mark needs a specific method.
  3. Show how it changes revenue or costs, holding the other things the same. Why: The development marks need the link to the profit gap.
  4. For why profit matters, link profit to reinvestment, rewards for owners or attracting investors. Why: These are the uses the schemes credit.
  5. For 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: two applied points with chains using the business's figures or plans. Why: Higher levels need context throughout.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Analyse1Use the case: show how profit funds this business's plans, rewards its owners or shareholders, and supports its position against rivals.
Define7Revenue: income from selling goods or services. Profit: total revenue minus total costs.
multiple-choice1Profit is what remains after all costs; gross profit only subtracts cost of sales; inflows are cash, not profit.
Outline1Give one reason and tie it to a fact from the case, such as the type of shop or number of customers.
Explain4One method, then two links showing how it widens the gap between revenue and costs.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Defining profit as money coming in, which is revenue.
  • Saying 'increase sales' with no method or link to costs.
  • Saying a higher price always raises profit, ignoring lost customers.
  • Listing several methods without explaining one, capped at one mark.
  • Confusing gross profit with profit after all costs.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one method, 3 marks: one method and two linked steps.
  2. Method: reduce costs by finding a cheaper supplier.
  3. Link 1: each item costs less, so the gap between price and cost grows.
  4. Link 2: if sales and price stay the same, total costs fall while revenue does not, so profit rises.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A small business could increase its profit by buying its stock from a cheaper supplier. This lowers the cost of each item it sells, so the gap between what it pays for an item and the price it charges gets bigger. If it keeps selling the same number of items at the same price, its revenue stays the same while its total costs fall, so its profit increases.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for identifying the method (cut costs via a cheaper supplier). 1 mark for the first link (lower cost per item widens the gap). 1 mark for the second link (same revenue with lower costs means higher profit).
