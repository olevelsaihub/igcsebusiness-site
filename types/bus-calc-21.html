Labour productivity: output per hour

Calculating productivity as output divided by labour input (hours or workers).

Type 3 Calculations BUS-CALC-21

★☆☆☆☆ · 1 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

Asks for productivity, or output per hour or per worker, and gives total output and hours or workers.

Knowledge

  • Productivity measures how efficiently inputs are turned into output.
  • Labour productivity = total output ÷ labour input (hours worked or number of workers).
  • The unit matters: 'smartphones per hour' or 'units per worker'.
  • Higher productivity means more output from the same labour, which can lower the cost per unit.

Before you begin

  • Meaning of output
  • Division

Specification: 5.2.1

Answer technique: Set out a calculation with working (2 marks)

Method

  1. Find total output. Why: This is the top of the division.
  2. Find the labour input (hours or workers). Why: This is what output is measured against.
  3. Divide output by labour input and state the unit. Why: Productivity is output per unit of labour.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Dividing hours by output (upside down).
  • Leaving out the unit such as 'per hour'.
  • Multiplying output by hours instead of dividing.

Worked explanation

  1. Output = 1 000 smartphones. Time = 20 hours.
  2. Productivity = 1 000 ÷ 20 = 50.
  3. Answer with unit: 50 smartphones per hour.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Labour productivity = output ÷ hours = 1 000 ÷ 20 = 50 smartphones per hour.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1: 1 000 ÷ 20 set up. Mark 2: 50. The correct answer alone earns both.
