Product life cycle and extension strategies

The phases of the product life cycle and strategies to extend it, such as new features, markets or promotion.

Type 2 4 Marketing BUS-S4-04

★★★☆☆ · 9 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

The question mentions the product life cycle, a phase (introduction, growth, maturity, decline), a numbered phase on a diagram, or extending a product's life.

Knowledge

  • The product life cycle is the set of phases a product passes through from launch until it is withdrawn.
  • The phases in order are introduction (phase 1), growth (phase 2), maturity (phase 3) and decline (phase 4).
  • Sales are low at introduction, rise quickly in growth, level off in maturity and fall in decline.
  • An extension strategy is an action taken to stop sales falling and make a product last longer, usually used in maturity.
  • Examples of extension strategies: new packaging, new features or flavours, lower prices, new markets or customers, and more advertising.
  • Star, cash cow, question mark and dog belong to the Boston matrix, not the product life cycle.

Before you begin

  • What a product is
  • Reading a simple sales-over-time line
  • The idea of sales revenue

Specification: 4.3.1

Answer technique: Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. If a phase is asked for, recall the order: introduction, growth, maturity, decline. Why: Multiple-choice parts ask for a phase by number or name.
  2. Choose one extension strategy: new packaging, new feature, lower price, new market or more promotion. Why: The first mark is for a valid strategy.
  3. Show how the strategy works for customers: it makes the product feel new or attracts new buyers. Why: This is the first linked expansion.
  4. End with the effect on sales: sales rise again and the product earns revenue for longer. Why: The final mark is for the effect on sales or revenue.
  5. For State and Outline, apply it to a named product in the case, such as one of the shop's own lines. Why: Answers out of context are not accepted.

State2One extension strategy applied to a product named in the case, such as redesigning one of its items.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Mixing up life cycle phases with Boston matrix categories such as star or dog.
  • Getting the phase order wrong, for example putting maturity before growth.
  • Listing several extension strategies instead of explaining one.
  • Naming a strategy without saying how it brings sales back up.
  • Giving a general strategy in State or Outline without naming the business's product.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one way, 3 marks: one strategy and two linked expansions.
  2. Choose one strategy: changing the packaging.
  3. Expand 1: how it changes the way customers see the product.
  4. Expand 2: the effect on sales during maturity or decline.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One way to extend the product life cycle is to change the product's packaging, for example giving it a brighter, more modern design or adding something extra inside the pack. The new look makes the product seem fresh again, so existing customers are reminded of it and new customers notice it on the shelf. This leads to more repeat purchases and new buyers, so sales rise again instead of falling into decline.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the strategy (change packaging). 1 mark for how it is done or how customers react (fresh, modern look gets noticed). 1 mark for the result (repeat purchases and new customers lift sales).
