Product life cycle and extension strategies

Written

A product moves through introduction, growth, maturity and decline. An extension strategy is an action, usually in maturity, that keeps sales from falling so the product lasts longer.

4

The method

  1. Know the order

    Introduction, growth, maturity, decline.

  2. Pick one strategy

    New pack, feature, flavour, price, market or more promotion.

  3. Say how it works

    It makes the product feel new or reaches new buyers.

  4. End on sales

    Sales rise again, so revenue lasts longer.

  5. Use the product

    Name the case product, not 'a product'.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Boston words

    Star and dog are not life cycle phases.

  • ✗
    Wrong order

    Maturity comes after growth.

  • ✗
    List, not explain

    One strategy, developed, beats three named.

  • ✗
    No effect on sales

    Say how sales are brought back up.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one way, 3 marks: one strategy and two linked expansions.
  2. Choose one strategy: changing the packaging.
  3. Expand 1: how it changes the way customers see the product.
  4. Expand 2: the effect on sales during maturity or decline.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One way to extend the product life cycle is to change the product's packaging, for example giving it a brighter, more modern design or adding something extra inside the pack. The new look makes the product seem fresh again, so existing customers are reminded of it and new customers notice it on the shelf. This leads to more repeat purchases and new buyers, so sales rise again instead of falling into decline.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the strategy (change packaging). 1 mark for how it is done or how customers react (fresh, modern look gets noticed). 1 mark for the result (repeat purchases and new customers lift sales).
