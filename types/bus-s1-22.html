Effects of interest rate changes

What an interest rate is and how a rise or fall affects business borrowing costs, investment, and consumer spending.

Type 10 1 Business activity and influences on business BUS-S1-22

★★★★☆ · 11 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question mentions interest rates rising or falling, bank lending rates, or the cost of loans and their effect on a business or its customers.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Stars describe how often this type appeared in past papers; they are not a prediction. 12 past-paper parts belong to this type.

Main plan: A-Grade Plan · Location, going global, currencies and interest rates

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • An interest rate is the percentage charged for borrowing money, or paid as a reward for saving it.
  • When interest rates rise, loans and overdrafts cost more to repay, so business costs rise.
  • Higher rates also mean customers with loans have less money to spend, and saving becomes more attractive, so demand can fall.
  • When interest rates fall, borrowing is cheaper, so businesses may invest and expand, and customers may spend more.
  • Businesses selling expensive or non-essential items are usually hit hardest by higher rates.

Before you begin

  • What a loan and an overdraft are
  • What costs, revenue and profit are

Specification: 1.6.3

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Evaluate and reach a supported conclusion (12 marks) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks)

Method

  1. Fix the direction: are interest rates rising or falling? Why: Every effect depends on the direction.
  2. Split the effect into two channels: the business's borrowing costs, and customers' spending. Why: These are the two main ways rates affect a business.
  3. Chain each channel to profit, sales, cash flow or expansion plans. Why: Marks come from the linked consequence.
  4. Use case details, such as whether the business has loans or sells luxury items. Why: Analyse and Evaluate answers must be applied to the business.
  5. For Evaluate, follow the Evaluate card: weigh how serious each effect is for this business, then conclude. Why: Judgement needs a supported conclusion.

How each command word is answered

Outline1Give one effect, then link it to the named business's customers or loans.
Explain4Direction plus two linked steps, for example higher repayments, higher costs, lower profit, delayed expansion. No marks for a definition.
Analyse3Use both channels, borrowing costs and customer spending, applied to the named business or its customers.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Getting the direction wrong, for example saying higher rates increase sales.
  • Defining interest rate when asked for an effect.
  • Only stating 'costs go up' without saying how this affects profit or plans.
  • Ignoring case details such as the business's loans or the type of product it sells.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Explain one impact, 3 marks; no definition needed.
  2. Fix the direction: rates increase.
  3. Choose a channel: borrowing costs.
  4. Chain: higher repayments, higher costs, lower profit, expansion delayed.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

An increase in interest rates makes borrowing more expensive for a business. If the business has a loan or an overdraft with a variable rate, it has to pay more interest each month. This raises its costs, which reduces its profit and may mean it has to delay or cancel plans to expand, such as opening new shops.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the impact (borrowing becomes more expensive). 1 mark for the link (more interest to pay, so costs rise). 1 mark for the consequence (lower profit and delayed expansion). Total 3.

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Evaluate · June 2025 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define interest rate; quote the rise from 3% (2024) to 3.2% (2025) and note it is only 0.2 percentage points.
  2. Cost chain: loans for bicycles or expansion cost more, raising costs and the sales needed to break even, so profit falls.
  3. Demand chain: Chinese customers have less disposable income and saving is more attractive, so demand for leisure tours and 938 CYN team-building tours may fall.
  4. Counter-argument: foreign tourists (English-speaking guides) are not affected by Chinese rates; good reviews protect demand; no loans means little effect.
  5. Judgement: overall impact small or large, depending on how much CCT borrows and the share of local versus foreign customers.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The interest rate is the cost of borrowing money and the reward for saving it. In China it is predicted to rise from 3% in 2024 to 3.2% in 2025, a rise of 0.2 percentage points.

The rise could increase CCT's costs. CCT delivers bicycles to tourists' hotels and gives free maps and locks, so it needs bicycles, delivery vehicles and equipment. If CCT borrows through a loan or overdraft to replace bicycles or expand its tours, its interest payments will be higher. This raises its costs, so CCT must sell more tours just to break even. Profit falls, leaving less money to invest, for example in hiring more guides. CCT could raise prices to cover the extra cost, but this might make its tours less attractive.

The rise could also reduce demand. Chinese customers with loans will pay more interest, so they have less disposable income. A bicycle tour is a leisure activity rather than an essential, so local people may cut it first. Higher rates also make saving more attractive, so some people may save instead of spend. Demand for the new team-building tours could fall too, because businesses facing higher borrowing costs may cut spending on staff events priced at 938 CYN per person. Lower demand means lower revenue for CCT.

However, the impact may be small. The increase is only 0.2 percentage points, so the extra interest on a loan would be modest. Many of CCT's customers appear to be foreign tourists, since its guides speak English and help with hotel bookings, and their spending depends on interest rates in their own countries, not in China. CCT also has good online reviews and ratings, so its strong reputation may keep demand steady. If CCT has no loans, the rise in costs hardly affects it, and any savings it holds would earn slightly more interest.

Overall, the increase is likely to have only a small negative impact on CCT. The main risks are higher costs if it borrows to expand, and lower demand from local customers and businesses. The size of the impact depends on how much CCT borrows and on what share of its customers are foreign tourists. If most customers are international and CCT has little debt, the effect will be minimal; if it relies on local customers and loans, the effect will be much greater.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines interest rate (AO1), uses the 3% to 3.2% figures, team-building price, bilingual guides and reviews (AO2), develops cost-to-break-even and income-to-demand chains (AO3), and weighs them against the small size of the rise and foreign tourist demand, ending with a judgement that states what the impact depends on (AO4).
