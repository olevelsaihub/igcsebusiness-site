The method

  1. Name the PR action

    Donation, sponsorship, recycling or a press release.

  2. Link to the image

    Trust, respect or a caring reputation.

  3. Reach a result

    More customers, sales, profit or loyal staff.

  4. Show the other side

    It costs money and bad publicity can undo it.

  5. Judge

    In a long answer, weigh benefit against cost.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one reason, 3 marks; no definition needed.
  2. Reason: good PR improves the business's image.
  3. Expand 1: the public trust and think well of the business, e.g. after community or charity work.
  4. Expand 2: customers are more willing to buy, so sales and profit rise.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Good public relations improves the image the public has of the business. For example, if a business is reported in the news for supporting a local charity or reducing waste, people see it as caring and trustworthy. Customers are then more willing to choose its products over those of competitors, which can increase its sales and overall profit.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (improves image). 1 mark for the link (public see the business as caring and trustworthy). 1 mark for the result (more customers choose it, so sales and profit rise).

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Evaluate · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define PR: managing public image through good publicity, not paid adverts.
  2. Chain 1: refillable glass bottles and sustainable packaging -> eco-aware buyers choose Lady Million/Invictus -> more sales and loyalty.
  3. Chain 2: refills bring customers back -> repeat purchases, less packaging; charity donations -> free positive news and proud staff.
  4. Counter: PR actions cost money and cut short-term profit; luxury buyers may care more about scent and brand than packaging; risk of looking 'for show'.
  5. Judgement: positive overall for a brand-led business; depends on customers' environmental concern and actions being genuine and lasting.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Public relations (PR) is how a business manages its image with the public, often through actions that create good publicity rather than through paid advertising.

A first effect is a stronger brand image and higher sales. Rabanne has introduced refillable glass bottles, which reduce the number of bottles thrown away, and it uses sustainable packaging. Many customers care about the environment, so they may choose a perfume such as Lady Million over a rival brand because buying it feels responsible. This can increase sales and customer loyalty, and loyal customers may also buy Rabanne's clothes, handbags and jewellery.

A second effect is repeat business. Refillable bottles mean customers come back to buy refills instead of new bottles, so Rabanne uses less glass and packaging, and each refill keeps the customer returning to the brand. Repeat purchases give more stable revenue.

Donating to charities also brings free positive publicity. News about Rabanne's donations reaches people who might never see its adverts, so its image improves without the cost of an advertising campaign. It can also make employees proud to work for the business, which helps motivation and retention among its many employees across the world.

However, PR has costs and limits. Developing refillable bottles and sustainable packaging, and making donations, costs money, which reduces profit in the short term. Many buyers of luxury perfumes and aftershaves such as Invictus care more about the scent, the brand and its fashion appeal than about packaging, so the effect on sales may be small. There is no promise that caring for the environment will raise sales volume. There is also a risk: if the public believes Rabanne's actions are only for show, its image could be harmed more than if it had said nothing.

Overall, working hard on PR is likely to have a positive effect for Rabanne. As a leading fashion and fragrance brand, its image is one of its most valuable assets, and refillable bottles both improve that image and encourage repeat purchases. However, the size of the effect depends on how much its customers care about the environment and on Rabanne's actions being genuine and continued over time. If its target customers mainly care about the product itself, PR will protect the brand but may not increase sales by much.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines PR (AO1); uses refillable glass bottles, sustainable packaging, charity donations, named products and global staff (AO2); developed chains to sales, repeat purchases, free publicity and staff pride (AO3); counter-arguments on cost, customer priorities and credibility, then a conditional judgement (AO4).
