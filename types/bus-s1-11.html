Primary, secondary and tertiary sectors

The three sectors of activity: extracting raw materials, making goods, and providing services, and placing a business or job in the right sector.

Type 2 1 Business activity and influences on business BUS-S1-11

★★★☆☆ · 9 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

The question asks which sector a business or job is in, what a sector means, or why a business is classed in a sector.

Knowledge

  • Primary sector: taking raw materials from the earth or sea, for example farming, fishing, mining and forestry.
  • Secondary sector: turning raw materials into finished or part-finished goods, for example manufacturing, building and engineering.
  • Tertiary sector: providing services, for example shops (retail), banks, insurance, hotels, restaurants and transport.
  • A shop that sells goods is tertiary, because selling is a service even though the products are goods.
  • To classify a business, ask what it actually does: extract, make, or serve.

Before you begin

  • What raw materials are
  • The difference between goods and services

Specification: 1.3.1

Answer technique: Define a business term (1 mark) · Answer a multiple-choice question · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks)

Method

  1. Ask what the business or worker actually does: extract, make, or provide a service. Why: The activity decides the sector, not the product sold.
  2. Match it: extract is primary, make is secondary, service is tertiary. Why: This gives the correct sector or meaning.
  3. For an Outline, give the reason and then a detail from the case showing that activity. Why: The second mark is only given when the reason fits the named business.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice6Retailers, waiters, insurance and store managers are tertiary; ship builders and engineering are secondary; farmers and coal mining are primary.
Define4One sentence naming the activity: extracting raw materials, converting raw materials into goods, or providing services.
Outline1Say the business converts raw materials into products, then name the actual materials and products from the case.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Putting retailers in the secondary sector because they sell goods.
  • Defining a sector by naming an example only, rather than the activity.
  • In an Outline, giving a general reason with no detail from the case.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Outline, 2 marks, about AWG specifically.
  2. State the rule: secondary means making goods from raw materials.
  3. Apply it with case facts: wool turned into jumpers and dresses in AWG's workshops.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

AWG is in the secondary sector because it manufactures goods. In its own workshops it takes natural raw materials such as wool and makes them into finished clothing such as jumpers, dresses and baby clothes.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (it makes goods from raw materials). 1 mark for applying it to AWG (wool made into jumpers, dresses and baby clothes in its workshops). Total 2.
