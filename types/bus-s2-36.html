Dismissing an employee

Reasons an employer may lawfully dismiss an employee, such as misconduct or poor performance.

Type 1 2 People in business BUS-S2-36

★☆☆☆☆ · 3 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

The question asks why a business could or may dismiss (sack) an employee.

Knowledge

  • Dismissal means the employer ends a worker's employment because of something the worker has done or cannot do.
  • Fair reasons include serious misconduct (theft, violence, breaking safety rules), repeated lateness or absence, and being unable to do the job properly.
  • Businesses usually follow a disciplinary procedure first, such as warnings.
  • Unfair dismissal is ending someone's job for a reason the law does not allow, such as discrimination.
  • Redundancy is different: the job itself is no longer needed, so it is not the worker's fault.

  • What an employer and employee are

Specification: 2.5.3

Answer technique: Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Give a fair reason linked to the employee's conduct or ability. Why: This earns the identification mark.
  2. Set it in the business's work, for example lateness on the production line. Why: Answers not in context are not accepted.
  3. For Outline, add the consequence for the business, such as delayed orders or safety risks. Why: The development mark comes from the effect on the business.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State2One fair reason in the business's context, for example repeatedly arriving late for shifts on the tractor production line.
Outline1One reason plus its effect on the business, such as stealing parts or breaking safety rules delaying orders and harming reputation.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Giving redundancy reasons (the business needs fewer staff) when the question asks about dismissal.
  • Giving a reason that could apply anywhere instead of using the named business.
  • Giving an unfair reason, such as the employee's age or religion.

Worked explanation

  1. Outline: one reason plus one development, both about Saha.
  2. Context: Saha makes robots for hospitals, hotels and restaurants and offers seven-day customer service.
  3. Reason: serious misconduct, such as stealing robot parts or ignoring safety rules.
  4. Development: robots damaged or workers put at risk, so orders are delayed and reputation suffers.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Saha may dismiss an employee for serious misconduct, such as stealing robot parts or repeatedly ignoring safety rules when assembling robots. This could damage robots or injure other workers, delaying orders to the hospitals and restaurants that rely on Saha and harming its reputation in a market with many new competitors.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for a fair reason (serious misconduct: theft or ignoring safety rules). 1 mark for developing its effect on Saha (damaged robots, delayed orders, reputation in a competitive market). Both parts are in Saha's context.
