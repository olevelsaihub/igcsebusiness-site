Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one reason, 3 marks: one reason plus two linked steps.
  2. Reason: it shows whether the business made a profit or loss.
  3. Link 1: compare with last year or rivals to see if performance is improving.
  4. Link 2: that comparison guides a decision, e.g. invest in a new product or cut expenses.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Businesses use the statement of comprehensive income to see whether they have made a profit or a loss over the year. By comparing this year's sales, costs and profit with last year's, or with similar businesses, managers can tell whether performance is getting better or worse. This helps them decide, for example, whether they can afford to launch a new product or whether they need to cut expenses first.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (shows profit or loss for the period). 1 mark for the first link (comparison with past years or rivals shows the trend). 1 mark for the second link (guides a decision such as investing or cutting costs).

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Evaluate · June 2023 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Explain what financial documents show (income statement, cash flow forecast); quote Figure 2: sales €62,400, cost of sales €17,376, gross profit €45,024.
  2. Calculate gross margin (about 72%) and chain it: strong profitability suggests a second shop could succeed and shows funds available.
  3. Chain: documents support a bank loan application; a cash flow forecast shows if rent and wages can be paid in early months.
  4. Limits: no expenses shown despite the new higher-rent central Paris shop and four florists; only one year; past data and forecasts are not certain.
  5. Non-financial factors: competition near the new site, finding skilled florists, customer demand for premium flowers.
  6. Judgement: useful but not enough alone; depends on also using operating profit, past years and non-financial evidence.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Financial documents include the statement of comprehensive income, the statement of financial position and cash flow forecasts. They show a business's revenue, costs and profit.

Aude Rose's statement of comprehensive income is useful because it shows how profitable the shop is. Sales are €62,400 and cost of sales is €17,376, giving gross profit of €45,024. That is a gross profit margin of about 72% (45,024 ÷ 62,400 × 100), so Aude Rose keeps about 72 cents from every euro of sales after paying for its flowers. This suggests its high-quality arrangements sell at a good price, so a second shop selling similar products could also be profitable. The figures also show how much money may be available to fund the expansion and whether Aude needs to borrow. A bank would want to see these documents before lending, so they could help Aude Rose get a loan for the second shop.

Financial documents can also help with planning. Aude Rose could prepare a cash flow forecast for the second shop, estimating when money will come in and go out. This would show whether it can pay rent and wages in the first months, when sales are likely to be low, and how much overdraft it might need.

However, financial documents have limits. The extract shows only gross profit, not expenses such as rent and the wages of four florists. Aude Rose has just moved to a larger shop in central Paris with a higher rent, so its operating profit could be much lower than €45,024. The figures also cover only one year; earlier years are needed to see whether profit is rising. Documents show past results and cannot prove how a new location will perform, and a cash flow forecast is only an estimate. Non-financial factors matter too, such as competition near the new site, whether Aude can find more skilled florists, and whether customers there want premium flowers.

Overall, financial documents are very useful to Aude Rose, because they show whether the business is profitable enough to afford a second shop and they are needed to persuade a bank to lend. However, they are not enough on their own. Their usefulness depends on Aude also checking operating profit after the higher rent, comparing figures with previous years, and considering non-financial factors such as local competition before deciding.

Why it reaches the top level: Explains financial documents (AO1), uses Figure 2 and calculates a 72% gross margin, plus the higher-rent central Paris move and four florists (AO2), develops chains to affordability, bank lending and cash-flow planning (AO3), weighs missing expenses, one-year data, forecasts and competition, then judges usefulness with a 'depends on' (AO4).
