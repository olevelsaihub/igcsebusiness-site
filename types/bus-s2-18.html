Health and safety at work

Complying with health and safety laws, including safety training at induction, and why it matters to staff and the business.

Type 5 2 People in business BUS-S2-18

★★☆☆☆ · 6 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question mentions health and safety laws or legislation, safety training at induction, protective clothing, accidents or keeping staff and customers safe.

Knowledge

  • Health and safety laws require businesses to protect employees and customers from harm at work.
  • A hazard is anything that could cause harm, such as hot equipment, trailing wires or heavy machinery.
  • Ways to comply: safety training (often in induction), protective clothing, safety signs, checking equipment and clear emergency procedures.
  • Complying reduces accidents and injuries, so fewer staff are off sick.
  • A business that breaks the law can be fined, sued or even closed down, and its reputation can suffer.
  • Staff who feel safe are more confident and motivated, and customers are more willing to use the business.

Before you begin

  • What induction training is
  • That laws must be followed and can lead to fines
  • What a reputation is and why it matters for customers

Learn these types first: Training: induction, on-the-job and off-the-job

Specification: 2.3.1 · 2.5.3

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Name one safety action or reason (training, equipment checks, protective clothing, following the law). Why: This earns the identification mark.
  2. Link it to the hazards in this workplace. Why: State and Analyse answers need the case's real risks, such as coffee machines or gym equipment.
  3. Explain the result: fewer accidents, staff knowing what to do in an emergency. Why: This is the first development.
  4. End with the business effect: avoiding fines or legal action, fewer absences, protected reputation and customer numbers. Why: The final link shows why it matters to the business.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State3One reason or way that names the workplace risk, for example using tongs when serving food or safe use of coffee machines.
Explain2One reason, then two linked steps: awareness of hazards, fewer accidents, then lower legal risk or absence.
Analyse2Two chains in context, for example inspecting equipment prevents injuries and complaints; trained staff act confidently in emergencies.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Saying 'to keep people safe' with no further development in a 3-mark Explain.
  • Giving a generic safety rule in a State question rather than one linked to the business's workplace.
  • Listing several safety measures without explaining any effect on the business.
  • Forgetting the legal consequence (fines, being sued) that schemes often credit.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain card: one reason plus two linked expansions.
  2. Reason: staff learn the hazards of their workplace before starting work.
  3. Expansion 1: they know how to use equipment safely and what to do in an emergency.
  4. Expansion 2: fewer accidents, so fewer absences and less risk of fines or legal action.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Health and safety training at induction teaches new employees about the dangers in their workplace before they start work. They learn how to use equipment correctly, where the emergency exits are and what to do if there is an accident. This reduces the chance of injuries, so fewer staff are off work, and the business is less likely to be fined or taken to court for breaking health and safety law.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (learning the hazards before starting). 1 mark for explaining what they learn (safe use of equipment, emergency procedures). 1 mark for the consequence: fewer accidents and lower legal risk.
