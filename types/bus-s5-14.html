(e) Explain one reason why the quality of goods manufactured by a business is important to its success. (3)

Written-answer check
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Command is Explain for 3 marks: one reason plus two linked developments.
  2. Reason: high-quality goods build a good reputation.
  3. First link: satisfied customers buy again and recommend the business, rather than switching to a rival.
  4. Second link: this raises sales, helping the business succeed and make profit.
  5. Do not define quality; start with the reason.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Making high-quality goods gives a business a good reputation. Customers who are pleased with a product are likely to buy from the business again and to recommend it to friends, instead of switching to a competitor. This increases the number of customers and the sales of the business, which helps it to make more profit and succeed.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (quality builds a good reputation). 1 mark for the link (repeat purchases and recommendations rather than switching to rivals). 1 mark for the result (higher sales and profit). Total 3.

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Evaluate · November 2024 · Paper 1 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define TQM: all employees responsible for quality at every stage; aim of zero defects.
  2. Positive 1: fewer faulty robots in hospitals, hotels and restaurants -> reputation vs NIO, Zoox, Vention -> sales, possibly higher price.
  3. Positive 2: less waste and fewer repairs/service calls -> lower costs; helps design home robots right first time.
  4. Negative: training all 15 staff costs time and money; liquidity only moderate (current ratio 12/7 about 1.7, acid test 8/7 about 1.1); disruption and late deliveries.
  5. Judge: positive overall for a firm whose edge is long-lasting robots; depends on affordability and management support; phase it in if cash is tight.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Total quality management (TQM) is an approach where every employee in every department is responsible for quality at every stage of production. The aim is zero defects and continuous improvement, so faults are prevented rather than found at the end.

One positive effect is fewer faults and a better reputation. Saha's robots move medical supplies in hospitals and serve customers in hotels and restaurants. A faulty robot in a hospital could cause serious problems. If all 15 employees check quality at every stage, fewer robots will break down after delivery. This helps Saha meet its aim of making high-quality, long-lasting robots. Reliable robots lead to good reviews and repeat orders, which matters because many new businesses, including NIO, Zoox and Vention, are competing for market share. A strong quality reputation could help Saha win customers and even charge a higher price, increasing revenue.

A second positive effect is lower costs in the long run. When problems are prevented, less material is wasted, fewer robots need repairing, and fewer customers need help from the seven-day customer service team. This lowers costs and raises profit. Trained, involved employees may also feel more valued and motivated. TQM would be especially useful as Saha starts designing home robots, because faults can be designed out before full production begins.

However, there are negative effects. Introducing TQM needs training for every employee, which costs money and takes time. Saha's liquidity is acceptable but not strong: current assets of TRY 12 million and current liabilities of TRY 7 million give a current ratio of about 1.7, and the acid test ratio is (12 − 4) ÷ 7, about 1.1. Spending on training reduces cash available for its ambitious growth plans. Changing processes may also slow production for a while, causing late deliveries to hospitals and hotels. Higher costs might push up prices, making Saha less competitive in the short term.

Overall, introducing TQM is likely to have a positive effect on Saha. As a young business whose robots are used in hospitals and which aims to become a multinational, its reputation for reliable, long-lasting products is its main competitive advantage, and TQM protects this. With only 15 employees, training everyone is also more manageable than in a large firm. The effect depends on whether Saha can afford the training without harming its cash flow, and on managers fully supporting the change. If cash is tight, Saha could introduce TQM step by step.

Why it reaches the top level: Accurate TQM definition (whole workforce, zero defects, prevention); applied to Saha's hospital/hotel robots, 15 staff, named rivals, seven-day service, home robots, and liquidity ratios worked from Figure 3 (1.7 and 1.1); chains to reputation, revenue, cost and cash; balanced judgement with a 'depends on' condition. Meets Level 3 on AO1-AO4.

Evaluate · June 2021 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

  1. Define quality and why customers expect it; quality builds reputation and brand loyalty for a fashion retailer.
  2. Importance 1: NEXT buys from overseas makers (5,000 dresses from Turkey at 101.10 TRY, about £15.00 each at £1 = 6.74 TRY) -> checks at every stage stop faulty stock reaching shops -> fewer returns, protected sales (revenue £4,221m).
  3. Importance 2: aim of improving techniques and cutting costs while keeping quality -> less waste and rework -> protects operating profit of £590m.
  4. Counter: inspection and supplier monitoring cost money and time; cost-cutting pressure may conflict with quality; customers may accept small faults for lower prices.
  5. Judge: very important because one quality scandal can lose loyal customers; depends on NEXT controlling supplier quality without raising costs too much.

Evaluate · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

  1. Define quality service: meeting or exceeding customer expectations for hotels, cruises, car hire and day trips.
  2. Benefit 1: satisfied customers rebook (aim of keeping reputation so customers return) -> repeat revenue from 27 million customers.
  3. Benefit 2: word-of-mouth and good reviews -> new customers across 180+ destinations and holidays for families, couples and those with disabilities.
  4. Counter: quality service needs more and better-trained staff; employee costs rose from €39,631k (2021) to €57,498k (2022), about 45% -> lower profit if prices cannot rise.
  5. Judge: benefits outweigh costs in a competitive travel market; depends on TUI keeping consistent standards across many destinations and controlling costs.

Evaluate · June 2019 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q4(c)

  1. Define competitive advantage: a feature that makes customers choose Yellow over rivals (price, range, speed).
  2. Source 1: wide range of fashionable clothes for a wide demographic -> whole family shops in one store -> more sales per visit.
  3. Source 2: low costs -> limited advertising and shelf-ready clothes from suppliers cut costs; T-shirt costs 11.05 BDT and sells for 13.75 BDT (profit 2.70 BDT, about 19.6% margin) -> competitive prices.
  4. Counter: limited advertising may limit awareness; wide range may not suit every taste; fashion is easy to copy and some buyers want durable clothes.
  5. Judge: advantage mainly from low cost plus range; depends on rivals' ability to copy and on Yellow keeping its fast supply chain.

Evaluate · June 2022 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

  1. Define competitive advantage and how brand, quality and innovation create it.
  2. Source 1: long history since 1932 and 'Toy of the Century' twice -> trusted quality brand -> parents willing to pay more, loyal customers.
  3. Source 2: licensed products from Star Wars and Harry Potter -> appeal to fans of all ages -> new sales; scale of 15 factories and 18,000 staff -> global reach.
  4. Counter: premium prices may push some buyers to cheaper rivals; film-linked toys depend on the film's success; rivals can copy.
  5. Judge: brand and quality are the most lasting sources; depends on LEGO continuing to innovate and keeping prices acceptable. Liquidity strong (current ratio 34,946/12,763 about 2.7) supports investment.
