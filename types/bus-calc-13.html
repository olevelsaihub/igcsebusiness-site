Markup: percentage, selling price or cost

Calculating markup as profit per unit over cost times 100, or using a markup to find selling price from cost or cost from selling price.

Type 18 Calculations BUS-CALC-13

★★★★☆ · 14 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Gives a cost and a selling price and asks for the markup (or 'percentage increase' on cost), or gives a markup and asks for the price or the cost.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Stars describe how often this type appeared in past papers; they are not a prediction. 15 past-paper parts belong to this type.

Main plan: A-Grade Plan · Markup, margins and the income statement

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Markup is the profit added on top of the cost, shown as a percentage of the cost.
  • Markup % = (selling price − cost) ÷ cost × 100 = profit per unit ÷ cost × 100.
  • Divide by the COST, not the selling price. Dividing by price gives a different measure (a margin).
  • Selling price from cost: cost × (1 + markup/100). Cost from selling price: price ÷ (1 + markup/100).
  • Markup can be over 100%, for example when the price is more than double the cost.
  • Pearson provides the ratio formulae in the exam, but you must know which figures go where and how to round. Usually give markup to 2 decimal places and money in the stated currency.

Before you begin

  • Profit per unit = price − cost
  • Percentage of an amount and percentage change
  • Rounding to 2 decimal places

Learn these types first: Increase or decrease a value by a percentage

Specification: 3.5.1

Answer technique: Set out a calculation with working (2 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Find the cost and the selling price per item. Why: Markup compares these two.
  2. Profit per unit = selling price − cost. Why: This is the amount added on.
  3. Divide by the cost and multiply by 100. Why: Markup is measured against cost.
  4. For a price or a cost, use cost × (1 + markup) or price ÷ (1 + markup). Why: Some questions run the formula backwards.
  5. Round as asked and add % or the currency. Why: Final mark needs the correct rounding.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Dividing the profit by the selling price instead of the cost.
  • Dividing price by cost and multiplying by 100 without subtracting first (giving over 100% too much).
  • Finding the cost by taking the markup percentage off the price instead of dividing by 1 + markup.
  • Dividing cost by profit instead of profit by cost.

Worked explanation

  1. Cost = £190. Selling price = £350.
  2. Profit per drum kit = 350 − 190 = £160.
  3. Markup = 160 ÷ 190 × 100 = 84.2105…%.
  4. To two decimal places: 84.21%.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Markup = (selling price − cost) ÷ cost × 100 = (350 − 190) ÷ 190 × 100 = 160 ÷ 190 × 100 = 84.21%.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1: (350 − 190) ÷ 190 × 100 set up correctly. Mark 2: 84.21%. The correct percentage alone earns both.

Calculation check

  • 350-190 = 160
  • (350-190) ÷ 190 × 100 = 84.21
