Knowledge card

Markup: percentage, selling price or cost

Calculation

Markup % = profit per unit ÷ cost × 100. It is always measured against the cost, never the selling price. To go the other way, use cost × (1 + markup) or price ÷ (1 + markup).

The calculation

Markup % = (selling price − cost) ÷ cost × 100

Profit per unit ÷ cost, as a percentage.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find Profit per unit:SP − C

To find Selling price:C × (1 + M ÷ 100)

To find Cost:SP ÷ (1 + M ÷ 100)

Try itA chair costs $80 to make and sells for $100. Find the markup.

  1. Profit = $100 − $80 = $20
  2. Markup = $20 ÷ $80 × 100 = 25%

5

The method

  1. Find cost and price

    Note both figures per item.

  2. Profit per unit

    Selling price − cost.

  3. Divide by cost

    Profit ÷ cost × 100.

  4. Backwards?

    Price = cost × (1 + markup). Cost = price ÷ (1 + markup).

  5. Round and label

    Add % or the currency.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Dividing by price

    The base is always the cost.

  • ✗
    Skipping the subtract

    $52 ÷ $40 gives 130%, not 30%.

  • ✗
    Cost from price

    Do not take the % off the price. Divide by 1 + markup.

  • ✗
    Upside down

    Profit goes on top, cost underneath.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Cost = £190. Selling price = £350.
  2. Profit per drum kit = 350 − 190 = £160.
  3. Markup = 160 ÷ 190 × 100 = 84.2105…%.
  4. To two decimal places: 84.21%.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Markup = (selling price − cost) ÷ cost × 100 = (350 − 190) ÷ 190 × 100 = 160 ÷ 190 × 100 = 84.21%.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1: (350 − 190) ÷ 190 × 100 set up correctly. Mark 2: 84.21%. The correct percentage alone earns both.

Calculation check

  • 350-190 = 160
  • (350-190) ÷ 190 × 100 = 84.21
