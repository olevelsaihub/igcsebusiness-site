Recruitment documents: job description to CV

Job description, person specification, application form and CV: what each contains, who writes it, and how each helps a business hire well.

Type 4 2 People in business BUS-S2-09

★★★☆☆ · 9 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

The question names a job description, person specification, application form or CV, or asks which document is sent, completed or provided by whom.

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Recruitment means finding and attracting people to apply for a job; selection means choosing between them.
  • A job description is written by the business and lists the job title, tasks, duties and responsibilities of the job.
  • A person specification is written by the business and lists the qualifications, skills, experience and qualities the ideal person should have.
  • An application form is designed by the business and filled in by the applicant, so every applicant gives the same information in the same order.
  • A curriculum vitae (CV) is written by the applicant and summarises their education, qualifications, work experience and referees.
  • Together these documents help the business compare applicants fairly and choose someone who matches the job.

Before you begin

  • What a job vacancy is
  • What qualifications and experience mean
  • The difference between the employer and the applicant

Specification: 2.2.2 · 2.2.3

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Name the document and say who produces it: the business or the applicant. Why: Many multiple-choice items test exactly who writes or sends each document.
  2. State what the document contains. Why: Accurate contents earn the knowledge marks.
  3. Link the contents to matching the right person to the job. Why: The purpose is to hire suitable staff, which is where development marks come from.
  4. Carry the chain to a business result: less training, fewer poor hires, better products or service. Why: Analyse answers need connected chains ending in an effect on the business.
  5. Use case details (the job, the skills the business needs) in State, Outline and Analyse answers. Why: Answers not in context are not accepted in these forms.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice5Ask who writes it: business documents are the job description and person specification; the applicant provides the CV and completes the application form.
State2Give one item that fits the named job, such as experience of food safety for a chocolate factory worker.
Define3One precise sentence: the job description covers the job's duties; the person specification covers the ideal person's qualities.
Explain1One reason, then two linked steps, for example an up-to-date CV lets you apply quickly, so you do not miss opportunities.
Outline1One reason plus one development linked to the business, for example checking reception skills so less training is needed.
Analyse2Two chains in context: what the document shows, how that lets the business compare applicants, and why that produces better hires.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Mixing up the job description (about the job) and the person specification (about the person).
  • Saying the business writes the CV or that the applicant writes the job description.
  • Giving document items that do not suit the named business in a State question.
  • Describing what a document is without explaining how it helps the business hire well.

Worked explanation

  1. Analyse card: two developed chains, each applied to Samsung.
  2. Pull context: skilled engineers, innovative TVs and smartphones, competition with Apple and Xiaomi, 270,000 staff.
  3. Chain 1: person specification sets required skills, so unsuitable people do not apply and HR time is saved.
  4. Chain 2: CVs and application forms let Samsung compare experience fairly and pick the strongest engineer, which supports innovation and competitiveness.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Samsung can use a person specification to set out the qualifications and experience it needs, such as an engineering degree and experience of designing smartphones or televisions. People who do not have these can see they are unsuitable and are less likely to apply. This means Samsung's human resources staff spend less time reading weak applications and can focus on the strongest candidates for its skilled engineering jobs.

Samsung can also compare applicants' CVs and application forms against the job description. Because an application form asks every applicant the same questions, Samsung can fairly compare each person's design experience and qualifications side by side. This makes it more likely that Samsung appoints the engineers with the most relevant skills, who can design the innovative products it needs to keep competing with Apple and Xiaomi in the smartphone market.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked: AO2 (3) for applying documents to Samsung's engineers and products throughout; AO3 (3) for two logical chains, from document contents to fairer comparison or fewer weak applicants, to better hires and competitiveness. Detailed context in both chains reaches Level 3 (5-6).
