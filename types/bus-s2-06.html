Types of employment: full-time, part-time, temporary

Full-time, part-time, job share and casual, seasonal or temporary work: meanings, why each is used, and choosing between them.

Type 13 2 People in business BUS-S2-06

★★★★★ · 15 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question names full-time, part-time, job share, casual, seasonal, temporary or permanent staff, or asks which contract a business should offer.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Stars describe how often this type appeared in past papers; they are not a prediction. 18 past-paper parts belong to this type.

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Full-time employees work the business's normal working week, often around 35-40 hours.
  • Part-time employees work fewer hours than full-time staff, for example only mornings or weekends.
  • Job share means one full-time job is split between two part-time workers who share the hours and duties.
  • Casual staff are called in only when needed; seasonal staff work at busy times of year; temporary staff are hired for a fixed period, for example to cover illness.
  • Permanent, full-time staff give stability, commitment and experience, but must be paid even in quiet periods.
  • Part-time, casual, seasonal and temporary staff give flexibility and lower wage costs in quiet periods, but may be less committed and need more training.

Before you begin

  • What wages and salaries are
  • That demand for a business's products can rise and fall during the year
  • Fixed costs versus variable costs (helpful for cost-based Justify questions)

Specification: 2.2.1 · 2.4.1 · 2.4.2 · 3.3.1

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Justify a choice between two options (9 marks) · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Name the type of employment and say what it means in one phrase. Why: You need to be clear which contract you are writing about.
  2. Look in the case for the work pattern: busy seasons, long opening hours, sickness cover or skilled long-term work. Why: Credit is given for matching the contract to this business's needs.
  3. Give one benefit or reason for using that type. Why: This is the identification mark.
  4. Develop it through cost, flexibility or staff commitment and quality. Why: These are the effects examiners reward.
  5. For a 9-mark Justify, follow the Justify card: weigh both contract options in the case and choose one. Why: Justify answers need both options assessed before a decision.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State7One reason tied to the business, for example extra holiday-season demand at TUI or cover for a sick delivery driver at IKEA.
Define1Give the exact meaning in one sentence, for example job share: one job split between two part-time workers.
Explain3One benefit or reason, then two linked steps through flexibility, wage costs or commitment to an effect on customers or profit.
Outline1One reason plus one development that uses the case, such as a farm needing pickers only at harvest time.
Analyse1Two developed chains in context, for example casual chefs called in only for classes, saving salary costs and matching demand.
Justify5Follow the Justify card: compare the two contracts on cost, flexibility and commitment in this case, then choose and explain why.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Writing a definition of seasonal or part-time work when the question asks for a benefit or reason; definitions earn no marks in Explain questions.
  • Giving a generic reason in State or Outline questions that does not use the named business.
  • Confusing part-time with temporary: part-time is about hours per week, temporary is about how long the job lasts.
  • In Justify questions, praising one option without weighing the drawback of the chosen option.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain card: one benefit plus two linked expansions; do not define seasonal work.
  2. Benefit: the business can change the size of its workforce to match demand.
  3. Expansion 1: extra workers are hired and paid only in busy months.
  4. Expansion 2: wage costs stay low in quiet months while customers are still served in busy months, so sales are not lost.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Using seasonal employees lets a business change the size of its workforce to match demand. It only takes on and pays extra workers during its busiest times of year, such as holiday periods, when its permanent staff could not cope alone. This means customers are still served quickly at peak times so sales are not lost, while the wage bill stays lower during the quiet months.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the benefit (workforce matched to demand / flexibility). 1 mark for explaining that extra staff are used only in busy periods. 1 mark for the consequence: demand met at peak times and lower wage costs in quiet times.

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Justify · June 2022 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Q3(e)

Answer plan

  1. Option 1 permanent: job security -> committed, motivated chef -> high-quality desserts to stand out from nearby restaurants.
  2. Limit: stuck with wage if desserts fail; family business may not want an outsider long term.
  3. Option 2 temporary: trial the new dessert range first -> end contract if it fails -> protects profit.
  4. Limit: skilled chefs may refuse insecure jobs and work at nearby rivals.
  5. Recommend temporary with possible permanent offer later; depends on attracting a skilled chef on those terms.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Option 1, a permanent contract, means the chef is employed with no end date. The chef has job security, so they are likely to be more committed and loyal to Can Culleretes. A motivated chef is more likely to create high-quality desserts, which matters because the restaurant is close to many other restaurants and must stand out to tourists. The chef also has time to learn the family's traditional Mediterranean style. However, if the new desserts do not sell, the restaurant is stuck paying a permanent wage, and a family-run business with six family members already working there may not want an outsider in the business long term.

Option 2, a temporary contract, means the chef is employed for a fixed period. This lets Montserrat and Alicia test whether the new range of desserts is popular before making a long-term commitment. If the desserts do not sell well, the contract simply ends and the restaurant avoids an ongoing wage cost, which protects profit. However, a skilled dessert chef may not want a job without security and may choose to work at one of the many other restaurants nearby. Can Culleretes might then only attract a less skilled chef, and the desserts could fail because of poor quality rather than poor demand.

I recommend Option 2, a temporary contract. The new dessert range is untested, and a family business that has survived since 1786 is likely to want to limit risk. A temporary contract gives the desserts a fair trial with lower financial risk. If the desserts are popular, the chef can be offered a permanent contract later, and this possible reward also motivates the chef during the trial. However, this depends on whether a good chef will accept a temporary job. If no skilled chef applies, a permanent contract would be needed to attract the right person, because poor desserts would harm the restaurant's long reputation.

Why it reaches the top level: Both contracts applied to Can Culleretes (family-run since 1958, open since 1786, nearby rivals, untested dessert range) (AO2); developed chains on commitment/quality and risk/cost (AO3); a drawback for each and a clear, conditional recommendation (AO4).

Justify · June 2019 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q2(f)

  1. Option 1 full-time: fewer staff per store, more consistent service and loyalty from sales assistants.
  2. Limit: must be paid even when the store is quiet -> higher wage costs.
  3. Option 2 part-time: flexible hours -> extra staff at busy times and long opening hours, e.g. when 'Design Your Own Shirt' needs more help for customers.
  4. Limit: part-time staff may be less committed -> higher staff turnover and recruitment costs.
  5. Recommend part-time (or full-time) with reasons; depends on how much customer demand changes during the day/week and the training needed for the new custom product.

Justify · June 2022 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q2(f)

  1. Aim: retain committed skilled employees at Cadbury.
  2. Option 1 part-time: flexibility for staff (e.g. carers) -> they stay; Cadbury can add hours for seasonal peaks like Christmas.
  3. Limit: more staff to manage; extra employment costs for part-time workers' rights.
  4. Option 2 job share: two people cover one full-time job -> cover if one is ill, two sets of ideas (e.g. wrapper design).
  5. Limit: the two must communicate and work as a team; handover problems.
  6. Recommend one; depends on the type of job (continuous role suits job share; seasonal demand suits part-time).

Justify · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q2(f)

  1. Need: extra production workers at certain times of the year.
  2. Option 1 casual: called in only when needed -> no cost when quiet; suits workers wanting 'on call' work around family.
  3. Limit: no guaranteed work -> low commitment; may not be available when called.
  4. Option 2 part-time: regular set hours, e.g. students in holidays -> no full-time permanent commitment.
  5. Limit: may not have the skills or hours a luxury brand needs; must meet each country's employment laws.
  6. Recommend one; depends on how predictable the busy periods are and skill level needed for quality products.

Justify · November 2025 · Paper 1 · Q3(e)

  1. Goal: increase profit; current full-time tour guides; 5% commission to sales team.
  2. Option 1 part-time guides: pay only for busy tour times -> lower wage costs -> higher profit, especially in quiet seasons.
  3. Limit: knowledgeable, passionate guides may leave for full-time jobs -> weaker tours and reviews in a competitive market.
  4. Option 2 cut commission below 5%: lower cost per tour sold -> higher profit if sales stay the same.
  5. Limit: sales team less motivated -> fewer tours sold -> revenue may fall more than costs.
  6. Recommend one with reasons; depends on how seasonal demand is and how sensitive sales staff are to commission.
