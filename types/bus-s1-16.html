Multinationals and globalisation

What a multinational is and what globalisation means; benefits and drawbacks of operating in more than one country, for the business itself and for the host country or economy where it locates.

Type 13 1 Business activity and influences on business BUS-S1-16

★★★★☆ · 11 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question mentions a multinational, globalisation, a global business, operating in or entering other countries, or the benefits to a country where a large firm locates.

Knowledge

  • A multinational is a business that operates (produces or sells) in more than one country.
  • Globalisation is the growth of trade and business links across the whole world, so firms buy, make and sell in many countries.
  • Globalisation gives even small businesses opportunities, such as selling to customers abroad online, but also threats, such as more foreign competition.
  • Benefits to a multinational: more customers and sales, lower costs by producing where it is cheapest, economies of scale, risk spread across countries, and access to skilled staff.
  • A large group can own businesses in several markets; earnings and brand strength from one part can support the others.
  • Benefits to a host country (where the multinational locates): jobs, new skills and training, tax revenue for public services, and investment in infrastructure.
  • Possible drawbacks to a host country: profits sent abroad, local firms squeezed out, low wages or harm to the environment.

  • What economies of scale are, in simple terms
  • What tax revenue pays for (public services)

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks)

Method

  1. Decide whose side the question takes: the business itself or the host country. Why: Benefits differ for each, and answering for the wrong side loses marks.
  2. Pick one clear gain, such as a larger market, lower costs, spread risk, or new jobs. Why: A valid benefit earns the first mark.
  3. Chain it to an outcome: sales, revenue, costs and profit for a firm; jobs, taxes and living standards for a country. Why: Explain and Analyse marks come from linked consequences.
  4. For Analyse, follow the Analyse card: two benefits, each applied to the named business or country using case facts. Why: Higher levels need context throughout.
  5. For Define, give the short exact meaning only; for Explain, do not spend words defining. Why: Definitions earn the Define mark but no marks in Explain.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Define3Multinational: a business operating in more than one country. Globalisation: businesses trading and operating across the world.
Outline1One benefit linked to the named business, for example its brand or revenue helping the wider group it belongs to.
Explain3One benefit plus two linked steps leading to revenue, costs, risk or profit. No marks for a definition.
Analyse5Two developed benefits for the named firm or country, using case facts such as where it sells, makes products or employs people.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Writing benefits for the business when the question asks about the host country, or the other way round.
  • Defining 'multinational' when the question asks for an advantage.
  • Mixing up globalisation (a world-wide trend) with a multinational (one business).
  • Listing benefits without linking them to revenue, costs, jobs or the named business.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Explain one benefit, 3 marks; no definition needed.
  2. Pick one benefit: it sells in many countries, so its risk is spread.
  3. Link it: a fall in sales in one country is balanced by sales elsewhere.
  4. Add the consequence: total revenue is steadier, so the business is safer.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One benefit of being a multinational is that the business spreads its risk across many countries. For example, Huawei sells smartphones and tablets in many different markets, so if demand falls in one country, perhaps because of a recession there, strong sales in other countries can make up for it. This keeps its total revenue more stable, so the business is less likely to face cash problems or have to cut production.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the benefit (risk spread across countries). 1 mark for the link (falling sales in one country balanced by sales elsewhere). 1 mark for the consequence (steadier revenue, a safer business). Total 3.
