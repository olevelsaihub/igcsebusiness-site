External factors affecting business decisions

Social, technological, environmental and political factors outside the business, their impact on decisions, and how a business responds.

Type 11 1 Business activity and influences on business BUS-S1-26

★★☆☆☆ · 7 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question mentions social, technological, environmental or political factors, being environmentally friendly, reducing waste, or how a business reacts to outside changes.

Knowledge

  • External factors are things outside a business's control that affect its decisions.
  • Social factors: changes in people's lifestyles and tastes, such as becoming more health conscious.
  • Technological factors: new technology, such as online selling or new machines.
  • Environmental factors: concern about pollution, waste, recycling and climate, and pressure to be 'green'.
  • Political factors: government actions, such as taxation and laws.
  • Businesses respond by changing products, methods or marketing, which can raise costs but improve reputation and sales.

Before you begin

  • What a business's customers want
  • What costs, revenue and profit are
  • What a social objective is

Specification: 1.1.1 · 1.6.1 · 1.7.1 · 4.2.2 · 4.3.4 · 5.2.2

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Classify the factor: social, technological, environmental or political. Why: This shows knowledge and points you to the right effects.
  2. Find what the business in the case already does or faces, such as recycling or a new trend. Why: Context answers must use the named business.
  3. Ask what the factor changes: customers' wishes, costs, or reputation. Why: This is the first link in the chain.
  4. Follow it to an outcome: sales, profit, or meeting the business's aims. Why: Analysis marks reward connected chains.
  5. For Analyse, follow the Analyse card: two developed points tied to the case. Why: Higher levels need detailed application throughout.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State2Give one factor or response that fits the case, such as changing a menu for health-conscious customers.
multiple-choice1Political factors come from government, such as taxation; global warming and habitat destruction are environmental.
Analyse5Two chains from the factor to customers, costs or reputation and then to sales or aims, each using the named business's actions.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Putting a factor in the wrong category, for example calling taxation environmental.
  • Describing the factor in general without using what the named business does.
  • Listing effects with no chain to sales, costs or the business's aims.
  • Giving an internal factor when the question asks for an external one.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Analyse, 6 marks; two connected, applied points.
  2. Classify: environmental factors (waste, recycling, being green).
  3. Point 1: Waste Not reduces waste and builds community support.
  4. Point 2: the recycling hub meets green demand and can cut costs.
  5. Link both to Loaf's sales, profit and social aims.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Environmental factors are important for Loaf because more people now want businesses to be 'green'. Loaf meets this through its 'Waste Not' scheme, giving leftover food to charities instead of throwing it away. This reduces the waste the bakery creates and shows local people that Loaf cares about its community. As a result, more customers may choose to buy its bread and book its cookery courses, increasing its revenue.

Loaf also provides a recycling hub for the local community. This attracts environmentally aware shoppers and fits Loaf's aim of helping its local high street. If Loaf also recycles its own packaging, it can cut its costs, so it makes more profit. Because Loaf is a social enterprise, this extra profit can be put back into its charitable work, which further improves its reputation and helps it meet its social objectives.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): both points use Loaf's own actions, the Waste Not scheme and recycling hub (AO2, 3 marks), and each follows a logical chain to reputation, costs, revenue and social aims (AO3, 3 marks). Total 6.
