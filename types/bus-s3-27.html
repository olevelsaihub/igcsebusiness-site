Liquidity and how useful accounting ratios are

The meaning of liquidity and how profitability and liquidity ratios help a business judge performance and compare.

Type 9 3 Business finance BUS-S3-27

★☆☆☆☆ · 4 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

The question defines liquidity or asks how useful a named ratio (acid test, current ratio, gross profit margin, profitability ratios) is to a business, usually with figures.

Knowledge

  • Liquidity is how easily a business can turn its assets into cash to pay its short-term debts.
  • The current ratio is current assets divided by current liabilities.
  • The acid test ratio is current assets minus inventory, divided by current liabilities; it leaves out stock because stock may be slow to sell.
  • An acid test ratio below 1 means the business has less than one unit of quick assets for every unit it owes in the short term.
  • Profitability ratios, such as gross profit margin and operating profit margin, show how much profit is made from each unit of sales.
  • Ratios are most useful when compared with previous years or with similar businesses.
  • Ratios use past figures and do not explain why a change happened.

Before you begin

  • Current assets, inventory and current liabilities (BUS-S3-23)
  • Gross and operating profit (BUS-S3-25)
  • Comparing decimals and percentages

Learn these types first: Calculate the current ratio · Calculate the gross profit margin · Calculate the acid test ratio · Return on capital employed (ROCE)

Specification: 3.5.1 · 3.5.2 · 3.5.3

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark)

Method

  1. Say what the ratio measures in plain words: liquidity or profitability. Why: Credit starts with knowing what the ratio tells the business.
  2. Read the given figures and describe the trend or compare with a benchmark such as 1. Why: Quoting the data is the main application mark.
  3. Link the trend to what the business can now do: pay suppliers, seek finance, change prices or costs. Why: Chains from figure to action earn the analysis marks.
  4. Add a point on what the ratio cannot show, e.g. it is past data or ignores reasons. Why: This deepens the analysis of usefulness.
  5. Follow the Analyse card: two applied points, each with a full chain of reasoning. Why: Level 3 needs detailed context throughout.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Defining liquidity as how much cash a business has, missing the idea of converting assets into cash.
  • Describing what the ratio is without using the case figures.
  • Saying a rising ratio means all is well while it is still below 1.
  • Confusing liquidity ratios with profitability ratios.
  • Stating a usefulness point without linking it to a business decision.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Analyse usefulness, 6 marks, levels-marked: apply to TBTS and build chains.
  2. Say what the acid test ratio measures: paying short-term debts without selling stock.
  3. Quote the data: 0.75 (2018), 0.87 (2019), 0.88 (2020); rising but always below 1.
  4. Chain 1: improving liquidity means suppliers of toys can be paid on time, keeping their support.
  5. Chain 2: below 1 means caution: plan cash or arrange short-term finance before buying new stock.
  6. Optional depth: the rise has slowed and the ratio does not explain why.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The acid test ratio compares TBTS's current assets, leaving out its inventory of toys, with its current liabilities. It is useful because it shows whether TBTS could pay its short-term debts without first having to sell its stock. Figure 2 shows the ratio rising from 0.75 in 2018 to 0.87 in 2019 and 0.88 in 2020. This tells TBTS that its liquidity is improving, so it is becoming more able to pay its toy suppliers on time, which keeps a good relationship with them and makes them more willing to keep supplying on credit.

However, the ratio is below 1 in every year. This means that, without selling inventory, TBTS has less than 1 SGD of quick assets for every 1 SGD it owes in the short term. As a result, TBTS can see it must be careful with cash, for example by keeping enough money aside before buying new stock for its three shops and website, or by arranging short-term finance such as an overdraft in advance, so it is not left unable to pay its creditors. The ratio is also only a snapshot of past figures: the increase slowed from 0.12 to just 0.01, and the ratio does not explain why, so TBTS would need to look at its cash-flow forecast as well before making big spending decisions.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked, 6 marks (AO2 3, AO3 3). Application: correct meaning, quoting all three figures and the below-1 point, linked to TBTS's toys and shops. Analysis: two developed chains (rising ratio leading to supplier trust; below 1 leading to caution and finance) plus a limitation, reaching Level 3.
