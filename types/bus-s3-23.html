Statement of financial position: assets and liabilities

Current and non-current assets and liabilities, inventory, capital employed, and the purpose of the statement of financial position.

Type 3 3 Business finance BUS-S3-23

★★★☆☆ · 9 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question names an asset, liability, inventory or capital employed, asks which item appears on the statement of financial position, or asks why the statement is useful.

Knowledge

  • The statement of financial position shows what a business owns and what it owes on a particular date.
  • Assets are things the business owns. Non-current assets are kept for more than a year, e.g. buildings, machinery and vehicles.
  • Current assets are cash or items that will become cash within a year, e.g. cash, inventory and money owed by customers.
  • Inventory is the stock of goods or materials a business holds to sell or to use in production.
  • Liabilities are debts the business owes. Current liabilities must be paid within twelve months, e.g. an overdraft or money owed to suppliers; non-current liabilities are due after twelve months, e.g. a long-term loan.
  • Capital employed is the long-term money invested in the business.
  • Sales, cost of sales and gross profit belong in the income statement, not the statement of financial position.

Before you begin

  • The difference between owning and owing
  • What a debt and a loan are
  • What stock or inventory is

Specification: 2.5.3 · 3.4.2 · 3.5.3

Answer technique: Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Ask: does the business own it (asset) or owe it (liability)? Why: This is the first split for every item.
  2. Ask: will it be used up or paid within twelve months (current) or later (non-current)? Why: The twelve-month rule completes the classification.
  3. For State questions, give an item that fits the named business, e.g. its machinery or vehicles. Why: The schemes want items in context.
  4. For purpose questions, say what the statement shows and what decision it helps, then the result. Why: The Explain card's point-plus-two-links pattern earns all three marks.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Define4Give the meaning with the twelve-month rule where it applies, e.g. current liabilities are debts due within twelve months.
multiple-choice4Check owned or owed, then within or after twelve months; reject income-statement items such as sales or gross profit.
State1Name an asset or liability that clearly belongs to the named business and its activity.
Explain1One purpose, e.g. checking whether short-term debts can be paid, then two links to a decision and its result.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Classing a building, vehicle or factory as a current asset.
  • Mixing up current and non-current liabilities by forgetting the twelve-month rule.
  • Putting sales or gross profit on the statement of financial position.
  • Defining the statement in an Explain question instead of giving a purpose, which earns nothing.
  • Naming a non-current asset that does not fit the business in the case.

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Explain one purpose, 3 marks: one purpose plus two links.
  2. Purpose: shows whether the business can pay debts due soon.
  3. Link 1: managers compare current assets with current liabilities.
  4. Link 2: if short, they act early, e.g. reduce inventory or arrange finance.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One purpose of the statement of financial position is to show whether a business will be able to pay the debts it owes in the near future. Managers can compare its current assets, such as cash and inventory, with its current liabilities, such as money owed to suppliers. If the liabilities are larger, they can act early, for example by holding less inventory or arranging extra finance, before suppliers stop giving the business credit.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the purpose (shows whether short-term debts can be paid). 1 mark for the first link (comparing current assets with current liabilities). 1 mark for the second link (early action such as cutting inventory or arranging finance).
