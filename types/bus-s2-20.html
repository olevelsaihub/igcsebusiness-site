Motivation theories: Maslow, Taylor and Herzberg

The ideas of Maslow's hierarchy of needs, Taylor's money-driven view, and Herzberg's motivators and hygiene factors.

Type 6 2 People in business BUS-S2-20

★☆☆☆☆ · 4 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question names Maslow, Taylor or Herzberg, a level of the hierarchy of needs, hygiene factors or motivators.

Knowledge

  • Motivation is the desire to work hard and do a job well.
  • Taylor believed workers are motivated mainly by money, so pay should be linked to output (for example piece rate).
  • Maslow's hierarchy of needs has five levels, from bottom to top: basic (physiological), safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation.
  • In Maslow's theory a worker must meet lower needs before higher needs motivate them; social needs are level 3.
  • Herzberg split factors into hygiene factors (pay, working conditions, job security) and motivators (achievement, recognition, responsibility, promotion).
  • Herzberg said missing hygiene factors cause dissatisfaction, but only motivators truly motivate workers.

Before you begin

  • What motivation means
  • Financial and non-financial rewards
  • Basic idea that people have different needs

Specification: 2.4.1

Answer technique: Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Identify which theory is named. Why: Each theory has its own key idea and terms.
  2. Recall the key idea and place the factor or level correctly in it. Why: Accurate theory knowledge earns the first mark.
  3. Give a workplace example of that factor or level. Why: This develops the point and shows understanding.
  4. Explain the effect on workers' satisfaction or effort. Why: The final mark links the theory to motivation and performance.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Mixing up the theorists, for example linking hygiene factors to Maslow.
  • Counting Maslow's levels from the top instead of the bottom.
  • Saying Herzberg's hygiene factors motivate staff, when he said they only prevent dissatisfaction.
  • Naming two factors with no explanation of either.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain card: one factor plus two linked expansions; do not try to explain both factors.
  2. Pick a motivator factor.
  3. Expansion 1: what it is, with an example such as recognition or responsibility giving a sense of achievement.
  4. Expansion 2: the effect: staff feel satisfied, so they work harder and are more productive.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One factor in Herzberg's theory is a motivator, such as being given more responsibility or being recognised for good work. Herzberg said motivators come from the job itself, so an employee gains a feeling of achievement and personal growth when they do the work well. Because of this, employees become more satisfied and genuinely motivated, so they put in more effort and are more productive.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for naming the factor (a motivator). 1 mark for explaining it with an example (achievement, recognition, responsibility from the job itself). 1 mark for the effect on the worker (satisfaction, effort, productivity).
