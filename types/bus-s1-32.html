Reasons why businesses fail

Causes of business failure: cash-flow problems or lack of finance, losing competitiveness, and not adapting to market change.

Type 8 1 Business activity and influences on business BUS-S1-32

★☆☆☆☆ · 4 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question asks why a business might fail or close, or for a reason linked to failure for the named business.

Knowledge

  • Business failure means a business has to stop trading and close.
  • Cash-flow problems: more cash goes out than comes in, so bills, wages and suppliers cannot be paid on time.
  • Lack of finance: the business cannot raise the money it needs to keep going or to grow.
  • Not being competitive: rivals offer better prices, quality or service, so customers go elsewhere.
  • Failing to adapt: the business does not change when customer tastes, technology or the market change.
  • Insolvency means the business cannot pay its debts when they are due.

Before you begin

  • The difference between cash and profit
  • What cash inflows and outflows are
  • What competitors are

Learn these types first: Why cash matters and how to improve cash flow

Specification: 1.8.2

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Name one cause of failure clearly (cash flow, finance, competition or not adapting). Why: A valid cause earns the first mark.
  2. Explain what this cause does inside the business, such as cash running out or sales falling. Why: The second mark rewards the first link in the chain.
  3. Finish the chain at the end point: unpaid bills or debts, losses, and closure. Why: The final mark comes from showing how the cause stops the business continuing.
  4. For Analyse or State, use evidence from the case, following the Analyse card for two developed reasons. Why: Context answers are credited only when they fit the named business.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State1Give one reason tied to the case, such as the owner's limited experience raising costs.
Explain3One cause plus two linked steps ending in the business being unable to pay debts or continue.
Analyse1Two reasons from the case, each followed through to falling sales, cash shortage or closure for this business.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Listing several causes with no explanation, which earns only one mark.
  • Stopping the chain early without showing why the business cannot continue.
  • Confusing cash with profit: a profitable business can still fail if it runs out of cash.
  • Giving a general reason in a State or Analyse answer that does not use the case.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Explain one reason, 3 marks.
  2. Choose the clearest cause: cash-flow problems.
  3. Say what happens: outflows are bigger than inflows.
  4. Finish the chain: bills and wages cannot be paid, leading to closure.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A business may fail because of cash-flow problems. This happens when more cash flows out of the business than flows into it over a period, so the money in its bank account runs down. The business then cannot pay its suppliers, its workers' wages or its loan repayments on time, so creditors may take legal action and the business can be forced to close.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (cash-flow problems). 1 mark for explaining it (outflows larger than inflows). 1 mark for the consequence (cannot pay suppliers and workers, so it may close). Total 3.
