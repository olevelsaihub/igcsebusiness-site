The method

  1. Classify

    Internal or external? Short-term or long-term?

  2. Recall features

    Cost, speed, repayment and effect on control.

  3. Match the need

    How much, how long, and for what?

  4. Show the chain

    Give one feature, then its effect on the business.

  5. Make a choice

    Weigh both options against the case and pick one.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong group

    Retained profit and selling assets are internal; loans are external.

  • ✗
    Revenue as finance

    Sales revenue is not a source of finance.

  • ✗
    Features listed

    Explain one feature rather than listing many.

  • ✗
    Shares for sole traders

    Only companies can issue shares.

Sources of finance and why businesses need finance · June 2021 · Paper 1 · Question 2 · part (e) · 3 marks

(e) Explain one benefit to a business of using crowdfunding as a source of finance. (3)

View full original question and diagrams
Hint for part (e)

State one feature of the source, then show its consequence for the business in linked steps.

Your answer

Write your full answer first. Then compare it with the model answer and the mark checklist.

Written-answer check
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Explain one benefit, 3 marks, so one benefit and two linked expansions.
  2. Recall what crowdfunding is: many people each giving a small amount through a website.
  3. Pick one benefit: it can provide money when lenders such as banks will not.
  4. Link 1: say why banks may refuse, e.g. a new idea with no track record looks risky.
  5. Link 2: show the result for the business: the project can still go ahead.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One benefit of crowdfunding is that a business can raise money even when banks are not willing to lend to it. A new or unusual project may have no trading record, so a bank may see it as too risky, but on a crowdfunding website many members of the public can each put in a small amount because they like the idea. This means the business can still collect the total it needs and go ahead with its project instead of dropping it for lack of finance.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for identifying a benefit (finance available when lenders refuse). 1 mark for the first linked step (many people each contribute a small amount online, so the risk is spread). 1 mark for the second linked step (the business gets enough money to go ahead).

Justify · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Q3(e)

Answer plan

  1. Option 1 Rachel's savings: €7,500 available at once -> eight kennels and CCTV built fast; capacity from 7 to 15 pets; no interest.
  2. Limit 1: Rachel may not have enough; leaves no emergency fund; all risk is personal.
  3. Option 2 small bank loan: fixed instalments over time paid from new kennel income; savings kept as a safety net.
  4. Limit 2: interest raises costs; approval takes time; repayments due even if bookings are low.
  5. Choose savings for a small sum; depends on Rachel having enough plus a reserve, otherwise a loan.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Option 1 is using Rachel's personal savings, which is owner's capital. If Rachel has €7,500 saved, PPS can use it immediately without applying to a bank. The eight new kennels and the CCTV can be built quickly, so PPS can start taking more dogs and cats sooner. At present the hotel has room for only seven pets, so eight more kennels would more than double its capacity and could greatly increase revenue. There is also no interest to pay, so costs stay lower and all the extra profit stays in the business.

However, Rachel may not have €7,500 spare. If she uses all her savings, she has nothing left for emergencies, such as an urgent repair or a quiet month, and she carries all the risk personally.

Option 2 is a small bank loan, an external source. The bank lends €7,500, which PPS repays in instalments over an agreed period, usually at a fixed rate of interest. PPS can pay the instalments from the extra income earned by the new kennels, so the cost is spread over time rather than paid all at once. Rachel keeps her savings as a safety net and keeps full ownership.

However, PPS must pay interest, which adds to its costs and reduces profit. The bank may ask for security and takes time to approve the loan. If bookings for the new kennels are lower than expected, PPS still has to make every repayment.

I recommend Option 1, Rachel's personal savings. €7,500 is a fairly small sum, and using savings avoids interest and lets PPS build the kennels quickly so they start earning revenue sooner. This depends on Rachel actually having enough savings and still keeping some money back for emergencies. If her savings are not enough, the small bank loan becomes the better choice, because predictable repayments can be covered by income from the extra eight kennels.

Why it reaches the top level: Both sources applied to PPS facts (€7,500, eight new kennels with CCTV, capacity of seven pets, Rachel as owner); chains to capacity, revenue, cost and risk; limitation for each option; clear recommendation with a 'depends on' condition and alternative. Meets Level 3 for AO2, AO3 and AO4.

Evaluate · June 2022 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define venture capital: investors give finance for a share of ownership and profits; external; used for new, risky ventures.
  2. Positive 1: no interest or repayments -> better cash flow while new Can Café builds customers near many rivals.
  3. Positive 2: investor food-industry expertise for a new fast-service idea (restaurant is lunch and dinner only) -> pricing, hours, tourist marketing -> revenue.
  4. Negative: family-run since 1958, sisters co-own -> loss of control, risk to 1786 heritage image; share of profit goes to investors though family does the work.
  5. Judge: positive short term, costly long term; depends on share demanded and buy-back terms, or whether retained profit could be used instead.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Venture capital is finance from investors who put money into a new or growing business that is seen as risky. In return they receive a share of the ownership and therefore a share of the profits, rather than interest. It is an external source of finance.

One positive impact is that Can Café gets the money it needs to open without taking on debt. Can Culleretes needs finance for the new premises, equipment and stock. Because venture capital is not a loan, there are no monthly repayments or interest. This helps cash flow in the first months, when a new café near many other restaurants may take time to build up customers. With less pressure on cash, the café has a better chance of surviving its start-up period.

A second positive impact is expert advice. Venture capitalists often invest in businesses they understand and may have experience in the food industry. Can Café is a new idea for the family: drinks and small plates for people in a hurry, whereas the restaurant only opens for lunch and dinner. An investor who knows fast-service cafés could help with menu pricing, opening times or marketing to tourists near the attractions, which could raise revenue and profit.

However, there are negative impacts. The Agut-Manubens family has run the business since 1958, and sisters Montserrat and Alicia are co-owners. Selling shares to investors means losing some control. Investors may push for changes the family dislikes, such as cheaper food or longer hours, which might weaken the traditional image that makes a restaurant dating from 1786 special. Also, if Can Café becomes profitable, part of every year's profit goes to the investors, even though six family members do the daily work. This could cause disagreement in the family.

Overall, venture capital has a mainly positive impact in the short term, because it gives Can Café start-up money without interest and may bring useful expertise, raising its chance of success. In the long term, however, the family gives away part of the profit and some control. The decision depends on how large a share the investors want and whether the family can agree terms that let them buy the shares back later. If the restaurant could fund the café from its own retained profit, the family might prefer that to keep full control.

Why it reaches the top level: Accurate definition of venture capital (equity, share of profit, external); applied to the family ownership since 1958, 1786 heritage, lunch/dinner-only restaurant, tourists and rivals; chains to cash flow, revenue and control; balanced judgement on short vs long term with a 'depends on' condition. Meets Level 3 on AO1-AO4.

  2. Limit 1: may not hit the target; takes time; ideas are public so rival shoe shops in the same shopping centre could copy.
  3. Option 2 overdraft: flexible bank facility for buying stock; interest only on the amount used.
  4. Limit 2: high interest rates; bank can ask for repayment at any time; meant for short-term needs, not a long expansion.
  5. Judge: depends on the amount needed and how quickly the new stock sells; overdraft for a short stock gap, crowdfunding if a larger long-term sum is needed.
