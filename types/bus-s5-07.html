Written-answer check
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Command is Explain for 3 marks: one reason plus two linked developments.
  2. Reason: JIT cuts the amount of inventory held.
  3. First link: less money is spent on storage and fewer goods spoil or get damaged.
  4. Second link: so overall costs fall, which can raise profit.
  5. Avoid opening with a definition of JIT; begin with the reason.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One reason is that JIT cuts the amount of inventory a manufacturer has to hold. Because materials are delivered only when they are about to be used, the business needs less warehouse space and fewer materials go out of date or get damaged while waiting. This reduces storage and waste costs, so the overall costs of the business fall and it can make more profit.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (less inventory held). 1 mark for the link (less storage space needed and less spoiled or damaged stock). 1 mark for the consequence (lower overall costs, so higher profit possible). Total 3.

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Justify · November 2025 · Paper 2 · Q3(e)

Answer plan

  1. Option 1 JIT: electronic parts arrive only when needed at the new Texas factory, cutting storage costs and money tied up in stock.
  2. Chain: lower unit costs help Samsung compete on price with Apple and Xiaomi; less risk of parts going out of date.
  3. Option 1 drawback: late deliveries stop the line; smaller, frequent deliveries can raise transport costs.
  4. Option 2 Kaizen: skilled staff suggest small improvements that cut waste over time, but it needs training and takes time in a new factory.
  5. Judgement: choose one for minimising costs; depends on reliable suppliers near Texas and how quickly savings are needed after the $17 million investment.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Samsung should choose just-in-time (Option 1) for its new Texas factory, but this depends on having reliable suppliers.

Option 1, JIT, means parts such as screens and electronic components arrive only when they are needed on the production line, so very little inventory is held. This cuts costs in several ways. Samsung would need less warehouse space at the new factory, so it saves on storage, insurance and security. Less money is tied up in stock, so cash can be used elsewhere, and there is less risk of parts going out of date, which matters in electronics, where technology changes quickly. Lower unit costs could help Samsung compete on price with Apple and Xiaomi. However, JIT depends on suppliers delivering on time. If a delivery is late, the line could stop while workers are still paid. Smaller, more frequent deliveries could also raise transport costs.

Option 2, Kaizen, means continuous improvement: all employees regularly suggest small changes to how work is done. Over time this reduces waste of materials and time, improves quality and lowers costs. Samsung employs skilled engineers, so staff could suggest useful improvements, and being involved can motivate workers. However, Kaizen takes time to show results, because savings come from many small changes. In a brand-new factory, staff would first need training and time to learn the processes before they could suggest improvements, which raises costs in the short term.

On balance, JIT is the better choice because the aim is to minimise costs, and JIT cuts storage and inventory costs from the day the factory opens, while Kaizen's savings build up slowly. Having invested $17 million in the factory, Samsung will want savings quickly. However, this depends on Samsung finding reliable suppliers near Texas; if supplies are uncertain, Kaizen is the safer option, and in the long run Samsung could use both methods together.

Why it reaches the top level: Applies both methods to Samsung's new $17 million Texas factory and its rivals (AO2), develops chains for each (less inventory to lower storage and unit costs; employee suggestions to less waste) (AO3), weighs supplier risk, delivery cost, training time and slow results, and justifies a choice with a 'depends on' condition (AO4).

Justify · November 2020 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q2(f)

  1. Option 1 Kaizen: HRMW employees, who know production first-hand, suggest small improvements to making footballs, gloves and hockey sticks.
  2. Chain: less waste and better quality lower costs and support exports across the world; staff feel involved.
  3. Option 1 drawback: depends on workers being willing to take part, and savings build slowly.
  4. Option 2 JIT: materials such as leather arrive only when needed, so less storage space and less money tied up in stock.
  5. Option 2 drawback: late supplier deliveries can stop production, risky for an exporter with fixed orders.
  6. Judgement: choose one; depends on supplier reliability and staff willingness to suggest ideas.
