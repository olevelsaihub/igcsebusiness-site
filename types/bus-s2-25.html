Financial and non-financial methods of motivation

Remuneration, bonus, commission, promotion and fringe benefits, plus job rotation, job enrichment and autonomy: meanings, effects and choosing between them.

Type 14 2 People in business BUS-S2-25

★★★★★ · 24 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question names a pay or reward method such as bonus, commission, fringe benefits, promotion, job rotation, job enrichment or autonomy, or asks which method to use.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Stars describe how often this type appeared in past papers; they are not a prediction. 31 past-paper parts belong to this type.

Main plan: A-Grade Plan · Motivating staff

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Financial methods involve money: remuneration (pay for work), bonus (extra payment for reaching a target), commission (pay based on how much an employee sells), promotion (a higher job with higher pay), and fringe benefits.
  • Fringe benefits are extras on top of normal pay, such as staff discounts, a company car, free gym use or health insurance.
  • Non-financial methods do not involve extra money: job rotation, job enrichment and autonomy.
  • Job rotation means employees move between different jobs or departments for periods of time, reducing boredom.
  • Job enrichment means giving employees more challenging tasks and responsibility so the job is more interesting.
  • Autonomy means giving workers the freedom to decide how they do their own work.
  • Financial methods can quickly raise effort but add costs; non-financial methods can build skills and loyalty but may not suit everyone.

Before you begin

  • What motivation is and why it matters
  • Wages and salaries
  • Motivation theories (Taylor, Maslow, Herzberg) for longer answers

Specification: 2.4.1 · 2.4.2

Answer technique: Define a business term (1 mark) · Evaluate and reach a supported conclusion (12 marks) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Justify a choice between two options (9 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Name the method and say whether it is financial or non-financial. Why: Many multiple-choice items test this classification.
  2. Give its meaning in plain words if needed. Why: Define questions and first marks rely on an accurate meaning.
  3. Explain how it affects employees: effort, skills, interest or loyalty. Why: This develops the point.
  4. Link to the business: productivity, sales, staff turnover, and the cost of the method. Why: Credit goes to the effect on the business and its costs.
  5. For a 9-mark Justify or 12-mark Evaluate, follow that card: weigh the methods for this business's staff and products. Why: Choices must be supported with case evidence on both sides.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State8One method that fits the business and its staff, for example commission on each cycle tour booked, or rotating staff between outlets.
multiple-choice3Sort options into financial (bonus, remuneration, fringe benefits) and non-financial (job rotation, enrichment, autonomy); a bonus is added to basic pay.
Define7One accurate sentence, for example commission: pay linked to the number or value of sales an employee makes.
Explain3One method or impact, then two linked steps through staff effort or loyalty to productivity, turnover or costs.
Outline1One reason plus one context link, for example commission makes TUI agents sell more holidays because their pay rises with sales.
Justify8Follow the Justify card: compare the two methods' effects and costs for this workforce, then choose one with reasons.
Evaluate1Follow the Evaluate card: weigh benefits and drawbacks of financial methods for these employees, then judge their overall impact.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Classifying fringe benefits or promotion as non-financial, or job rotation as financial.
  • Confusing job rotation (moving between jobs) with job enrichment (more challenging tasks).
  • Confusing bonus (target reward) with commission (linked to sales).
  • In Justify answers, ignoring the cost of financial methods or that some staff may not want extra responsibility.
  • Giving a method in a State question that does not fit the named business.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain card: one impact plus two linked expansions.
  2. Impact: fringe benefits make employees more loyal, so fewer leave (lower staff turnover).
  3. Expansion 1: staff value extras that other employers may not offer, so they are less likely to move.
  4. Expansion 2: the business spends less on recruiting and training replacements, keeping costs down.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One impact is that staff turnover falls, because fringe benefits such as staff discounts or free gym membership make employees more loyal. Employees value these extras, which other employers may not offer, so they are less likely to leave for a competitor. As a result, the business spends less time and money recruiting and training replacements, which keeps its costs down.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the impact (lower staff turnover / loyalty). 1 mark for explaining why staff stay (valued extras not offered elsewhere). 1 mark for the consequence: reduced recruitment and training costs.

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Justify · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Q2(f)

Answer plan

  1. Option 1 financial: bonus when shop sales rise -> work harder, sell more food -> extra income keeps staff.
  2. Limit: expensive across 32,000+ stores; some value work-life balance over money.
  3. Option 2 non-financial: enrichment and promotion on barista -> coffee master -> manager path; 'partners' culture -> feel valued and loyal.
  4. Limit: some staff just want more pay; training cost.
  5. Recommend non-financial (fits partner culture) with reasons; depends on basic pay being fair.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Option 1, financial methods, means money rewards such as a bonus when a coffee shop increases its sales, or a pay rise. Employees can see a direct link between working hard and earning more, so they serve customers quickly and try to sell extra cakes and sandwiches. Extra income helps them pay their bills, so they are less likely to leave for a better-paid job. However, money rewards are expensive across more than 32,000 stores, and the effect may not last: once a bonus is paid regularly, employees may start to expect it. Some employees also care more about a good work-life balance than about extra pay.

Option 2, non-financial methods, includes job enrichment, training and promotion. Starbucks already calls its employees 'partners' and has a clear career path from barista to coffee master to coffee shop manager. Giving partners more responsibility and new skills makes them feel valued and trusted, and shows them a future inside the business. Because they can see promotion ahead, they are more likely to stay, which lowers recruitment and training costs. However, not every employee wants more responsibility; some simply want more money to pay their bills, and training takes time and money.

I recommend Option 2, non-financial methods. Starbucks already has a culture of partners working towards common goals and a ready-made promotion ladder, so non-financial methods fit the way the business works and build long-term loyalty, which is the aim of keeping employees. A competitor can easily match a bonus, but it is harder to copy a career path and a sense of belonging. However, this depends on the basic pay already being fair. If Starbucks' wages are lower than other coffee shops, employees will leave anyway, so non-financial methods work best when combined with a competitive basic wage.

Why it reaches the top level: Both methods applied to Starbucks (32,000 stores, 'partners', barista-to-manager path) (AO2); developed chains to effort and retention (AO3); limits of each and a justified choice with a 'depends on' condition about basic pay (AO4).

Evaluate · June 2023 · Paper 1 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define financial methods: bonus, commission, pay rise, promotion with higher pay.
  2. Chain 1: monthly sales/production bonus -> more effort -> revenue above €330 606 in a street with many rival chocolate shops.
  3. Chain 2: promote a loyal employee to supervise new staff -> retention as the business expands.
  4. Affordability: gross profit €80 282 minus other expenses €12 692 = about €67 590.
  5. Counter: with only three employees, one winner may demotivate the other two; quantity bonus may lower handmade quality.
  6. Judgement: positive short term if a fair team bonus; depends on targets including quality and adding non-financial rewards.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Financial methods of motivation use money to encourage employees, for example bonuses, commission, a pay rise, or a promotion that brings higher pay.

One impact is higher productivity and sales. Irsi Chocolatier could pay a monthly bonus if employees make or sell a target amount of handmade chocolates and jelly fruit sweets. Because extra effort earns extra money, employees may work faster and try harder to persuade customers to buy, so revenue rises above the current €330 606. This matters because there are many other chocolate shops nearby, so every extra sale helps Irsi compete.

A second impact is loyalty as the business expands. Irsi has three loyal employees. Promoting one to a supervisor role with higher pay would reward their experience and give them responsibility for training new staff. This keeps skills in the business, reduces staff turnover and saves recruitment costs as Irsi grows.

The business can afford some extra pay. Gross profit is €80 282, and after other operating expenses of €12 692 about €67 590 is left before costs such as tax, so a small bonus scheme is affordable.

However, there are risks. With only three employees, if the same person wins the bonus every month, the other two may feel they have no chance and become demotivated, so productivity could fall. Promotion has the same problem: only one person can be promoted, so the others may feel unrecognised and might leave, causing a recruitment problem at the very time the business is expanding. In addition, handmade chocolate needs care; a bonus based only on quantity might push employees to rush, lowering quality and harming Irsi's reputation. Finally, the motivating effect of money often fades once a bonus becomes expected.

Overall, I think financial methods would have a positive impact on Irsi Chocolatier in the short term, especially a team bonus shared equally by all employees when the shop's sales target is met. A team bonus avoids jealousy within a team of three and encourages them to help each other and train new staff. However, this depends on the target being fair and on quality as well as quantity being rewarded. In the long term, Irsi may need to combine money with non-financial methods, such as responsibility and praise, to keep a small, close team happy.

Why it reaches the top level: Knows bonus/promotion as financial methods (AO1); uses Irsi's three employees, nearby rivals and Figure 1 figures (AO2); two developed chains to sales and retention (AO3); counter-arguments on jealousy, quality and fading effect, then a supported, conditional judgement (AO4).

Justify · June 2019 · Paper 1 · Q2(f)

  1. Option 1 bonus: e.g. for highest monthly shoe sales -> staff work harder and sell more in a busy centre with rival shoe shops.
  2. Limit: staff who never win may lose motivation; competition may harm teamwork.
  3. Option 2 job enrichment: more responsibility, e.g. setting up the new men's and children's ranges -> staff feel trusted and use their abilities.
  4. Limit: staff not chosen may resent it; extra training time.
  5. Recommend one; depends on whether staff value money or responsibility, and on the expansion into men's and children's shoes needing skilled staff.

Justify · June 2019 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q3(e)

  1. Aim: retain staff using non-financial rewards.
  2. Option 1 job enrichment: extra responsibility such as supervising new recruits -> feel valued -> more committed.
  3. Limit: more responsibility may lead staff to ask for more pay.
  4. Option 2 job rotation: move between shop floor, stock room and displays -> less boredom; managers can move staff at busy times.
  5. Limit: training time and cost; new skills may help staff find other jobs.
  6. Recommend one; depends on whether staff leave because of boredom (rotation) or lack of recognition (enrichment).

  2. Option 1 commission per new pupil: teachers help find pupils -> more revenue for a business with unreliable profits.
  3. Limit: one-off payment; hourly pay unchanged so may not keep teachers long term.
  4. Option 2 higher hourly rate: teachers feel well paid -> stay; easy to understand.
  5. Limit: raises costs every lesson; Ollie invested all his savings and profits are unreliable.
  6. Recommend commission (or hourly rise) with reasons; depends on TADS's cash and whether teachers can attract new pupils.

  3. Limit: new skills may help staff find jobs elsewhere; time lost while moving.
  4. Option 2 job enrichment: more challenging tasks like window displays -> use skills, feel valued.
  5. Limit: training time and cost; not all staff want extra responsibility.
  6. Recommend one; depends on what staff want and NEXT's budget for training.

Justify · November 2021 · Paper 2 · Q2(f)

  1. Aim: keep staff so smartphone/tablet production is continuous and customers are kept.
  2. Option 1 financial (bonus, piece rate): rewards output -> staff productive and stay.
  3. Limit: missed targets mean no bonus -> demotivation; piece rate may lower quality.
  4. Option 2 non-financial (job enrichment, promotion to supervisor): new skills, feel valued -> loyal.
  5. Limit: some staff prefer more money to pay bills.
  6. Recommend one; depends on whether pay is already competitive and on quality needs in production.

Justify · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q3(e)

  1. Aim: retain staff; M&M rose from 20th to 2nd best workplace in India; Maslow's hierarchy in Figure 1.
  2. Option 1 job rotation: move from making parts to helping design parts -> new skills and interest (higher Maslow needs).
  3. Limit: may not raise pay; disruption to production.
  4. Option 2 bonus for meeting production targets: feel valued, work harder (lower Maslow needs).
  5. Limit: group bonus may seem unfair if some work less hard.
  6. Recommend one; depends on which level of needs staff already have met and whether bonuses are individual or team.

Justify · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Q3(e)

  1. Aim: motivate and retain factory workers; IKEA makes affordable flat-pack furniture.
  2. Option 1 bonus: e.g. for making more tables and chairs -> higher output and extra pay -> stay.
  3. Limit: rushing may lower quality; not all staff want the extra effort.
  4. Option 2 job enrichment: training for new tasks like packing -> feel valued, promotion chances.
  5. Limit: training cost; some staff prefer more money.
  6. Recommend one; depends on whether IKEA's priority is low-cost output or long-term loyalty.
