Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Business · 4BS1

Convert prices between currencies with exchange rates

Converting an amount from one currency to another using a given exchange rate, including totalling quantity times unit price before or after conversion.

Type 15 Calculations BUS-CALC-01

★★★★★ · 21 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question gives an exchange rate line such as '£1 = 9.35 CNY' and asks how much is paid, or what the price is, in a different currency.

Knowledge

  • An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another. €1 = 5 MYR means one euro buys five Malaysian ringgits.
  • Find the currency that has the '1' in front of it. Going from that currency to the other one: multiply by the rate. Going the other way: divide by the rate.
  • Rates can be written either way round, for example €1 = £0.84 or 5.65 MYR = £1. Read which side has the 1 every time.
  • If the question gives a quantity (50 handbags, 4 weeks, 6 bicycles), find the total first: quantity × price per unit. You can convert before or after multiplying; the answer is the same.
  • Money answers are normally given to 2 decimal places. Follow any other instruction exactly, such as 'to the nearest euro'.
  • Always write the answer in the currency asked for, with its symbol or code (€, £, MYR, CNY).
  • Sense check: if one unit of the new currency is worth less (many of them for 1), your number should get bigger; if it is worth more, your number should get smaller.

Before you begin

  • Multiplying and dividing decimals with a calculator
  • Rounding to 2 decimal places or to the nearest whole number
  • Common currency symbols and codes (€, £, $, MYR, CNY)

Specification: 1.5.3

Answer technique: Set out a calculation with working (2 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Write the rate as '1 [first currency] = x [second currency]' and underline the currency the answer must be in. Why: This tells you whether to multiply or divide.
  2. Work out the total in the starting currency: quantity × price, or price × number of weeks. Why: Many wrong options leave out the quantity or use the wrong number of weeks.
  3. Multiply by x to go from the '1' currency to the other; divide by x to go the other way. Why: Multiplying when you should divide is the most common trap.
  4. Round as the question asks and add the currency symbol or code. Why: The answer must be in the right currency and to the right accuracy.
  5. Sense check the size of the answer against the rate. Why: A quick check catches a multiply/divide mix-up.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice19Calculate the full answer first. Options are built from dividing instead of multiplying, forgetting the quantity, or adding the rate to the price. Use the Multiple-choice card.
Calculate5Use the Calculate card: write the conversion as one expression, e.g. 1 590 ÷ 0.93, then the answer to 2 decimal places with the currency.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Dividing by the rate when you should multiply, or multiplying when you should divide.
  • Forgetting to multiply by the quantity, or using the wrong number of units or weeks.
  • Adding or subtracting the exchange rate instead of multiplying or dividing by it.
  • Leaving the answer in the starting currency, or multiplying the number of units by the rate and forgetting the price.

Worked explanation

  1. The rate is €1 = 5 MYR, so the euro is the '1' currency.
  2. The bill is in euros (€2 250) and the answer must be in MYR, so we go from the '1' currency to the other: multiply.
  3. 2 250 × 5 = 11 250 MYR.
  4. Sense check: each euro is worth 5 ringgits, so the MYR figure should be bigger. 11 250 is bigger. Match to option C.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Cost in MYR = €2 250 × 5 = 11 250 MYR. Answer: C (11 250).

How the marks are earned

One mark for choosing option C. The 150 kg is extra information; the €2 250 is already the total cost, so it is converted directly.
