4

The method

  1. Name the change

    New rivals, new tastes or changed spending in the case.

  2. Pick a response

    New products, price change, promotion or longer hours.

  3. Link to customers

    Say how it keeps or wins customers, then sales.

  4. Weigh it up

    Add the cost, or when little change is needed.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    No response

    Describing the change is not enough.

  • ✗
    Generic answers

    Use the business in the case.

  • ✗
    No conclusion

    In Evaluate, finish with a clear judgement.

Written-answer check
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Command: Analyse, 6 marks, levels-based: two applied, developed points.
  2. Case facts: many nearby restaurants, new Italian and Turkish restaurants, Mediterranean menu, lunch and dinner only, second oldest restaurant, family-run since 1958, near tourist attractions.
  3. Point 1: new restaurants give customers more choice, so responding protects sales.
  4. Point 2: its long history is a strength, so responses should keep its traditional image.
  5. Chain each point to customers and revenue.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Barcelona now has a number of new restaurants offering different menus, such as Italian and Turkish food, and Can Culleretes is already close to many other restaurants. This gives diners and tourists much more choice. If customer tastes move towards these newer menus while Can Culleretes keeps the same Mediterranean dishes and opens only for lunch and dinner, customers may go elsewhere, so its sales and share of the local market could fall. By responding, for example by adding some new dishes or opening at new times, it can keep its regular customers and attract new ones, protecting its revenue.

However, Can Culleretes is the second oldest restaurant in Spain and has been run by the same family since 1958. This long history and traditional food are what make it different from the new restaurants, and they attract tourists visiting the nearby attractions. If it changed too much, it could lose the traditional image that customers come for. So responding to the market is important, but Can Culleretes should make careful changes that keep its unique identity rather than copying its new competitors.

How the marks are earned

AO2 (3): uses the new Italian and Turkish restaurants, the nearby competitors, its Mediterranean menu, opening times, its 1786 history and family ownership. AO3 (3): each point carries a logical chain to customer choice and revenue, with a balancing chain on its unique image. Level 3 (5-6).

Evaluate · June 2025 · Paper 1 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Changes: market growing 7% a year, more spending on health and gym membership, technology to monitor fitness, online home workouts.
  2. Response 1: market the 14 qualified instructors and social activities -> more members; gross margin about 67.5% (113 362 / 168 000) so extra members add much gross profit.
  3. Response 2: upgrade information-only website to allow booking and personal training plans, sell online classes -> keep home-exercisers.
  4. Counter: small two-gym business may lack funds to keep up; bigger, cheaper gyms may win customers; online classes may replace full memberships.
  5. Judgement: recommend website upgrade using instructors' strength; depends on available profit and members wanting digital services.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The market for Four Fitness is changing in three ways: customers are spending more on their health, new technology makes it easier to monitor fitness, and more people can exercise at home using online workouts.

The first change is a growing market. The global health and fitness market grows by an average of 7% a year, and people in health-conscious Italy are spending more on gym membership. Four Fitness could respond by marketing its 14 professionally qualified instructors and its social activities to attract new members. As demand rises, revenue (currently €168 000) should grow. Four Fitness has a high gross profit margin of about 67.5% (€113 362 ÷ €168 000), so each extra member adds a lot to gross profit, which could be reinvested in new equipment.

The second change is technology. Customers can now track their own fitness, and many watch workout videos online, so they can exercise at home at a lower cost. This is a threat to Four Fitness. It could respond by upgrading its website, which currently gives information only, so that members can book activities and access personal training plans online. It could also sell online classes led by its own instructors. This keeps customers connected to Four Fitness even when they train at home, protects its customer base and creates a new source of revenue.

However, responding has costs and risks. Four Fitness is a small business with only two gyms, so it may not have the money to keep up with fast-changing technology or to build an advanced website and app. Larger gym chains may offer more equipment, more classes and lower prices, and could take customers even while the market grows. Online classes might also pull some members away from paying for full gym membership.

Overall, I think the best response is to upgrade the website to allow online booking and personal training plans, because this is affordable and uses Four Fitness's greatest strength, its qualified and enthusiastic instructors. It meets customers' changing needs without competing on price against bigger gyms. This depends on Four Fitness having enough profit after its other expenses to pay for the website, and on its members wanting digital services; if most members value the social activities and face-to-face training, Gianni should focus on improving the experience inside the gyms instead.

Why it reaches the top level: Identifies changing needs, spending and technology (AO1); uses 7% growth, 14 qualified instructors, information-only website, Figure 4 with a calculated 67.5% margin (AO2); developed response chains (AO3); limits of size, rivals and cannibalisation, then a recommended response with a 'depends on' condition (AO4).

Evaluate · November 2020 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

  1. Identify changes: more people travelling for work and holidays (higher consumer spending) and more low-cost airlines (competition).
  2. Case: 265+ aircraft, 180+ destinations, many service awards, rating fell from first (2016) to fourth (2017).
  3. Chain 1: higher spending on travel -> more demand for Emirates flights -> higher revenue.
  4. Chain 2: low-cost airlines -> more consumer choice -> lower market share and revenue for Emirates.
  5. Counter: meeting extra demand needs pilots, who are costly (730 000 AED) and slow to train, and more aircraft; but strong service can win back customers who do not want low-cost travel.
  6. Judgement: growth helps more than competition hurts if Emirates restores its service rating; depends on pilot supply and its premium positioning.
