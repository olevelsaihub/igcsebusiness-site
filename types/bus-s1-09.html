Social enterprises: meaning and purpose

What a social enterprise is and why an owner might set a business up this way, putting social aims alongside making money.

Type 3 1 Business activity and influences on business BUS-S1-09

★☆☆☆☆ · 5 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

The question asks you to define a social enterprise, or why an owner set up a business as a social enterprise.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Main plan: Grade 8 Plan · Social enterprise, government, trade and external factors

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • A social enterprise is a business that sells goods or services but has a main aim of helping people or the environment.
  • Most of its profit is put back into its social or environmental purpose, or into the community, rather than paid out to owners.
  • People set one up because they want to help their community or a cause while still running a business.
  • A social objective is an aim to benefit society; a social enterprise is a whole business built around such aims.

Before you begin

  • What profit is
  • What a social objective is

Learn these types first: Forms of business ownership and limited liability

Specification: 1.2.3

Answer technique: Define a business term (1 mark) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Say it is a business that trades, like any other business. Why: It still sells goods or services.
  2. Add that its main purpose, and use of profit, is to benefit society or the environment. Why: This social purpose is what earns the mark.
  3. For a State question, give one reason that fits the owner in the case. Why: Context answers must match the named business.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Define4One sentence: a business whose main aim is to help people or the environment, using its profits for that purpose.
State1Give one reason tied to the case, such as using the profits to help the local community.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Describing a charity that does not trade instead of a business.
  • Saying a social enterprise makes no profit; it can make profit but uses it for its purpose.
  • Leaving out the social or environmental purpose, so the definition fits any business.

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A social enterprise is a business that sells goods or services, but its main aim is to help society or the environment, so most of its profit is used for that purpose, such as supporting the local community, rather than being paid to its owners.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for a meaning that combines running a business with using its profits to benefit the community or a social or environmental cause.
