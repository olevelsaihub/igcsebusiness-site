Social enterprises: meaning and purpose

Written

A social enterprise is a business that trades, but its main aim is to help people or the environment. Most of its profit goes back into that purpose, not to the owners.

4

The method

  1. Say it trades

    It is a business that sells goods or services.

  2. Add the purpose

    Its main aim is to benefit society or the environment.

  3. Use of profit

    Most profit goes into that purpose, not to owners.

  4. Why set one up

    Link to the owner: they want to help a cause and still run a business.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Charity

    A social enterprise trades; a charity that only takes donations does not.

  • ✗
    No profit

    It can make profit; it uses it for its purpose.

  • ✗
    Missing the aim

    Without the social purpose, the answer fits any business.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Define, 1 mark.
  2. Say it is a business that trades.
  3. Add the social purpose and what happens to its profit.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A social enterprise is a business that sells goods or services, but its main aim is to help society or the environment, so most of its profit is used for that purpose, such as supporting the local community, rather than being paid to its owners.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for a meaning that combines running a business with using its profits to benefit the community or a social or environmental cause.
