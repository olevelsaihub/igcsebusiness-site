Equal opportunities and minimum wage laws

Laws on equal opportunities (protected characteristics such as gender, race, disability, religion, age) and minimum wage, and how and why businesses comply.

Type 2 2 People in business BUS-S2-14

★★☆☆☆ · 7 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

The question mentions discrimination, equal opportunities, a protected group such as age or disability, employing disabled people, or the minimum wage.

Knowledge

  • Discrimination means treating someone unfairly because of who they are.
  • Equal opportunities laws protect people from discrimination because of gender, race, disability, religion, sexual orientation and age.
  • Skills, ability, experience, political views and 'ethical' or 'social' reasons are not the protected characteristics listed in the syllabus.
  • Businesses must treat people fairly in recruitment, pay, training and promotion; for example equal pay for the same work and open access to training.
  • Businesses may need to make reasonable changes so disabled people can work, such as ramps, adapted equipment or flexible hours.
  • The minimum wage is the lowest pay per hour that a business is legally allowed to pay a worker.
  • Breaking these laws can lead to court or tribunal cases, fines or compensation, and damage to the business's reputation.

Before you begin

  • What wages are and how hourly pay works
  • What recruitment, training and promotion are
  • That governments make laws businesses must follow

Specification: 2.2.4

Answer technique: Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question

Method

  1. Name the law or the protected characteristic involved. Why: Knowing which groups are protected answers most multiple-choice items.
  2. Say what the business must actually do, for example pay equally or adapt the workplace. Why: The practical action is often the identification mark.
  3. Explain the reason or effect: avoiding legal action, treating staff fairly, using a wider pool of talent. Why: This develops the point.
  4. End with the business consequence: reputation, costs of being sued, or better staff and motivation. Why: The final link completes the 3-mark chain.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice5Choose the characteristic the law protects, such as age; reject skills, ability, experience, political or ethical. Minimum wage is the lowest legal pay rate.
Define1Minimum wage: the lowest hourly rate a business is legally allowed to pay its workers.
Explain4One reason or way, then two linked steps, for example breaking the law leads to legal action, then costs and reputation damage.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Choosing 'ability' or 'skills' as a protected characteristic; businesses may choose staff on ability.
  • Writing a definition of equal opportunities when asked for a reason; definitions earn no marks in Explain.
  • Saying the minimum wage is a salary, overtime or bonus.
  • Listing several protected groups without explaining any consequence for the business.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain card: one reason plus two linked expansions; no definition needed.
  2. Reason: it is the law, and breaking it can lead to legal action.
  3. Expansion 1: unfair treatment because of age, gender or race lets the person take the business to a tribunal or court.
  4. Expansion 2: compensation, legal costs and bad publicity hurt the business's finances and reputation.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Businesses comply because equal opportunities is the law, and breaking it can lead to legal action. If a business treats an applicant or employee unfairly because of their age, gender, race or disability, that person can take the business to an employment tribunal or court. The business might then have to pay compensation and legal costs, and the bad publicity could damage its reputation, making it harder to attract customers and good employees.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (legal requirement, risk of legal action). 1 mark for explaining unfair treatment because of a protected characteristic. 1 mark for the consequence: compensation, costs and reputation damage. A definition alone earns nothing.
