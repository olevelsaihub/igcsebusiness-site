The method

  1. Say what the stage does

    Shortlisting filters. Interviews test.

  2. Use the job

    What skills does this job need?

  3. Show the benefit

    Time saved, claims checked, best person chosen.

  4. End on effect

    Service, safety, reputation or costs of a wrong hire.

    How-to answer

    Say why interviews help, not how to run one.

    Shortlisting is not advertising.

Worked explanation

  1. Analyse card: two chains, both about Emirates and pilots.
  2. Context: trainees pay around 730 000 AED for their own training; Emirates wants pilots with the best qualifications and skills; its rating fell from first to fourth; low-cost airlines are entering.
  3. Chain 1: interviews test skills and check CV claims, so only the most capable pilots are hired, protecting safety and reliability.
  4. Chain 2: interviews judge attitude and fit, avoiding the cost of recruiting again, delays and reputational damage from a wrong hire, and helping Emirates win back its reputation against competitors.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Interviewing lets Emirates ask pilots detailed questions about their flying experience and how they would handle problems in the air, and check that what they wrote in their applications is true. This means Emirates can be confident it only employs pilots with the best qualifications and skills, which is its aim. Highly skilled pilots help keep flights safe and on time, protecting the reputation for reliability that has won Emirates its awards.

An interview also lets Emirates judge each pilot's attitude and whether they will work well with cabin crew and give passengers a good experience. Hiring the wrong pilot would be costly for Emirates: it would have to recruit again, and a poor pilot could cause delays or damage its reputation. Because trainees pay around 730 000 AED for their own training, Emirates can choose from committed, well-qualified applicants and use the interview to pick the best of them. By choosing well, Emirates can improve its service and help regain its position as passengers' leading airline as more low-cost airlines compete for travellers.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked: AO2 (3) for applying interviewing to pilots, training cost, rating and competition; AO3 (3) for two connected chains from checking skills and attitude to safety, service, reputation and market position. Detailed context in both: Level 3 (5-6).
