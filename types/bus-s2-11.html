Selection: shortlisting and interviewing candidates

Selecting from applicants: shortlisting against requirements and interviewing to judge skills and fit.

Type 10 2 People in business BUS-S2-11

★☆☆☆☆ · 4 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question mentions shortlisting, interviewing, choosing between applicants or candidates, or selecting a group of applicants.

Knowledge

  • Selection is choosing the best person from the people who applied for a job.
  • Shortlisting means picking out the applicants who best match the job description and person specification, so only they are invited for interview.
  • An interview is a meeting where the business asks the candidate questions to judge their skills, knowledge, attitude and how well they would fit in.
  • Interviews let the business check that what the applicant wrote on the CV or application form is true.
  • Good selection avoids a poor hire, which would waste money on wages, training and recruiting again.

Before you begin

  • Recruitment documents: job description, person specification, CV and application form
  • The order of recruitment: advertise, receive applications, shortlist, interview, appoint

Learn these types first: Recruitment documents: job description to CV

Specification: 2.2.3

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks)

Method

  1. Say what the stage does: shortlisting filters applicants; interviewing tests the shortlisted ones face to face. Why: The purpose of the stage is the starting point for every chain.
  2. Link it to the job in the case and the skills that job needs. Why: Application marks need the specific role, such as pilots or baristas.
  3. Show the benefit: time saved, claims checked, the most suitable person chosen. Why: This is the first development step.
  4. End with the effect on the business: better service, safety, reputation, lower cost of a wrong hire. Why: Analyse rewards chains that reach a business consequence.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice1Shortlisting is the step that picks which applicants go forward to interview; training, marketing and advertising are not selection steps.
Outline1One reason for interviewing plus one context link, for example checking CV details and suitability for the chocolate production line.
Analyse3Two chains in context: what the stage checks, why that gives a better choice, and the effect on service, safety or costs.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Describing how to hold an interview instead of explaining why it helps the business.
  • Confusing shortlisting with advertising or recruitment documents.
  • Writing benefits that could fit any business, with no reference to the job in the case.
  • Stopping at 'the best person is chosen' without showing the effect on the business.

Worked explanation

  1. Analyse card: two chains, both about Emirates and pilots.
  2. Context: trainees pay around 730 000 AED for their own training; Emirates wants pilots with the best qualifications and skills; its rating fell from first to fourth; low-cost airlines are entering.
  3. Chain 1: interviews test skills and check CV claims, so only the most capable pilots are hired, protecting safety and reliability.
  4. Chain 2: interviews judge attitude and fit, avoiding the cost of recruiting again, delays and reputational damage from a wrong hire, and helping Emirates win back its reputation against competitors.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Interviewing lets Emirates ask pilots detailed questions about their flying experience and how they would handle problems in the air, and check that what they wrote in their applications is true. This means Emirates can be confident it only employs pilots with the best qualifications and skills, which is its aim. Highly skilled pilots help keep flights safe and on time, protecting the reputation for reliability that has won Emirates its awards.

An interview also lets Emirates judge each pilot's attitude and whether they will work well with cabin crew and give passengers a good experience. Hiring the wrong pilot would be costly for Emirates: it would have to recruit again, and a poor pilot could cause delays or damage its reputation. Because trainees pay around 730 000 AED for their own training, Emirates can choose from committed, well-qualified applicants and use the interview to pick the best of them. By choosing well, Emirates can improve its service and help regain its position as passengers' leading airline as more low-cost airlines compete for travellers.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked: AO2 (3) for applying interviewing to pilots, training cost, rating and competition; AO3 (3) for two connected chains from checking skills and attitude to safety, service, reputation and market position. Detailed context in both: Level 3 (5-6).
