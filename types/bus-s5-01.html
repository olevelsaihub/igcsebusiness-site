Economies and diseconomies of scale

Falling average costs as a business grows (internal and external) and rising average costs from diseconomies.

Type 2 5 Business operations BUS-S5-01

★★★☆☆ · 10 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question mentions a business growing, getting bigger or expanding, and asks about average or unit costs rising or falling, or names economies or diseconomies of scale.

Knowledge

  • Average cost (unit cost) is the total cost divided by the number of units made: the cost of making one item.
  • Economies of scale: as a business grows and makes more, its average cost per unit falls.
  • Internal economies come from inside the firm, e.g. buying materials in bulk at a discount (purchasing) or borrowing more cheaply (financial).
  • External economies come from outside the firm, e.g. a whole industry growing in one area so skilled workers and suppliers are close by.
  • Diseconomies of scale: when a business grows too big, its average cost per unit starts to rise.
  • Common causes of diseconomies: poor communication across many layers or countries, slow decisions and paperwork (bureaucracy), and workers who feel unimportant and lose motivation.

Before you begin

  • What costs are and the difference between total cost and cost per unit.
  • That businesses can grow by opening more shops, factories or branches.

Specification: 5.1.1 · 5.1.2

Answer technique: Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Answer a multiple-choice question · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Decide whether the question is about costs falling (economies) or rising (diseconomies) as the business grows. Why: Mixing the two up loses the mark straight away.
  2. For a Define, write one sentence linking size or output to average cost per unit. Why: The mark needs both parts: growth and the direction of average cost.
  3. For a State, give one cause that fits the named business (e.g. its shops are in several countries). Why: State answers on a case need to fit that business.
  4. For an Explain, follow the Explain card: name one cause, then two linked steps ending in higher or lower cost per unit. Why: Each linked step earns a mark; a list of causes earns only one.
  5. Finish the chain with its effect on productivity or average cost. Why: The concept is about cost per unit, so the chain should end there.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State1One brief diseconomy that fits the case business, such as communication problems between its factory and shops in different countries.
Define7One sentence: growth or rising output linked to average cost per unit falling (economies) or rising (diseconomies).
multiple-choice1Rising average cost in a growing firm is a diseconomy; purchasing and financial economies both lower costs.
Explain2One cause of the cost change, then two linked consequences ending in a change to productivity or average cost. No marks for a definition alone.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Defining diseconomies as 'total costs rise': all growing firms have higher total costs; the idea is about average cost per unit.
  • Swapping the terms, e.g. calling bulk buying a diseconomy.
  • Listing several causes (communication, motivation, bureaucracy) without explaining any, which earns only one mark on Explain.
  • Writing a definition as the Explain answer, which earns no marks.
  • Stating a diseconomy that does not fit the business in the case.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Explain, 3 marks, so one reason plus two linked developments.
  2. Choose one clear cause: the business becomes so large that workers lose motivation.
  3. Link it: why do they lose motivation? They feel unnoticed in a huge organisation.
  4. Link again: what does that do? They work more slowly and make mistakes, so output per worker falls and cost per unit rises.
  5. Do not start with a definition and do not add a second reason.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

When a business grows very large, each worker can feel like a tiny and unimportant part of the organisation. Managers are far away and rarely notice or thank individual staff, so workers lose motivation and stop putting in extra effort. As a result they work more slowly and make more mistakes, so output per worker falls and the average cost of making each unit goes up.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (the firm becomes so big that workers feel unimportant). 1 mark for the first link (they lose motivation). 1 mark for the second link (slower work and mistakes, so productivity falls and unit cost rises). Total 3.
