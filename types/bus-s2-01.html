Communication: methods, barriers and why it matters

Written

Communication is passing a message from a sender to a receiver who understands it. It can be internal (inside the business) or external (customers, suppliers, bank).

The method

  1. Who talks to whom

    Staff to staff is internal. To customers or suppliers is external.

  2. Name one point

    A benefit, problem, barrier or method feature.

  3. Explain it

    What happens to the work: errors, speed, motivation?

  4. Carry on

    End with costs, sales or customer satisfaction.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Mixing up

    A staff meeting is internal.

  • ✗
    Listing problems

    Explain one problem, do not list three.

  • ✗
    Stopping at 'confused'

    Go on to errors, costs or customers.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Explain one impact, so give one point plus two linked expansions (Explain card).
  2. Pick one impact: staff make mistakes.
  3. Expand with the cause: unclear or late instructions mean workers misunderstand the task.
  4. Expand with the result for the business: wrong service, complaints, lost customers and the cost of putting things right.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One impact is that employees are likely to make mistakes in their work. If a manager's instructions are unclear, incomplete or arrive late, workers can misunderstand which task to do or how to do it. As a result, customers may receive the wrong service, which leads to complaints and lost repeat business, and the business wastes time and money putting the errors right.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for identifying the impact (mistakes in work). 1 mark for explaining how unclear instructions cause the misunderstanding. 1 mark for the linked consequence for the business (complaints, lost customers, wasted time and money). A list of three impacts would earn only 1.

Justify · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Q3(e)

Answer plan

  1. Option 1 noticeboard: rotas and sales targets visible to all shifts (open 8am-5pm every day) -> no lost service time, fewer rota mistakes.
  2. Limit 1: one-way; no feedback; may not be read or updated on busy days.
  3. Option 2 weekly meeting: two-way; staff share customer comments on food and Ciabattas -> protects 5/5 reviews against many nearby cafés; staff feel valued.
  4. Limit 2: must be held outside opening hours -> extra pay or absent staff.
  5. Choose meetings for a small 20-seat café whose edge is service; depends on finding a time all staff can attend (use noticeboard as backup).
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Option 1, a daily noticeboard, is a cheap, written method. Kumaran could post the day's sales targets and staff rotas in the staffroom, so every employee can check their shifts and goals when they arrive. Because Café Pinch is open 8am–5pm every day, staff work different shifts and are not all in the café at once. A noticeboard reaches everyone without stopping service, so the café keeps serving customers and earning revenue while information is shared. A written record also lets staff check the rota again later, which reduces mistakes over shifts.

However, a noticeboard is one-way communication. Staff cannot ask questions or give feedback, and there is no proof they have read it. If Kumaran forgets to update it on a busy day, staff may work from old information.

Option 2, weekly staff meetings, is two-way, face-to-face communication. Kumaran can explain targets, and staff can ask questions and share what customers say about the food, filled Ciabattas and drinks. This feedback helps keep service quality high, which matters because the 5 out of 5 reviews are a key reason customers choose Café Pinch over the many cafés around it. Staff who are listened to feel valued, so motivation may rise and fewer may leave.

However, meetings take time. As the café opens every day, a meeting would have to be held before 8am or after 5pm, so Kumaran may have to pay for the extra time, or some staff may not attend and miss information.

I recommend Option 2, weekly staff meetings. Café Pinch is small, with only 20 seats, so it is likely to have few staff and a short weekly meeting is easy and cheap to organise. Its competitive advantage is excellent customer service, and two-way communication protects that better than a notice that may be ignored. This depends on Kumaran choosing a time when all staff can attend; if he cannot, he should also post rotas on the noticeboard so nobody misses key information.

Why it reaches the top level: Both options applied to Café Pinch facts (8am-5pm daily, 20 seats, 5/5 reviews, nearby rivals); each option has a developed chain to service, motivation or revenue plus a limitation; clear recommendation with a 'depends on' condition and fallback. Meets Level 3 for AO2, AO3 and AO4.

Evaluate · June 2022 · Paper 1 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define internal (managers-staff) and external (customers, suppliers) communication.
  2. Internal: three shops (two malls, Jewel Changi Airport) must give one message on eco-friendly, quality toys and apply VIP 15% correctly (22.80 -> 19.38 SGD) -> consistent service, sales.
  3. External: website messages to VIP members -> repeat purchases; clear orders to suppliers -> right eco-friendly stock on time.
  4. Counter: costs of IT and training; too many emails annoy; communication cannot fix unpopular toys.
  5. Judge: very important, external slightly more so; depends on how many shops and online sales TBTS adds.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Internal communication is the passing of information between people inside a business, such as managers and shop staff. External communication is between the business and outside groups, such as customers and suppliers. Good communication means the message is clear, reaches the right person and is understood.

Good internal communication is important because TBTS runs three shops in different places, two in busy shopping malls and one at Jewel Changi Airport, as well as a website. Managers must make sure staff in all three shops give the same message, for example that every toy is environmentally friendly and chosen for play, value, design and quality. If staff know these points, they can explain them to parents, turning browsers into buyers and raising sales. Clear information about prices also matters. Staff must know that VIP members get 15% off, so the 22.80 SGD dinosaur toy costs 19.38 SGD for a VIP member. If one shop applies the discount wrongly, customers may complain and TBTS loses revenue or goodwill.

Good external communication is also important. TBTS uses its website to tell VIP members about special rates and exclusive offers. Clear, regular messages encourage members to return and buy again, which builds loyalty in malls full of competing shops. With suppliers, TBTS must clearly explain what it needs: high-quality, environmentally friendly toys. If orders and standards are communicated well, deliveries arrive on time and match TBTS's values, so shelves stay stocked and the brand promise is kept. Poor communication could mean the wrong stock or late deliveries, causing lost sales.

However, good communication has costs and limits. Keeping three shops and a website up to date takes time and possibly spending on IT systems or staff training. Too many emails may annoy VIP members. Even excellent communication cannot create sales if the toys are not what children want.

Overall, good communication is very important to TBTS. External communication through the website and VIP scheme is probably the most important, because it directly drives repeat sales, while internal communication keeps the three shops consistent. How important it is depends on how large and spread out TBTS becomes: the more shops and online sales it has, the more it relies on clear communication to keep the same quality message everywhere.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines internal and external communication accurately; applies to three shops, airport site, website, VIP 15% (19.38 SGD worked out), eco-friendly toys and suppliers; developed chains to sales, loyalty and stock; weighs costs and limits; prioritised judgement with a 'depends on' condition. Meets Level 3 on AO1-AO4.

Justify · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q2(f)

  1. Option 1 email/text: reaches Rabanne employees in many countries instantly and in writing -> everyone gets the same update on products such as Invictus or Lady Million.
  2. Limit 1: one-way, easily ignored or misread; depends on internet access; time zones may delay replies.
  3. Option 2 weekly meetings: two-way, so staff can discuss problems and ideas -> fixes the stated problem of employees struggling to talk to each other.
  4. Limit 2: staff are spread across the world, so face-to-face weekly meetings are costly and time-consuming (travel, time zones); video calls may be needed.
  5. Judge: email/text suits a global business for routine updates; depends on whether problems need discussion, in which case add regular online meetings.
