Government spending, taxation and laws

Why governments spend and tax, public services, and how laws affect what businesses may do.

Type 7 1 Business activity and influences on business BUS-S1-20

★☆☆☆☆ · 5 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question mentions government spending, public services, tax rates, legislation, laws or regulations and their effect on a business or the public.

Knowledge

  • The government spends money to provide public services, such as the police, schools, hospitals and roads.
  • Taxes are the main way a government raises money (revenue) to pay for this spending.
  • Legislation means laws passed by the government, often to protect people such as customers, workers or the public.
  • Laws can force businesses to change what they do, for example health and safety rules or limits on ingredients.
  • Following new laws can raise a business's costs (training, new equipment, changing products), but can also protect its reputation.

Before you begin

  • What a government does
  • What costs and demand are

Specification: 1.6.1 · 1.6.2 · 1.7.1 · 2.2.4

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Answer a multiple-choice question · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Name the government action: spending, tax, or a law. Why: Each has a different purpose and effect.
  2. Say what it forces or allows a business or the public to do. Why: This is the first link in the chain.
  3. Follow it to an outcome: costs, prices, demand, health or government spending. Why: Analysis marks come from the chain from policy to result.
  4. For Analyse, follow the Analyse card: two connected points applied to the case. Why: Higher levels need context and logical chains.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State1Give one effect of a law on the named business, for example staff needing retraining in new safety rules.
multiple-choice2Governments raise taxes mainly to raise revenue; public services are those provided by government, such as the police.
Define1Legislation: a law made by the government, often to protect people or businesses.
Analyse1Give two connected reasons or effects of the law, linking it to the public, government spending and businesses in the case.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Thinking higher taxes are used to raise demand or reduce unemployment.
  • Choosing a private business, such as a shop, as a public service.
  • In Analyse answers, stating the law without explaining why it was needed or what it changes.
  • Giving a general effect of laws that does not fit the named business.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the command: Analyse, 6 marks; two connected, applied points.
  2. Point 1: less sugar improves customers' health.
  3. Point 2: healthier people mean less pressure and cost on the health service.
  4. Link to the case: chocolate makers like Cadbury must change their recipes.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Governments have passed laws to cut the sugar in chocolate mainly to improve people's health. Eating too much sugar can cause people to become overweight and can lead to illnesses such as diabetes and tooth decay. If makers such as Cadbury must reduce the sugar in bars like Dairy Milk, customers will take in less sugar each time they eat chocolate, even if they do not change their habits.

A second reason is to reduce the cost to the country of treating these illnesses. When fewer people suffer from sugar-related illnesses, fewer need treatment from the health service. This lowers the demand on doctors and hospitals and reduces government spending on health care, so the money saved can be used for other public services such as schools.

A law also applies to every chocolate maker, so no single firm loses out to rivals by making its products healthier.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): reasons applied to sugar in chocolate and Cadbury's brands (AO2, 3 marks), with logical chains from less sugar to better health and from fewer illnesses to lower health spending (AO3, 3 marks). Total 6.
