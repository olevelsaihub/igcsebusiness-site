Technology and robotics in production

Using robotics and technology in production and balancing cost, productivity, quality and flexibility.

Type 3 5 Business operations BUS-S5-11

★★★☆☆ · 10 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Mentions robots, robotics, automation, computer technology, apps or machines replacing workers in making goods or delivering a service.

Knowledge

  • Robotics means using robots, programmed machines, to carry out production tasks without people doing them.
  • Productivity is the output produced per worker or per hour; technology usually raises it.
  • Robots are accurate and repeat tasks identically, so there are fewer errors and quality is more consistent.
  • Robots can work 24 hours a day without breaks, so more is produced and cost per unit falls.
  • Other technology, such as ordering apps or computer systems, can speed up processes and free staff for other tasks.
  • Drawbacks: high buying and maintenance costs, fewer jobs for workers, and less flexibility than people for one-off tasks.
  • Firms must balance cost, productivity, quality and flexibility when choosing technology.

Before you begin

  • What productivity means (output per worker or per hour).
  • The idea of unit cost and profit.
  • Basic idea of capital-intensive production.

Learn these types first: Capital-intensive and labour-intensive production

Specification: 1.7.1 · 5.2.1 · 5.2.3

Answer technique: Analyse in context (6 marks) · Define a business term (1 mark) · Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks) · Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks) · State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Method

  1. Name one specific gain: fewer errors, faster output, 24/7 working or lower labour cost. Why: A precise gain gives you the first mark.
  2. Explain how the technology causes that gain (programmed to repeat, no breaks, orders reach kitchen instantly). Why: The 'how' is the linked development examiners credit.
  3. End with the result: better quality, higher productivity, lower unit cost or higher profit. Why: Finishing the chain reaches the final development mark.
  4. Use the case product or process when a business is named (e.g. its smartphones, toys or perfume). Why: Outline and Analyse marks need context.
  5. Where asked to judge, weigh the gain against cost of the technology and job losses. Why: The topic is about balancing cost, productivity, quality and flexibility.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State1One benefit of the technology fitting the business, e.g. better sound quality for a drum school.
Define1Robotics: robots carrying out production tasks in place of people.
Explain2One reason or impact (fewer errors, more output) plus two linked effects ending in quality, cost or profit. No definition marks.
Outline3One reason plus one linked effect in context, e.g. robots work non-stop so more of the firm's product is made.
Analyse3Two developed benefits in context, such as fewer faulty items rejected and higher output lowering cost per unit.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Saying robots are 'better' or 'more efficient' without saying how or with what result.
  • Defining robotics as 'using technology' without mentioning machines doing tasks instead of people.
  • Giving a generic reason when the question names a business and its product.
  • Listing several benefits (speed, accuracy, no breaks) without developing one, which earns one mark on Explain.
  • Claiming robots are cheap: they lower unit cost over time but cost a lot to buy.

Worked explanation

  1. Command is Explain for 3 marks: one reason plus two linked developments.
  2. Reason: robots reduce production errors.
  3. First link: why? They are programmed to repeat the same task identically and do not tire.
  4. Second link: so products are made to a consistent standard and fewer faulty items are produced.
  5. Stick to one reason; do not also list speed or 24/7 working.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Using robots reduces mistakes in production. A robot can be programmed to carry out exactly the same movement in exactly the same way every time, and it does not get tired or bored like a person might. This means every product is made to the same standard, so quality is more consistent and fewer faulty items have to be thrown away or repaired.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the reason (fewer production errors). 1 mark for how (programmed to repeat identically without tiring). 1 mark for the result (consistent, higher quality and fewer faulty items). Total 3.
