Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Business · 4BS1

Calculate the operating profit margin

Calculating operating profit margin as operating profit divided by revenue times 100, deriving operating profit from gross profit minus expenses when needed.

Type 14 Calculations BUS-CALC-15

★☆☆☆☆ · 5 of 32 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Asks for the operating profit margin, usually with revenue, gross profit and other operating expenses in a figure.

See frequency evidence and how ratings work

Main plan: Grade 8 Plan · Statement of financial position and ratio calculations

KnowledgeMethodWorked examplePracticeTeach it back

Knowledge

  • Operating profit = gross profit − other operating expenses (overheads such as rent, salaries, advertising).
  • Operating profit margin = operating profit ÷ revenue × 100.
  • It is always lower than the gross profit margin because overheads have also been taken away.
  • Pearson provides the ratio formulae in the exam, but you must know which figures go where and how to round.
  • Usually give the answer to 2 decimal places as a percentage.
  • Figures in £000s or millions are fine when both figures use the same unit.

Before you begin

  • Gross profit and gross profit margin (BUS-CALC-14)
  • Meaning of operating expenses
  • Rounding to 2 decimal places

Learn these types first: Calculate the gross profit margin

Specification: 3.5.1

Answer technique: Set out a calculation with working (2 marks)

Method

  1. Find operating profit; if not given, gross profit − other operating expenses. Why: The top of the formula is operating profit, not gross profit.
  2. Find revenue. Why: The bottom of the formula is revenue.
  3. Divide operating profit by revenue and multiply by 100. Why: This gives the margin as a percentage.
  4. Round as asked and add %. Why: Rounding earns the second mark.

How each command word is answered

Calculate7Use the Calculate card: show gross profit − expenses = operating profit, then operating profit ÷ revenue × 100 and the rounded percentage.

Common mistakes

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Using gross profit instead of operating profit.
  • Dividing by gross profit instead of by revenue.
  • Subtracting the expenses from revenue instead of from gross profit.

Worked explanation

  1. From Figure 1: revenue €330 606, gross profit €80 282, other operating expenses €12 692.
  2. Operating profit = 80 282 − 12 692 = €67 590.
  3. Operating profit margin = 67 590 ÷ 330 606 × 100 = 20.444…%.
  4. To two decimal places: 20.44%.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Operating profit = 80 282 − 12 692 = €67 590. Operating profit margin = 67 590 ÷ 330 606 × 100 = 20.44%.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1: operating profit of €67 590 (or the ratio set up). Mark 2: 20.44%. The correct answer alone earns both.
