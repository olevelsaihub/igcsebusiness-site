Before you start

  • Plans are learning targets, not grade guarantees. A plan name says what it aims to prepare you for; your result depends on your own work and the exam.
  • Stars describe the past, not the future. They summarise how often a type appeared in the 32 eligible 4BS1 papers from 2019 to 2025 (June 2021 assessment material is kept for practice but not counted). They are not predictions.
  • Model answers are authored. Worked examples and model answers are written by olevels.com and checked against the official schemes; they are not official Pearson mark schemes.

Answer technique cards

How each command word is marked, and a method for answering it. Use them alongside every type.

01

Analyse in context (6 marks)

Write a longer answer that opens up a business idea or issue and follows its effects step by step, always using the business in the case.

02

Set out a calculation with working (2 marks)

Use maths on the data you are given to work out an answer, showing your working (or fill in missing values in a table).

03

Define a business term (1 mark)

Give the exact meaning of a business term from the course.

04

Evaluate and reach a supported conclusion (12 marks)

Write a longer answer that uses your business knowledge and the case to reach a judgement that is backed up with reasons.

05

Explain: point plus two linked expansions (3 marks)

Give one fact about a business idea and then add two further points that develop it; no case is needed.

06

Justify a choice between two options (9 marks)

Write a longer answer that uses the information given to recommend which one of two options a business should choose.

07

Answer a multiple-choice question

Choose the correct answer from the options given; some test what you know and some need a quick calculation.

08

Outline: two linked points in context (2 marks)

Give two linked points about a business idea, placed in the context of the business in the question.

09

State or give a point from the case (1 mark)

Give a short answer, no more than a sentence, using a piece of information about the business in the case (Give tests recall; Identify means reading the answer from a graph or table).
