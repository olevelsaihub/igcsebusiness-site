Cash-flow forecasts: terms, uses and limits

Before a school trip, you might write down the money you expect to get and spend each week. Then you can see early if you will run short, and do something about it. A cash-flow forecast estimates the cash expected to come in and go out each month in the future. Net cash flow is inflows minus outflows. The closing balance is the opening balance plus net cash flow, and it becomes next month's opening balance.

Step one: check the term. Net cash flow is always inflows minus outflows, and it is negative when more goes out than comes in. Look at February: net cash flow is minus two thousand. Step two: say what the forecast shows in advance, then step three, link it to an action. Seeing a shortfall early gives time to arrange an overdraft, delay spending, or show a bank before borrowing. Step four: remember the limit. A forecast is only an estimate. If sales fall or costs rise, the figures will be wrong.

Here is a real question from June 2023, Paper two. Explain one reason why cash-flow forecasting is important to a business. It is worth three marks. Mark one: the reason. The forecast shows in advance the months when cash may run short. Mark two: knowing early gives managers time to act, for example by arranging an overdraft or putting off a large purchase. Mark three: the business can keep paying its staff and suppliers that month, instead of running out of cash and being forced to close.

A common mistake is subtracting the wrong way round. Net cash flow is inflows minus outflows. Another is saying a forecast guarantees success. It is only an estimate, and it can be wrong.

Now you try. A bike shop forecasts April inflows of six thousand dollars and outflows of eight thousand five hundred. Its opening balance is one thousand five hundred. What is the closing balance, and what should the shop do? Pause and decide.

Net cash flow is six thousand minus eight thousand five hundred, which is minus two thousand five hundred. The closing balance is one thousand five hundred minus two thousand five hundred, which is minus one thousand dollars. So the shop should act now, for example arrange an overdraft of at least one thousand dollars, or delay a large purchase. Then it can still pay its suppliers and staff in April.
