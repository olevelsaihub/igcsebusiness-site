Idea. Think about a fridge at home stuffed with food. Some of it goes off before anyone eats it. That is waste: wasted food, wasted space and wasted money. Lean production means making goods with as little waste as possible: waste of materials, time, space and money. Inventory, or stock, is the materials and finished goods a business holds.

Method. Just-in-time, or JIT, means materials arrive, or goods are made, only when they are needed. Look at these example figures: a business using JIT holds far less stock. Kaizen means continuous improvement. All employees regularly suggest small changes to how work is done. It can improve quality and cut waste, but it depends on staff being willing to take part. To answer any lean question, name the lean idea, say which waste it removes, then chain it to lower costs, better quality or higher productivity. Use the case product if a business is named.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question from Paper two in June 2025. Explain one reason why many manufacturing businesses use just-in-time. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the reason. JIT cuts the amount of inventory a manufacturer holds. Mark two links it. Materials arrive only when they are about to be used, so less warehouse space is needed, and fewer materials go out of date or get damaged. Mark three gives the result. Storage and waste costs fall, so overall costs fall and the business can make more profit. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is mixing up the two methods. JIT is about stock arriving when needed. Kaizen is about continuous small improvements. Another mistake is saying JIT means fast production. It means receiving or making stock only when it is needed.

Your turn. Now you try. At a bicycle factory, workers meet every Friday to suggest small changes, such as moving tools closer to the assembly line. Which lean method is this? Give one benefit and one risk. Pause and decide.

Answer. It is Kaizen: continuous small improvements suggested by the workers. Moving tools closer saves time on every bicycle, so productivity rises and costs fall. The risk: it only works if staff are willing to take part. Remember the method: name the lean idea, say which waste it removes, then link it to lower costs or better quality for the business.
