Market research data: types and reliability

Idea. Imagine a juice bar is thinking of selling a new mango smoothie. Before it spends money, it asks fifty customers what they think. Forty of the fifty say they would buy it. Information in the form of numbers like this is called quantitative data. It is easy to compare and put into a chart, but it does not tell you why. Some customers also say: it tastes too sweet. Information about opinions, feelings and reasons is called qualitative data. It gives deeper reasons, but it is harder to analyse.

Method. Step one: identify the data type. Step two: say what it tells this business. Step three: link it to a decision, such as changing the price, changing the product or targeting a new group. Social media is one way to collect data. Polls, comments, likes and reviews give a business data quickly and cheaply, from huge numbers of people. Step four: ask how trustworthy the data is. A small or biased sample, dishonest answers, or data that is out of date are all unreliable, and can lead to poor decisions.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper two in November 2023. Analyse the benefits to Kraft Heinz of using social media to collect market research data. It is worth six marks: aim for two points, each applied to Kraft Heinz with a chain. First pull out the case facts. Kraft Heinz is a global food business. It has launched a new tomato sauce and changed its Mac and Cheese recipe. Point one. Social media reaches huge numbers of people in many countries. A poll on the new sauce gives data within days, and more cheaply than a survey in each country. So Kraft Heinz can decide early to make more, change it, or withdraw it. Point two. Customers explain in their own words what they think of the new recipe. This qualitative data shows why they like or dislike the taste. Kraft Heinz can adjust the recipe, which protects sales and the brand.

Common mistake. Mistake one: swapping the definitions. Opinions are qualitative. Numbers are quantitative. Mistake two: writing about social media as advertising. This question is about collecting research data, not promoting the product.

Your turn. Now you try. A cinema asks one hundred visitors to score its new seats out of ten, and also asks what they would improve. Which answers are quantitative, and which are qualitative? Give one way the cinema could use the qualitative data. Pause and decide.

Answer. The scores out of ten are numbers, so they are quantitative. The suggestions for improvement are opinions and reasons, so they are qualitative. For example, if many visitors say the seats are too close together, the cinema knows why some scores are low. It can add more legroom, so visitors are happier and come back. Remember the method: name the data type, say what it tells the business, then link it to a decision, and check it is reliable. Now try the practice questions.
