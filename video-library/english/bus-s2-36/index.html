Dismissing an employee

Idea. Imagine a shop worker is caught taking money from the till. The manager ends their job. This is called dismissal. Dismissal means the employer ends a worker's job because of something the worker has done, or cannot do. Redundancy is different: the job itself is no longer needed, so it is not the worker's fault.

Method. Fair reasons include serious misconduct, such as theft, violence or breaking safety rules, repeated lateness or absence, and being unable to do the job properly. Businesses usually follow a disciplinary procedure first, for example a verbal warning, then a written warning. Dismissal comes only if the problem continues. For serious misconduct, such as theft, a worker may be dismissed without earlier warnings, but only after a fair investigation. Unfair dismissal is ending someone's job for a reason the law does not allow, such as their age, religion, gender or race. To answer an Outline question, give one fair reason, set it in the business's own work, then add the consequence for the business.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one in November twenty twenty-four. Outline one reason why Saha may dismiss an employee. It is worth two marks. Saha makes robots for hospitals, hotels and restaurants. Mark one is the reason. Serious misconduct, such as stealing robot parts or ignoring safety rules when assembling robots. Mark two is the development. This could damage robots or injure other workers, so orders to hospitals and restaurants are delayed and Saha's reputation suffers.

Common mistake. Mistake one: giving a redundancy reason. Needing fewer staff is not the worker's fault, so it is not dismissal. Mistake two: giving an unfair reason, such as age or religion. That would break the law.

Your turn. Now you try. A bus company has a driver who keeps arriving late for shifts, even after warnings. Outline one reason the company may dismiss this driver. Pause and decide.

Answer. The reason is repeated lateness. When the driver is late, buses leave late and passengers miss their connections. The company gets complaints and may lose customers. That is the pattern for this type: give a fair reason, set it in the business, then add the consequence. Now try the practice questions.
