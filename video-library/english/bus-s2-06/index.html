Types of employment: full-time, part-time, temporary

Idea. Think of an ice-cream shop by the beach. In summer it is packed and needs lots of staff. In winter it is quiet and needs only a few. So businesses employ people on different kinds of contract, to fit the work they have. These are called types of employment.

Method. Full-time employees work the normal working week, often around thirty-five to forty hours. Part-time employees work fewer hours, for example only mornings or weekends. A job share splits one full-time job between two part-time workers. Other types are about when, or for how long, people work. Casual staff are called in only when needed. Seasonal staff work at busy times of year. Temporary staff are hired for a fixed period, for example to cover illness. Permanent full-time staff give stability, commitment and experience, but must be paid even when it is quiet. Flexible staff keep wage costs down in quiet times, but may be less committed and need more training. To explain, name the type, find the work pattern in the case, such as busy seasons or sickness cover, give one benefit, then develop it through cost, flexibility or commitment to an effect on customers or profit.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one in June twenty twenty-four. Explain one benefit to a business of using seasonal employees. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the benefit. The business can change the size of its workforce to match demand. Mark two explains how. It takes on and pays extra workers only in its busiest times, such as holidays, when permanent staff could not cope alone. Mark three is the consequence. Customers are still served quickly at peak times, so sales are not lost, while the wage bill stays lower in quiet months. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is writing a definition. The question asks for a benefit, and a definition earns no marks in an Explain answer. Another mistake is mixing up part-time and temporary. Part-time is about hours each week. Temporary is about how long the job lasts.

Your turn. Now you try. A garden centre's delivery driver will be off sick for three months. Which type of employment should it use to cover the driver, and what is one benefit? Pause and decide.

Answer. A temporary contract for three months. The cover is only needed while the driver is away, so deliveries carry on and customers are kept happy. When the driver returns, the garden centre stops paying the extra wage. Remember the method: name the type, match it to the work pattern in the case, then develop one benefit to customers or costs.
