Idea. Think of your favourite crisps. Every year or so the brand brings out a new flavour. Why? Because people get bored, and a new flavour gives them a reason to buy again. A new product is one part of the marketing mix. The mix is the four Ps: product, price, place and promotion. A product can be a good or a service. A good is a physical item you can touch, such as a car. A service is an activity done for you, such as washing a car or giving advice.

Method. Businesses keep introducing new products to keep customers interested, keep up with trends, and replace products that are declining. A new feature can also give a unique selling point: something that makes it different from rivals. Step one: when a case gives options, say which part of the mix each one changes. A new drink changes the product. Half-price Mondays change the price. Selling online changes the place. A radio advert is promotion. Step two: link the option to customers: who it attracts, and why they would buy. Then carry the chain to revenue or market share, using a fact from the case. Step three: give a drawback. New products cost money to develop and may fail, so research and careful planning reduce the risk.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper two R in November 2020. Explain one benefit to a business of continually introducing new products. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the benefit: new products keep customers interested in the business. Mark two is the link. When older products go out of date, or customers get bored, a new one gives them a reason to come back instead of switching to a competitor. Mark three is the result. The business keeps up with changing trends, so its sales are maintained over time.

Common mistake. Mistake one: calling a service a good. Gardening is done for you, so it is a service. Mistake two: adding profit or production to the marketing mix. The four Ps are product, price, place and promotion.

Your turn. Now you try. A café's sales are falling. Option A is to add a new range of smoothies. Option B is to cut all prices by ten per cent. Which part of the mix does each option change? Give one drawback of option A. Pause and decide.

Answer. Option A changes the product. Option B changes the price. A drawback of option A: the café must pay for blenders and fresh fruit, and if customers do not like the smoothies, that money is lost. That is the method for this type: name the part of the mix, link it to customers and sales, then weigh the drawback. Now try the practice questions.
