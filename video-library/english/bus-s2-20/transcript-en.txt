Idea. Why do you work hard at school? Some students do it for a reward, some for praise, and some because they enjoy a challenge. Motivation is the desire to work hard and do a job well. Three theorists explain workplace motivation. Taylor said money. Maslow said a ladder of needs. Herzberg said two kinds of factor.

Method. Taylor believed workers are motivated mainly by money, so pay should be linked to output. This is piece rate. Make forty items at fifty cents each, and you earn twenty dollars. Maslow's hierarchy has five levels. From the bottom: basic needs, safety, social, esteem, and self-actualisation at the top. Lower needs must be met before higher needs motivate. Social needs are level three. Herzberg split factors into two groups. Hygiene factors, such as pay, working conditions and job security, only stop staff feeling dissatisfied. Motivators, such as achievement, recognition, responsibility and promotion, truly motivate. To answer, identify the theory, place the factor or level correctly, give a workplace example, then explain the effect on workers.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper two in June twenty twenty-four. Explain one of the factors in Herzberg's motivational theory. It is worth three marks. Choose just one factor. Mark one names the factor with an example. A motivator, such as being given more responsibility or recognition for good work. Mark two explains it. Motivators come from the job itself, so the worker gains a feeling of achievement and personal growth. Mark three gives the effect. Staff are more satisfied and truly motivated, so they work harder and are more productive.

Common mistake. Mistake one: saying Herzberg's hygiene factors, like pay, motivate staff. He said they only prevent dissatisfaction. And never link hygiene factors to Maslow. Mistake two: counting Maslow's levels from the top. Always count from the bottom.

Your turn. Now you try. A supermarket gives its workers permanent contracts instead of temporary ones. Which level of Maslow's hierarchy does this meet? Pause and decide.

Answer. A permanent contract gives job security, so it meets safety needs, level two. With that need met, social needs at level three can start to motivate the workers. That is the pattern for this type: identify the theory, place the factor correctly, then explain its effect on workers. Now try the practice questions.
