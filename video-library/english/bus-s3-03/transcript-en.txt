Idea. Imagine you want to start a car-washing business. You need buckets, sponges and a hose. Where could the money come from? Your savings, selling an old bike, or borrowing from a bank. Finance is the money a business needs to start up, to run day to day, or to grow. Short-term finance is for less than a year, such as paying bills when cash is low. Long-term finance is for big items, such as a new factory.

Method. Step one: classify the source. Internal sources come from inside the business or its owner. External sources come from outside. An overdraft lets you take out more than is in your account. Trade credit means buying now and paying the supplier later. A bank loan is a set amount repaid with interest. Share capital is money from selling shares, and only companies can do this. Venture capital comes from investors in new, risky businesses, who take a share of ownership. Crowdfunding is many people each giving a small amount online. Step two: recall each source's features: its cost, how fast it arrives, whether it is repaid, and whether owners lose control. Step three: match the source to the need in the case.

Worked example. Here is a question from the June 2021 assessment materials, Paper one. Explain one benefit to a business of using crowdfunding as a source of finance. It is worth three marks. Mark one: the benefit. A business can raise money even when banks will not lend to it. Mark two: a new idea with no trading record looks risky to a bank, but many members of the public can each put in a small amount because they like it, so the risk is spread. Mark three: so the business collects the total it needs and its project can go ahead, instead of being dropped.

Common mistake. A common mistake is mixing up internal and external. Retained profit and selling assets are internal. A bank loan is external. Another is saying a sole trader can sell shares. Only companies can raise share capital.

Your turn. Now you try. A bakery needs eight hundred dollars for two weeks to buy flour, until a big wedding order is paid. Which suits it better, an overdraft or share capital? Give one reason. Pause and decide.

Answer. An overdraft. The need is small and short-term. An overdraft is quick to arrange and is soon paid off when the wedding money arrives, and the owners keep full control. That is the pattern for this type: classify the source, match it to the need, then explain its effect on the business. Now try the practice questions.
