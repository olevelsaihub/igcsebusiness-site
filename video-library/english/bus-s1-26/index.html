External factors affecting business decisions

Idea. Picture a small juice bar. One year, people start worrying about sugar. A new app lets customers order online. The town bans plastic straws. None of these were the owner's choice, but each one changes what the juice bar must do. These are external factors: things outside a business's control that affect its decisions. There are four groups to learn: social, technological, environmental and political. Social means changes in lifestyles and tastes, such as becoming more health conscious. Technological means new technology, like online selling. Environmental means concern about pollution, waste and recycling. Political means government actions, such as taxes and laws.

Method. Step one. Classify the factor: social, technological, environmental or political. Step two. Find what the business in the case already does or faces. Step three. Ask what the factor changes: what customers want, the business's costs, or its reputation. Step four. Follow it to an outcome: sales, profit, or meeting the business's aims.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one R in November 2020. Loaf is a bakery and cookery school, and it is a social enterprise. Analyse the importance of environmental factors for Loaf. It is worth six marks. First, classify. More people now want businesses to be green. This is an environmental factor. Point one. Loaf's Waste Not scheme gives leftover food to charities instead of throwing it away. This cuts waste and shows local people that Loaf cares, so more customers may buy its bread and book its cookery courses, raising revenue. Point two. Loaf's recycling hub attracts shoppers who care about the environment. If Loaf recycles its own packaging too, it can cut costs and make more profit, which it can put back into its social aims.

Common mistake. Mistake one: putting a factor in the wrong group, for example calling taxation environmental. Taxes and laws are political. Mistake two: describing the factor in general, without using what the named business does. Always bring in the case.

Your turn. Now you try. PageTurn is a bookshop. More and more customers now read e-books on their phones. Which kind of external factor is this, and what effect could it have on PageTurn? Pause and decide.

Answer. This is a technological factor. Customers who read e-books buy fewer printed books, so PageTurn's sales and profit fall. It may respond by selling e-books online, which costs money at first but could win back customers. Remember the method: classify the factor, find it in the case, say what it changes, then follow it to an outcome. Now try the practice questions.
