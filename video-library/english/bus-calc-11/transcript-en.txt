Increasing or decreasing a value by a percentage is one of the most common calculations in International GCSE Business. Let's start from the very beginning. Per cent means out of one hundred. So ten per cent means ten out of every hundred, which is the same as zero point one. Imagine a T-shirt that costs forty dollars. Ten per cent of forty dollars is forty times zero point one, which is four dollars. If the price rises by ten per cent, we add the four dollars on, giving forty-four dollars. If the price is cut by ten per cent, we take the four dollars away, giving thirty-six dollars.

Before any working, decide the direction. Pay rises, interest added to a loan, tax added to a price, and growth all make the value bigger. Discounts, cuts and reductions make it smaller. Next, find the base: the value the percentage is taken from. If a customer buys several items, multiply first to get the full total. Then apply the percentage once, to that total. Then find the percentage amount: the base times the rate, divided by one hundred. Add it for an increase, or subtract it for a decrease. Round as the question asks, usually to two decimal places for money. There is also a quick route. For an increase, multiply by one plus the rate: a three point five per cent rise means times one point zero three five. For a decrease, multiply by one minus the rate: a twelve point five per cent discount means times zero point eight seven five.

Here is a real exam question from Paper 1 in June 2024. A minimum wage of seven euros forty-five an hour rises by three point five per cent. Calculate the new hourly wage. It is worth two marks. Find three point five per cent of seven forty-five. Seven forty-five times zero point zero three five is zero point two six zero seven five, about twenty-six cents. Now add it on. Seven forty-five plus zero point two six gives seven euros seventy-one. Check with the quick route. Seven forty-five times one point zero three five is seven point seven one, to two decimal places. The same answer.

The most common mistake is stopping too early. Twenty-six cents is only the increase. The question asks for the new wage. Another mistake is adding the percentage as if it were money: seven forty-five plus three point five. Always turn the percentage into an amount first.

Now you try. A school orders twenty-five calculators at twelve dollars each. The supplier gives a fifteen per cent discount. Calculate the total cost after the discount. Pause here and work it out.

First the base. Twenty-five times twelve dollars is three hundred dollars. A discount is a decrease. Fifteen per cent of three hundred is forty-five dollars. Take it away: three hundred minus forty-five is two hundred and fifty-five dollars. Or three hundred times zero point eight five, which is also two hundred and fifty-five. Remember the method: find the base, decide up or down, find the amount, then add it or take it away. Now try the practice questions for this type.
