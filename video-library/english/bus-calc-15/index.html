Imagine a lemonade stall that sells one hundred dollars of lemonade in a month. The lemons, sugar and cups cost forty dollars. That leaves sixty dollars. This is the gross profit. But the stall has other costs too: fifteen dollars rent for the pitch and five dollars for posters. These are overheads, also called other operating expenses. Take them away and sixty dollars falls to forty dollars. This is the operating profit. The operating profit margin shows how much operating profit is made from each dollar of sales. Forty divided by one hundred, times one hundred, is forty per cent. The stall keeps forty cents from every dollar it sells.

The formula is operating profit divided by revenue, times one hundred. It is given in the exam. The top is operating profit. The bottom is revenue. Step one, find operating profit. If it is not given, take the other operating expenses away from gross profit. Step two, divide by revenue and multiply by one hundred. Step three, round as asked, usually to two decimal places, and add the per cent sign.

Here is a real question from Paper one, June twenty twenty-three, about a chocolate shop in Brussels. Calculate, to two decimal places, the operating profit margin. It is worth two marks. The figure gives revenue of three hundred and thirty thousand six hundred and six euros, gross profit of eighty thousand two hundred and eighty-two euros, and other operating expenses of twelve thousand six hundred and ninety-two euros. First, operating profit. Eighty thousand two hundred and eighty-two minus twelve thousand six hundred and ninety-two is sixty-seven thousand five hundred and ninety euros. Now divide by revenue and multiply by one hundred. That gives twenty point four four four and more. To two decimal places, twenty point four four per cent.

The most common mistake is using gross profit instead of operating profit. That gives twenty-four point two eight per cent, which ignores the overheads. Another mistake is taking the expenses away from revenue instead of from gross profit. The expenses come off gross profit.

Now you try. A gym has revenue of one hundred and sixty thousand dollars, gross profit of sixty-four thousand dollars, and other operating expenses of thirty-seven thousand three hundred dollars. Calculate its operating profit margin to two decimal places. Pause here and work it out.

Operating profit is sixty-four thousand minus thirty-seven thousand three hundred, which is twenty-six thousand seven hundred dollars. Divide by one hundred and sixty thousand and multiply by one hundred: sixteen point six eight seven five. To two decimal places, sixteen point six nine per cent. Remember the method: find operating profit, divide by revenue and multiply by one hundred, then round and add the per cent sign. Now try the practice questions for this type.
