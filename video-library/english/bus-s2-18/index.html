Think about your school science lab. Before you light a Bunsen burner, you put on goggles, learn the rules and find the fire exit. Workplaces do the same, to stop people getting hurt. Health and safety laws require businesses to protect employees and customers from harm. A hazard is anything that could cause harm, such as hot equipment, trailing wires or heavy machinery.

How does a business comply? It gives safety training, often during induction. It provides protective clothing and safety signs, checks its equipment, and has clear emergency procedures. Complying brings benefits. There are fewer accidents, so fewer staff are off sick. Staff feel safer and more motivated, and customers are happier to use the business. Breaking the law is serious. The business can be fined, sued, or even closed down, and its reputation can suffer. To answer, use one chain. Name one safety action. Link it to the hazards in this workplace. Explain the result. Then end with the effect on the business.

Here is a real question from Paper two in November twenty twenty. Explain one reason why employees should undertake health and safety training as part of their induction programme. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the reason. New staff learn the dangers of their workplace before they start work. Mark two develops it. They learn how to use equipment safely and what to do in an emergency. Mark three is the effect on the business. There are fewer accidents, so fewer staff are off work, and there is less risk of fines or legal action.

The most common mistake is stopping at keeping people safe. In a three-mark answer, carry on to what that means for the business. Another mistake is listing many safety measures with no explanation. One measure, fully explained, scores more.

Now you try. A pizza restaurant uses very hot ovens and sharp knives. Explain one reason the restaurant should check its ovens regularly. Pause and decide.

Regular checks find faults before an oven burns someone. So fewer cooks are injured or off work, and orders keep going out on time. The restaurant also avoids fines and protects its reputation. That is the pattern for this type: name the safety action, link it to the hazards, then explain the effect on the business. Now try the practice questions.
