Think of your school report. It shows how you did last term, so you can decide what to work on next. A business has a report like this too: its income statement. The income statement shows revenue, costs and profit over a period, usually a year. Here is a café. Sales minus cost of sales gives gross profit. Gross profit minus expenses gives operating profit.

Step one: name the figure. Step two: compare it. A figure alone means little. This café's operating profit rose from eighteen thousand dollars last year to twenty-five thousand this year. Step three: link the comparison to a decision. Rising profit might mean the café can afford to open a second branch. Different people use these documents. Managers decide whether to expand or cut costs. Banks judge whether a loan can be repaid. Shareholders look at profits and likely dividends. But the documents show past results only. They cannot guarantee what will happen next year.

Here is a real question from June 2025, Paper two R. Explain one reason why businesses use statements of comprehensive income when making decisions. It is worth three marks. Mark one: the reason. It shows whether the business made a profit or a loss over the year. Mark two: comparing this year's sales, costs and profit with last year, or with similar businesses, shows whether performance is improving. Mark three: this guides a decision, such as whether the business can afford to launch a new product or must cut expenses first.

A common mistake is putting assets or capital employed on the income statement. They belong on the statement of financial position. Another is describing a figure without comparing it or saying what decision follows.

Now you try. A gym's operating profit fell from forty thousand dollars to twenty-two thousand dollars, but its revenue stayed the same. Explain one decision the managers might make. Pause and decide.

Operating profit fell by eighteen thousand dollars while revenue stayed the same, so the gym's costs must have risen. Managers might check which expenses rose and cut them before spending on anything new. That is the pattern for this type: name the figure, compare it, then link it to a decision.
