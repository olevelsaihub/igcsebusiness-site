Span of control, chain of command and delegation

Idea. Think about your school. The head teacher is in charge of a few heads of department. Each head of department is in charge of several teachers. Drawn as a chart, it looks like this. The span of control is the number of workers a manager is directly responsible for. Here, the head teacher directly manages three people, so the span of control is three. The chain of command is the line of authority that instructions pass down, from the top of the business to the workers. More levels mean a longer chain. Delegation means a manager passes the authority to do a task to someone lower down. It frees the manager's time, and staff who feel trusted are often more motivated.

Method. Step one. Define the term in your own words. Step two. Decide what the case shows: a wide or narrow span, or a long or short chain. Step three. State one benefit or reason. Step four. Link it to supervision, the speed and accuracy of communication, decision-making, or motivation.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper two in June 2019. Explain one benefit to a business of having a short chain of command. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the benefit. Messages pass through fewer levels of management. Mark two explains why that helps. Instructions travel more directly, so they are less likely to be changed or misunderstood on the way. Mark three is the consequence. Problems are sorted out and decisions acted on more quickly, so fewer mistakes are made.

Common mistake. The biggest mistake is mixing up the two terms. Span of control is how many people one manager controls. Chain of command is how many levels instructions pass through. Another mistake is stopping at communication is better. Always show the effect: fewer errors, or faster decisions.

Your turn. Now you try. At a supermarket, one store manager directly supervises fifteen cashiers. Is this a wide or a narrow span of control? Give one problem it could cause. Pause and decide.

Answer. Fifteen cashiers for one manager is a wide span of control. One problem: the manager cannot watch everyone closely, so mistakes may be missed and productivity may fall. Remember the method: define the term, decide what the case shows, state a benefit or problem, then link it to the effect on the business. Now try the practice questions.
