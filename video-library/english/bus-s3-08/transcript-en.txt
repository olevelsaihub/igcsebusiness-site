Idea. Imagine your friends owe you twenty dollars, but your pocket is empty at lunchtime. On paper you have money, but you cannot buy lunch today. Businesses face the same problem. Cash flow is the movement of money into the business, for example from customers, and out of it, for example to suppliers. Cash and profit are different. A business can make a profit but still run out of cash if its customers pay late.

Method. Step one: name a payment the business must make in cash: its suppliers, its overheads such as rent and electricity, or its employees' wages. Use the business in the case. Step two: trace what happens if it is not paid. Suppliers stop deliveries, there is nothing to sell, and the business may become insolvent: unable to pay its debts when they are due. To improve cash flow, ask one question. Does this bring cash in sooner, or send it out later? Asking customers to pay sooner helps. Letting them pay later makes it worse.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2025, Paper two. Explain one reason why it is important that businesses pay their suppliers on time. It is worth three marks. Mark one: the reason. Paying on time builds a trusting relationship with suppliers. Mark two: because the supplier knows it will be paid, it is more willing to help, for example with urgent deliveries or buying on credit. Mark three: so the business keeps its stock available and does not lose sales to competitors.

Common mistake. A common mistake is confusing cash with profit. Paying a supplier later keeps cash longer, but it does not change profit. Another is choosing to let customers pay later. That slows cash coming in, so cash flow gets worse.

Your turn. Now you try. A builder has finished a big job, but the customer will not pay for ninety days. The builder must pay wages every Friday. Explain one reason the builder needs cash. Pause and decide.

Answer. The builder needs cash to pay wages every Friday. If it cannot, workers may refuse to work or leave, so jobs are delayed and customers go elsewhere, even though the job made a profit. That is the pattern for this type: name a payment, use the case, then show what happens if it is missed. Now try the practice questions.
