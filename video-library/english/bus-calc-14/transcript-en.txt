Imagine a market stall that sells phone cases. In one week, customers pay it one hundred dollars. The cases it sold cost the stall sixty dollars to buy. Gross profit is revenue minus cost of sales. One hundred minus sixty is forty dollars. The gross profit margin shows gross profit as a percentage of revenue. Forty divided by one hundred, times one hundred, is forty per cent. The stall keeps forty cents of gross profit from every dollar of sales.

Step one: find gross profit. If it is not given, work it out as revenue minus cost of sales. Step two: find revenue for the same business, in the same column. Step three: divide gross profit by revenue, then multiply by one hundred. Gross profit goes on top. Revenue goes underneath. Step four: round as the question asks, usually to two decimal places, and add the per cent sign. Look at the third decimal place to decide whether to round up.

Here is a real exam question from June 2022, Paper one R. Calculate, to two decimal places, the gross profit margin. It is worth two marks. Figure two shows sales revenue of nineteen thousand two hundred and twenty euros, cost of sales of fourteen thousand seven hundred and twenty, and gross profit of four thousand five hundred. Four thousand five hundred divided by nineteen thousand two hundred and twenty, times one hundred, is twenty-three point four one three. To two decimal places, twenty-three point four one per cent.

A common mistake is dividing by cost of sales instead of revenue. Margin is always measured against revenue. Another is turning the formula upside down. A margin cannot be over one hundred per cent, so an answer like four hundred and twenty-seven per cent is a warning sign.

Now you try. A bookshop has sales revenue of forty-five thousand dollars and cost of sales of twenty-eight thousand six hundred dollars. Calculate its gross profit margin to two decimal places. Pause here and work it out.

First, gross profit. Forty-five thousand minus twenty-eight thousand six hundred is sixteen thousand four hundred dollars. Then sixteen thousand four hundred divided by forty-five thousand, times one hundred, is thirty-six point four four four. To two decimal places, thirty-six point four four per cent. Remember the method: find gross profit, divide by revenue and multiply by one hundred, then round and add the per cent sign. Now try the practice questions.
