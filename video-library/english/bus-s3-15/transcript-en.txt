Imagine you run a lemonade stall. You pay to rent the table, and you buy lemons and cups. These are costs: money a business spends to make and sell its products. Output is how many products a business makes. Now ask: if you make more glasses of lemonade, which costs go up? The table rent stays the same. The lemons and cups go up.

Fixed costs do not change when output changes. Rent, insurance and managers' salaries are paid whether the business makes ten items or ten thousand. On a graph, fixed costs are a flat line. Variable costs change directly with output. Make more, spend more. Raw materials and packaging are variable. Add them to fixed costs, and total cost rises as output rises. To classify any cost, use one test. Would this bill rise if the business made one more unit? If yes, it is variable. If no, it is fixed. In a State question, pick a cost that fits the business in the case. For a flower shop, the flowers are a variable cost and the shop rent is a fixed cost.

Here is a real question from Paper one R in June 2023, about a flower shop in Paris. Define the term variable costs. It is worth one mark, so one clear sentence is enough. Build the definition from three key words. Costs. That change. When output changes. So: variable costs are costs whose total changes when output changes. For example, the flower shop spends more on flowers when it makes more arrangements. The example supports the meaning but does not replace it.

A common mistake is saying fixed costs never change. Rent can rise next year. A fixed cost simply does not change when output changes. Another mistake is calling packaging or raw materials fixed. Every extra unit needs more of them, so they are variable.

Now you try. A pizza takeaway pays four costs: rent for the shop, cheese, pizza boxes, and the manager's salary. Which are fixed and which are variable? Pause and decide.

One more pizza needs more cheese and one more box, so those are variable. The rent and the manager's salary stay the same, so they are fixed. Remember the test: make one more unit. If the cost rises, it is variable. If it stays the same, it is fixed. Now try the practice questions.
