If you borrow ten dollars from a friend and they ask for eleven back, the extra dollar is interest. One dollar out of ten is ten per cent. An interest rate is the percentage charged for borrowing money, or paid as a reward for saving it. When rates change, businesses and their customers are both affected.

Step one: fix the direction. Are interest rates rising or falling? Every effect depends on this. Step two: split the effect into two channels. One is the business's own borrowing costs. The other is how much its customers spend. Step three: chain each channel to an outcome. When rates rise, repayments rise, so costs rise. Customers with loans have less to spend, and saving pays more, so demand falls. Either way, profit is likely to fall. Step four: use the case. Businesses selling expensive or non-essential items, such as cars, holidays or furniture, are usually hit hardest by higher rates.

Here is a real question from Paper two R in June 2024. Explain one impact on a business of an increase in interest rates. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the impact. An increase in interest rates makes borrowing more expensive for the business. Mark two explains why. If it has a loan or overdraft with a variable rate, it pays more interest each month, so its costs rise. Mark three gives the consequence. Higher costs reduce profit, and the business may have to delay plans to expand, such as opening new shops.

Mistake one: getting the direction wrong. Higher interest rates do not increase sales. Customers have less to spend, so sales usually fall. Mistake two: stopping at costs go up. Always finish the chain: what happens to profit or to the business's plans?

Now you try. A furniture shop sells sofas, and many customers pay for them with a loan. Interest rates fall. Explain one impact on the shop. Pause and decide.

Rates are falling, so borrowing is cheaper for customers. Monthly repayments on a sofa loan fall, so more people can afford one. The shop sells more sofas, so its revenue rises. Remember the method: fix the direction, choose costs or customers, then link it to profit or plans. Now try the practice questions for this type.
