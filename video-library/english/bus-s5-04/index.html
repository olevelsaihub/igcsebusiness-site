Read the narration

Think about a hairdresser. Almost all the work is done by a skilled person, with just scissors and a hairdryer. Now think about a car factory, where robots weld and paint the cars. In production, capital means machines, equipment and technology. Labour means the people who do the work. Careful: here, capital does not mean money. Capital-intensive production uses more machinery than labour, like a car plant full of robots. Labour-intensive production uses more workers than machinery, like a hairdresser, a chef or a fruit picker.

Step one. Ask a single question: who or what does most of the work here, machines or people? Machines means capital intensive. People means labour intensive. Step two. For a Define question, write one sentence that compares the two inputs. Say which one is used more than the other. Step three. In multiple choice, rule out the distractors. Kaizen and just-in-time are lean production methods. They are not about intensity. Also remember: job production is usually labour intensive, and flow production is usually capital intensive.

Here is a real exam question from Paper two in November 2023. Define the term labour intensive production. It is worth one mark. First, find the input it relies on. Labour means workers, so labour-intensive production relies mainly on people. Then add the comparison. Labour-intensive production uses more workers than machinery and equipment. That one sentence earns the mark. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

The most common mistake is thinking capital intensive means needing a lot of money. Here, capital means machinery. Another mistake is swapping the terms. A hairdresser is labour intensive, not capital intensive, because a person does the skilled work.

Now you try. A bakery uses huge automatic ovens and a packing machine, with just three workers watching the line. Is it capital intensive or labour intensive? Then write a one-sentence definition of that term. Pause and decide.

Machines do most of the work, and only three people watch the line. So the bakery is capital intensive. The definition: capital-intensive production uses more machinery than labour. Remember the method: ask who does most of the work, name the term, then compare the two inputs in one sentence. Now try the practice questions.
