Idea. Think about ordering at a busy café. You ask for a hot chocolate, but the noise means the server hears hot coffee. The message went wrong, so you get the wrong drink. Communication is passing a message from a sender to a receiver, who must understand it and may reply. That reply is called feedback. Internal communication happens inside the business, such as a manager talking to staff at a team meeting. External communication is with people outside, such as customers, suppliers or the bank.

Methods include face-to-face meetings, letters, email, noticeboards, and phone or video calls. They differ in cost, speed, privacy, and whether they leave a written record. A barrier is anything that stops a message being received or understood: jargon, a different language, noise, a long chain of command, poor technology, or a sender who is not trusted. To explain, decide who is communicating, name one impact or barrier, explain what happens to the work, then carry it on to a business result such as costs, productivity or customer satisfaction.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one in June twenty twenty-four. Explain one impact on a business of ineffective communication. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the impact. Employees are likely to make mistakes in their work. Mark two explains why. If a manager's instructions are unclear or arrive late, workers misunderstand what to do or how to do it. Mark three is the consequence. Customers get the wrong service, so they complain and may not return, and the business wastes time and money putting things right. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is listing three problems with no explanation. That earns only one mark. Develop one impact instead, and do not stop at staff being confused. Also check internal and external. A staff meeting stays inside the business, so it is internal communication.

Your turn. Now you try. A hotel manager gives cleaning instructions full of technical words, but most cleaners speak a different first language. Name the barriers, then explain one effect on the hotel. Pause and decide.

Answer. The barriers are jargon and a language difference. The cleaners misunderstand, so rooms are not cleaned properly. Guests complain and may not return, so the hotel loses sales. Simple language and asking for feedback would help. Remember the chain: name one impact, explain what happens to the work, then carry it on to a result for the business.
