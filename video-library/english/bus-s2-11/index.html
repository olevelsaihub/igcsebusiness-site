Selection: shortlisting and interviewing candidates

Idea. Imagine twenty students want to join the school football team, but there are only five places. The coach picks eight who fit best, then watches them play in a trial before choosing. In business this is called selection: choosing the best person from the people who applied. It has two stages: shortlisting, then interviewing.

Method. Shortlisting means picking out the applicants who best match the job description and person specification. Only they are invited to interview. Here, a café needs an experienced barista who can work weekends. An interview is a meeting where the business asks questions to judge skills, knowledge, attitude and fit. It also checks that what the applicant wrote is true. Why does it matter? A poor hire wastes money on wages and training, and the business has to recruit all over again. To answer, say what the stage does, link it to the job in the case, show the benefit, then end with the effect on the business.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper two in November twenty twenty. Analyse why Emirates considers interviewing pilots as an important part of the recruitment process. It is worth six marks, so write two chains, both about Emirates and its pilots. First, gather the evidence. Emirates wants pilots with the best skills. Trainees pay seven hundred and thirty thousand UAE dirhams for their own training. Its rating fell from first to fourth, and low-cost airlines are competing. Chain one. Interviews test pilots' skills and check what they claimed. So only the most capable pilots are hired, which keeps flights safe and on time and protects Emirates' reputation. Chain two. Interviews judge attitude and how well a pilot works with cabin crew. This avoids the cost and delay of recruiting again, and helps Emirates win back its rating against low-cost rivals.

Common mistake. Mistake one: describing how to hold an interview. The question asks why it helps the business. Mistake two: stopping at the best person is chosen. Show what that means for this business.

Your turn. Now you try. A hospital receives one hundred and twenty applications for four nurse jobs. Explain one benefit of shortlisting for the hospital. Pause and decide.

Answer. Shortlisting removes applicants without nursing qualifications, so managers interview only a few suitable people. This saves time, so senior nurses get back to caring for patients sooner. That is the pattern for this type: say what the stage does, link it to the job, then show the effect on the business. Now try the practice questions.
