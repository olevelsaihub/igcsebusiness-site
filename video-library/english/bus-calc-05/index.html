Work out profit or loss

Idea. Imagine you sell homemade cookies. You take in sixty dollars, and the ingredients and boxes cost forty dollars. Sixty minus forty leaves twenty dollars. That is your profit. There are three profits to know. Profit takes away all costs. Gross profit takes away only the cost of sales, the direct cost of the goods sold. Operating profit is gross profit minus operating expenses, such as rent.

Method. Step one, decide which profit is asked for. Step two, add up all the relevant costs. Leaving out one cost line is a common trap. Step three, subtract: revenue minus total costs. Step four, read the sign. Positive is a profit. Negative is a loss. Write which it is, with the currency.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper two R in June 2019. A T-shirt costs eleven point zero five taka to make and sells for thirteen point seven five taka. Calculate the gross profit if fourteen thousand are sold. The currency is the Bangladeshi taka. Revenue is thirteen point seven five times fourteen thousand, which is one hundred and ninety-two thousand five hundred. Cost of sales is eleven point zero five times fourteen thousand, which is one hundred and fifty-four thousand seven hundred. Subtract. One hundred and ninety-two thousand five hundred minus one hundred and fifty-four thousand seven hundred is thirty-seven thousand eight hundred taka. It is positive, so it is a profit. Check by the quick route. Each T-shirt makes thirteen point seven five minus eleven point zero five, which is two taka seventy. Two point seven times fourteen thousand is also thirty-seven thousand eight hundred.

Common mistake. A common mistake is giving the total costs as the answer. One hundred and fifty-four thousand seven hundred is the cost of sales, not the profit. Finish by subtracting. Another mistake is subtracting the wrong way round, or leaving out a cost line. Then a loss looks like a profit. Add every cost, and always do revenue minus costs.

Your turn. Now you try. A market stall sells four hundred phone cases at six dollars each. Its costs are fifteen hundred dollars for stock, five hundred for rent and six hundred for wages. Calculate its profit or loss. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. First, revenue. Four hundred cases at six dollars each is two thousand four hundred dollars. Next, add every cost line. Fifteen hundred plus five hundred plus six hundred is two thousand six hundred dollars. Now subtract. Two thousand four hundred minus two thousand six hundred is minus two hundred. The sign is negative, so the stall made a loss of two hundred dollars. Remember: revenue, all costs, subtract, read the sign. Now try the practice questions.
