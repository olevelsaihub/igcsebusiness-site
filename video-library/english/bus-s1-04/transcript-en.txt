Idea. Imagine you start selling phone cases on your own. You own the business, you take every decision, and you keep all the profit. You are a sole trader. But if the business owes money it cannot pay, you may have to pay its debts from your own money, even by selling your own things. This is unlimited liability. With limited liability, owners can only lose the money they invested in the business. Their personal possessions are safe. Companies have limited liability.

Method. Here are the forms of ownership. A sole trader has one owner with full control. A partnership has two or more partners who share decisions, skills, capital and profit. A private limited company, or L T D, is owned by shareholders, but its shares are not sold to the public. A public limited company, or P L C, sells shares to the public on the stock exchange, so it can raise large amounts of capital. A public corporation is different. It is owned and run by the government, usually to provide a service rather than to make the most profit. To answer, identify the form in the case, recall one key feature, such as control, liability or finance, then turn it into a benefit or drawback and add its consequence.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one in June 2022. Explain one advantage of being a sole trader. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the advantage. The owner has complete control of the business. Mark two explains why. There is only one owner, so there are no partners or shareholders to agree with. Mark three gives the consequence. Decisions are quick, so the owner can respond fast, for example by stocking a new product customers ask for.

Common mistake. Mistake one: saying a sole trader has limited liability. Sole traders, and usually partners, have unlimited liability. Mistake two: mixing up a public corporation with a public limited company. One is owned by the government; the other by shareholders.

Your turn. Now you try. Ali and Sara run a bike repair shop as a partnership. They plan to become a private limited company. Explain one advantage to them of limited liability. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. With limited liability, Ali and Sara can only lose the money they invested. If the shop fails, their personal possessions, such as their homes, are safe. So they may feel confident enough to expand, for example by opening a second shop. Remember the method: identify the form, recall one feature, then explain its effect on the owner.
