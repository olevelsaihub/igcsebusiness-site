Place: distribution channels and e-commerce

Read the narration

Think about how a bar of chocolate reaches you. It is made in a factory, taken to a shop, and bought by you. That route is called a distribution channel. Place is the part of the marketing mix about how and where customers can buy a product. A business can sell directly, or through others. A retailer is a shop that sells to the final customer. Using other retailers lets a producer reach more customers. E-commerce means buying and selling using the internet, websites or apps. A business that sells online is called an e-tailer.

Step one: name the channel the case describes. Is it the business's own shop, other retailers, a website or an app? Step two: pick one advantage or concern. E-commerce reaches customers anywhere at any time, and fewer shops can mean lower costs. But it costs money to set up and maintain, some customers will not download an app, and it needs technical skills. Step three: link the point to customers or costs: who can now buy, or which costs rise or fall. Then end with the result: revenue, profit or market share.

Here is a real question from Paper one in November 2025. Explain one advantage to a business of using e-commerce to sell its products. It is worth three marks, and no definition is needed. Mark one is the advantage. Selling through e-commerce can reduce the business's costs. Mark two explains why. Customers order and pay on a website or app, so the business does not need to rent and staff as many shops or offices. Mark three gives the result. With lower running costs, more of the money from each sale is kept as profit.

Mistake one: writing about online advertising. E-commerce means selling online, not promoting online. Mistake two: treating place as only where the shop is. Place also includes online channels.

Now you try. A family bakery sells only from its one shop in a town. It is thinking of selling through a mobile app with home delivery. Give one advantage and one concern for the bakery. Pause and decide.

An advantage: customers across the town can order at any time, even if they never pass the shop. More orders mean more revenue. A concern: the app costs money to build and maintain, and some customers may not download it. At first, the costs could be bigger than the extra sales. Remember the method: name the channel, link it to customers or costs, then end with revenue, profit or market share. Now try the practice questions.
