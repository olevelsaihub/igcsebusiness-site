Promotion methods: above and below the line

Read the narration

Think of a chocolate bar. You might see an advert for it on television, along with millions of other people. Or someone might hand you a free sample in a supermarket. Both are promotion. Promotion means communicating with customers to make them aware of a product and persuade them to buy it.

Step one: classify the method. Above the line uses mass media to reach a large audience: television, radio, newspapers, posters and billboards. Below the line is more direct and targeted: special offers, coupons, free samples, sponsorship, leaflets and loyalty schemes. Two methods need a closer look. Sponsorship means paying towards an event so that the business's name is shown. A product trial lets customers try before they buy, and gives the business feedback. Step two: match the method to the customers and the budget. A national brand can afford television to reach millions. A local café is better with cheaper leaflets for people nearby. Step three: link the method to awareness or persuasion, then to sales. Add a limitation if needed: it costs money, the effect may be short, and there is no guarantee people will buy.

Here is a real question from Paper two in November 2021. Explain one advantage for a business of using sponsorship. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the advantage: sponsorship increases brand awareness. Mark two explains how. The business's name and logo are shown to everyone at the event or watching it, which could be a local, national or global audience. Mark three is the result. People who follow the event become familiar with the brand and may choose to buy from it, which increases sales revenue.

Mistake one: putting special offers or sponsorship above the line. They are direct and targeted, so they are below the line. Mistake two: jumping straight to more sales. Show how customers are reached first.

Now you try. A new bike repair shop has a small budget and wants local customers. Should it use a national television advert or leaflets delivered to nearby homes? Classify each method, then choose one. Pause and decide.

Television is above the line; leaflets are below the line. Choose the leaflets. They are cheaper and reach people who live close enough to bring their bikes in, so more local customers visit. A drawback: some leaflets will be thrown away unread. That is the method for this type: classify the method, match it to the customers and budget, then link it to awareness and sales.
