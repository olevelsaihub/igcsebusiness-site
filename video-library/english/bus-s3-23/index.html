Statement of financial position: assets and liabilities

Idea. Think about what you own and what you owe. You might own a phone and have some savings. You might also owe your sister ten dollars. Writing both lists down on one day gives a snapshot of your money. A business does the same in its statement of financial position. It shows what the business owns, its assets, and what it owes, its liabilities, on a particular date.

Method. For any item, ask two questions. First, does the business own it, or owe it? Second, will it be used up or paid within twelve months? If yes, it is current. If it lasts longer, it is non-current. Non-current assets are kept for more than a year, such as buildings, machinery and vehicles. Current assets are cash, or will become cash within a year, such as inventory and money owed by customers. Inventory is the stock of goods or materials the business holds. Current liabilities must be paid within twelve months, such as an overdraft or money owed to suppliers. Non-current liabilities are due after twelve months, such as a long-term bank loan. The statement also shows capital employed: the long-term money invested in the business, by its owners and through long-term loans. But some things are not on it at all. Sales, cost of sales and gross profit belong in the income statement.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper two, November twenty twenty-three. Explain one purpose for a business of the statement of financial position. It is worth three marks: one purpose, then two linked steps. Mark one is the purpose. It shows whether the business will be able to pay the debts it owes in the near future. Mark two explains how. Managers compare current assets, such as cash and inventory, with current liabilities, such as money owed to suppliers. Mark three gives the result. If the liabilities are larger, managers can act early, for example by holding less inventory or arranging extra finance, before suppliers stop giving credit.

Common mistake. Mistake one: calling a building, vehicle or factory a current asset. The business keeps these for years, so they are non-current. Mistake two: in an Explain question, just defining the statement. A definition earns nothing. Give a purpose, then link it to a decision and its result.

Your turn. Now you try. A taxi firm has a five-year bank loan. State one non-current asset the taxi firm owns. Then decide: is the five-year loan a current or a non-current liability? Pause and decide.

Answer. A good non-current asset is its taxis, because the firm owns them and keeps them for several years. The five-year loan is owed and is due after twelve months, so it is a non-current liability. Remember the method: ask whether it is owned or owed, then use the twelve-month rule.
