Idea. Imagine a small café near your school. For years it is the only place to buy a snack. Then a new bubble tea shop opens next door, and students start going there instead. The café's market has changed. Markets change in three main ways. Customer needs and tastes change. Spending patterns change: how much customers spend, and what they spend it on. And new competitors arrive, giving customers more choice. Spending patterns rise and fall with income and trends. If incomes fall, customers may spend less on extras such as eating out, and more on essentials. A business that sells extras will feel this first.

Method. Start by naming the actual change in the case. Then choose a response. Then link that response to keeping or winning customers, and so to sales or market share. Common responses are new or improved products, a change in price, more promotion, longer opening hours, or new services. For higher marks, consider the other side. Responding costs money and may not work. And a business with a strong, unique image may need only small changes.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one R, June twenty twenty-two, about Can Culleretes, a restaurant in Barcelona. Analyse the importance of Can Culleretes responding to changes in its market. It is worth six marks, so we need two developed points using the case. Point one. New Italian and Turkish restaurants have opened in Barcelona, and there are many restaurants nearby. Diners have more choice. If Can Culleretes keeps the same menu and hours, customers may go elsewhere. Adding new dishes or opening at new times can protect its sales. Point two gives balance. It is the second oldest restaurant in Spain, run by the same family since nineteen fifty-eight. Its history and traditional food attract tourists. If it changed too much, it could lose that image, so it should make careful changes.

Common mistake. Mistake one: describing the change but never saying how the business should respond. The marks come from the response and its effect on sales. Mistake two: assuming every change must be followed. Think about the cost, and the business's own strengths, before deciding.

Your turn. Now you try. A small bookshop finds that an online seller now offers the same books at lower prices, with home delivery. State one way the bookshop could respond, and explain how it helps. Pause and decide.

Answer. One answer: hold author visits and reading clubs. This gives customers a reason to visit that the online seller cannot offer, so they keep coming in and buying books, which protects the shop's sales. That is the pattern for this type: name the change, choose a response, link it to customers and sales, and weigh the cost. Now try the practice questions.
