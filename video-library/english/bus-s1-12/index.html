Choosing where to locate a business

Read the narration

Why are ice-cream kiosks found at busy beaches and not in quiet back streets? Because that is where the customers are. Where a business places its shop, factory or office is called its location. Businesses weigh up many factors: closeness to the market, to labour and to materials, where competitors are, the cost of land, the internet, and the law. Being near the market means being near customers, which raises sales and lowers delivery costs. Being near labour means enough workers with the right skills, at an affordable wage. Being near materials lowers transport costs and avoids delays. Some firms stay away from competitors, while others cluster near them, where shoppers already go.

Step one: decide what kind of business it is. A café or gym that serves customers in person needs to be near its market. A factory may need to be near materials or skilled labour. Step two: pick the factor that matters most, using evidence from the case. Step three: link it to costs, sales or staff, and then to profit.

Here is a real question from Paper one in November 2024, about Saha, a business in Türkiye that makes robots. Explain one reason why proximity, or closeness, to materials is important when locating a new factory. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the reason. Being close to materials lowers the business's transport costs. Mark two explains why. The parts and materials travel only a short distance from the supplier to the factory, so deliveries are cheaper. Mark three gives the consequence. Each of Saha's robots costs less to make, so it can earn more profit on each one, or charge lower prices than rivals.

Mistake one: defining location or proximity instead of giving a reason. Mistake two: listing several factors without explaining how any one of them affects costs or sales.

Now you try. A new gym must choose between a busy town centre with high rent, and a cheap industrial estate far from where people live. Explain one reason to choose the town centre. Pause and decide.

The town centre is close to the gym's market. Many people live and work nearby, so it is easy to drop in. More people join, so revenue rises and can cover the higher rent. The cheaper rent is a real advantage of the estate, but for a gym, being near customers usually matters more. Remember the method: decide what kind of business it is, pick the key factor, then link it to costs or sales and to profit. Now try the practice questions for this type.
