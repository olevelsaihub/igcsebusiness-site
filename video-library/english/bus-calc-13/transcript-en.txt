Idea. A sports shop buys a football for ten dollars and sells it for fifteen dollars. The five dollars it adds on top of the cost is its profit on each ball. Markup shows that profit as a percentage of the cost. Five divided by ten, times one hundred, is fifty per cent.

Method. Step one: find the cost and the selling price. Step two: profit per unit is the price minus the cost. Step three: divide by the cost, and multiply by one hundred. Some questions run backwards. To find the price, multiply the cost by one plus the markup. To find the cost, divide the price by one plus the markup. Step five: round as asked, usually to two decimal places, and add the per cent sign. Markup can be over one hundred per cent. A four dollar item sold for ten has a markup of one hundred and fifty per cent.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question from November 2020, Paper one. A drum kit costs one hundred and ninety pounds and sells for three hundred and fifty pounds. Calculate, to two decimal places, the markup. It is worth two marks. First the profit on each drum kit. Three hundred and fifty minus one hundred and ninety is one hundred and sixty pounds. Now divide by the cost. One hundred and sixty divided by one hundred and ninety, times one hundred, is eighty-four point two one zero. To two decimal places, eighty-four point two one per cent.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is dividing by the selling price instead of the cost. When working backwards, do not take the percentage off the price. Divide by one plus the markup instead.

Your turn. Now you try. A bakery sells a birthday cake for thirty-six dollars. Its markup is twenty per cent. Calculate the cost of making the cake. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Cost equals price divided by one plus the markup. Thirty-six divided by one point two is thirty dollars. Check it. Thirty times one point two is thirty-six. Taking twenty per cent off thirty-six would give twenty-eight dollars eighty, which is wrong. Remember the method: profit is price minus cost, divide by the cost and multiply by one hundred. To work backwards, multiply or divide by one plus the markup. Now try the practice questions.
