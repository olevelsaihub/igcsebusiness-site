Idea. Imagine you owe a friend twenty dollars today. You have fifteen dollars in cash, plus a box of old comics worth fifteen dollars. On paper you have thirty dollars. But the comics are not cash until someone buys them. For a business, the box of comics is its inventory: the goods it holds to sell. Inventory may take time to sell, so it is not counted as quick cash. The acid test ratio is a stricter test than the current ratio. It asks: can the business pay its short-term debts without selling any inventory?

Method. The formula is current assets minus inventory, all divided by current liabilities. The brackets matter. Subtract first, then divide. Step one, take inventory away from current assets. Step two, divide the answer by current liabilities. Step three, round as asked and write it against one. An answer below one means the business has less than one unit of quick assets for every unit it owes in the short term.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one, November twenty twenty-four, about Saha, a business that makes robots. Its figure gives current assets of twelve million lira, inventory of four million, and current liabilities of seven million. Calculate the acid test ratio to two decimal places. It is worth two marks. First, take inventory away from current assets. Twelve minus four is eight. Now divide by current liabilities. Eight divided by seven is one point one four two and more. To two decimal places, one point one four, or one point one four to one.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is forgetting to take inventory away. Twelve divided by seven gives one point seven one, which is the current ratio, not the acid test. Another mistake is dividing before subtracting. If you divide four by seven first and take that from twelve, you get eleven point four three, which makes no sense. Do the bracket first.

Your turn. Now you try. A shoe shop has current assets of forty-five thousand dollars, including inventory of twenty thousand dollars. Its current liabilities are thirty thousand dollars. Calculate its acid test ratio to two decimal places. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Forty-five thousand minus twenty thousand is twenty-five thousand. Twenty-five thousand divided by thirty thousand is zero point eight three three and more. So the answer is zero point eight three to one. That is below one, so without selling shoes, the shop could not cover its short-term debts. Remember the method: take inventory away from current assets, divide by current liabilities, then round and write it against one. Now try the practice questions for this type.
