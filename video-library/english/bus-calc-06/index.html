Break-even output using contribution

Read the narration

Imagine you run a lemonade stall at a school fair. Renting the table costs twenty dollars for the day, whether you sell one cup or a hundred. That is a fixed cost. Each cup sells for one dollar, but the lemons and the cup cost fifty cents. So each cup leaves fifty cents to help pay the rent. This is called contribution per unit. How many cups pay off the twenty dollars? Twenty divided by fifty cents is forty cups. At forty cups, total revenue equals total costs: no profit and no loss. That is break-even.

Step one: find three figures in the question. The fixed costs, the selling price, and the variable cost per unit. Step two: work out contribution per unit, the price minus the variable cost. Step three: divide the fixed costs by that contribution. Step four: if the answer is not a whole number, always round up. You cannot sell part of a unit, and rounding down would leave a small loss.

Here is a real exam question from June 2024, Paper one. A business plans a new line of dog treats, and Figure four gives the figures. Monthly fixed costs are one thousand euros, each treat costs three euros twenty-five to make, and sells for four euros fifty. Calculate the break-even output. It is worth two marks. First the contribution. Four fifty minus three twenty-five is one euro twenty-five per treat. Now divide. One thousand divided by one point two five is eight hundred. It is a whole number, so no rounding. The answer is eight hundred dog treats. Check it. Eight hundred treats bring in three thousand six hundred euros. Total costs are two thousand six hundred plus one thousand, also three thousand six hundred. Revenue equals costs.

A common mistake is dividing the fixed costs by the selling price, or by the variable cost. Always divide by the contribution. Another mistake is giving the answer in money. Break-even output is a number of units.

Now you try. A car wash has fixed costs of one thousand three hundred dollars a month. Each wash sells for nine dollars, and the soap and water cost three dollars fifty. Calculate the break-even number of washes. Pause here and work it out.

Contribution is nine minus three fifty, which is five dollars fifty. One thousand three hundred divided by five fifty is two hundred and thirty-six point three six. Round up: two hundred and thirty-seven washes. Remember the method: find the three figures, work out the contribution, divide the fixed costs by it, then round up and write the units.
