Imagine passing a message from the head of a big company down to a shop-floor worker. In some businesses it passes through many managers. In others it goes almost straight there. An organisational chart shows which kind a business has: who is responsible to whom. A hierarchical, or tall, structure has many levels, a long chain of command, and narrow spans of control. Each manager looks after only a few people. A flat structure has few levels, a short chain of command, and wide spans of control. Communication is faster and there are fewer managers to pay, but each manager may be overloaded. There is a second question: where are decisions made? Centralised means senior managers at head office make the important decisions. Decentralised means decisions are passed down to managers in branches or regions.

Step one. Identify the structure: tall or flat, centralised or decentralised. Step two. Recall its key feature: the number of layers, or where decisions are made. Step three. Link the feature to communication speed, control, cost or motivation. Step four. End with the effect on the business, such as faster decisions or meeting local customers' needs.

Here is a real question from Paper one in June 2022. Explain one benefit to a business of having a flat organisational structure. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the benefit. There are only a few layers of management between the owner and the workers. Mark two explains it. Messages pass through fewer people, so communication is quicker and less likely to be misunderstood. Mark three is the consequence. Decisions can be made and acted on faster, so the business responds quickly to customers' needs.

Mistake one: mixing up the features, such as saying a flat structure has a long chain of command. Flat means few levels, so the chain is short. Mistake two: confusing decentralised with flat. Flat is about the number of layers. Decentralised is about passing decisions down to branches.

Now you try. StyleHub has forty clothes shops. Each shop manager chooses which products to stock for local customers. Is StyleHub centralised or decentralised? Explain one benefit. Pause and decide.

StyleHub is decentralised, because shop managers make the decisions. They know their local customers, so they stock what those customers want. This could mean more sales for each shop. Remember the method: identify the structure, recall its key feature, link it to speed, control, cost or motivation, then end with the effect on the business. Now try the practice questions.
