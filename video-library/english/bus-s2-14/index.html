Equal opportunities and minimum wage laws

Read the narration

Imagine two friends apply for the same weekend job. They have the same skills. One is turned down only because of their religion. That is unfair, and it has a name: discrimination. Equal opportunities laws protect people from discrimination because of gender, race, disability, religion, sexual orientation and age. These are called protected characteristics. A second law is the minimum wage. It is the lowest pay per hour that a business is legally allowed to pay a worker. For example, at eight dollars an hour, twenty hours of work must earn at least one hundred and sixty dollars.

Be careful. Skills, ability and experience are not protected. A business is allowed to choose the most able person for the job. It just cannot reject someone because of a protected characteristic. What must a business actually do? Treat people fairly in recruitment, training and promotion. Give equal pay for the same work. And make reasonable changes for disabled staff, such as ramps or flexible hours. To answer a question, use one chain. Name the law or group. Say what the business must do. Explain why. Then end with the effect on the business: costs, reputation, or better staff.

Here is a real question from Paper two R in June twenty twenty-four. Explain one reason why businesses comply with equal opportunities legislation. It is worth three marks: one reason and two linked steps. Mark one is the reason. Equal opportunities is the law, and breaking it can lead to legal action. Mark two explains it. If a business treats someone unfairly because of their age, gender or race, that person can take the business to a tribunal or court. Mark three is the consequence. The business may pay compensation and legal costs, and bad publicity can damage its reputation with customers and future staff.

Mistake one: choosing ability or skills as a protected characteristic. Businesses are allowed to choose staff on ability. Mistake two: writing a definition when the question asks for a reason. A definition earns no marks in an Explain question.

Now you try. A café pays its kitchen staff less than the minimum wage to save money. Explain one problem this could cause for the café. Pause and decide.

The café is breaking the minimum wage law. Staff can report it, so the café may be fined and have to pay back the missing wages. Its costs rise, and bad publicity makes it harder to attract customers and staff. That is the pattern for this type: name the law, say what the business must do, then link it to the effect on the business. Now try the practice questions.
