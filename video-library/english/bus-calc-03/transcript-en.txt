Idea. Imagine you make friendship bracelets. You rent a market table for twenty dollars, whatever you make. Each bracelet uses one dollar of thread. If you make thirty bracelets, the thread costs thirty times one dollar, which is thirty dollars. Add the twenty dollars rent, and your total cost is fifty dollars. On a graph, the fixed cost is a flat line at twenty dollars. Total cost starts at twenty and climbs one dollar for every bracelet, reaching fifty dollars at thirty bracelets.

Method. Step one, pick out three figures: the fixed cost, the variable cost per unit, and the number of units. Tables often include a selling price too. The selling price is not a cost, so leave it out. Step two, multiply the variable cost per unit by the number of units. Step three, add the fixed cost. Step four, write the currency, and check the time period matches.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one in June 2022, about a toy shop in Singapore. Calculate the total cost of making one hundred and sixty wooden toy carts. It is worth two marks. From the table, fixed costs are five hundred and fifty-five Singapore dollars thirty-one. The variable cost is ninety-three cents per cart. Output is one hundred and sixty carts. Total variable cost is zero point nine three times one hundred and sixty, which is one hundred and forty-eight dollars eighty. Add the fixed costs: five hundred and fifty-five dollars thirty-one plus one hundred and forty-eight dollars eighty is seven hundred and four Singapore dollars eleven.

Common mistake. A common mistake is adding the fixed cost to the cost of only one cart. That gives five hundred and fifty-six Singapore dollars twenty-four. You must multiply by all one hundred and sixty carts first. Another mistake is forgetting the fixed cost completely, or using the selling price instead of the variable cost. Total cost needs both kinds of cost, and no price.

Your turn. Now you try. A bakery has fixed costs of three hundred dollars a week. Each loaf costs eighty cents to make and sells for two dollars fifty. It makes four hundred and fifty loaves a week. Calculate its total weekly cost. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Leave out the selling price. Variable cost is zero point eight zero times four hundred and fifty, which is three hundred and sixty dollars. Now add the fixed cost. Three hundred plus three hundred and sixty is six hundred and sixty dollars a week. Remember the method: pick out the three figures, multiply the cost per unit by the units, then add the fixed cost.
