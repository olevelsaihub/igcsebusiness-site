Idea. Think about a shoe shop. A five-year-old, a teenage footballer and a grandparent all need shoes, but each of them wants something very different. Dividing a market into groups of customers who share similar characteristics is called market segmentation. Each group is a market segment. Choosing which segment to aim your products and promotion at is called targeting. Then the business fits its product, price and promotion to that group.

Method. Step one: choose a basis for splitting customers. Common bases are age, income, location, lifestyle and demographics. Demographics means features of the population, such as age, gender and family type. Step two: describe what one segment needs, using facts from the case. Step three: link targeting to a product, price or promotion that fits that need. Step four: end with the result for the business. More sales, more loyal customers, or less money wasted promoting to people who will never buy.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one in November 2023. Explain one benefit to a business of using market segmentation to target its customers. It is worth three marks: one benefit, then two linked steps. Mark one is the benefit. Segmentation lets the business find groups of customers who share similar characteristics, such as the same age or income. Mark two explains why that helps. The business can design a product, set a price and choose promotion that match exactly what that group wants. Mark three gives the result. Because the product suits them better, those customers are more likely to buy and to come back, so sales and loyalty rise.

Common mistake. Mistake one: confusing segmentation with market research. Research collects information. Segmentation divides customers into groups. Mistake two: naming a basis but not using it. Say how this business would use the basis, and what happens as a result.

Your turn. Now you try. A sports drink company sells one drink to everyone. Identify one basis it could use to segment its market, and explain one benefit of targeting one segment. Pause and decide.

Answer. One good answer uses age. Target teenagers who play sport. Sell smaller bottles at a lower price and promote them on social media. The drink suits them better, so more teenagers buy it and sales rise. That is the pattern for every answer on this type: group the customers, fit the product, price and promotion to them, then show the result. Now try the practice questions.
