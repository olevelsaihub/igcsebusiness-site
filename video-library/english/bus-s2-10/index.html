Internal and external recruitment

Idea. Your football team needs a new captain. You could choose one of your own players, who already knows everyone. Or you could bring in a player from another team, who might bring fresh ideas. In business, filling a job with someone who already works there is internal recruitment, often a promotion. Finding someone from outside, through adverts, agencies or job websites, is external recruitment.

Method. Internal recruitment is quicker and cheaper, the person already knows the business and needs less training, and promotion can motivate staff. But there is a smaller choice, no new ideas, and another vacancy is left behind. External recruitment gives a wider pool of applicants, new skills and fresh ideas. But advertising, shortlisting and interviewing cost time and money, the new person needs induction, and existing staff may feel overlooked. So first decide which route the question asks about. Pick a point that fits the job in the case, develop it through cost, time, training, ideas or motivation, then link it to the business.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one in June twenty twenty-five. Analyse the benefits to Four Fitness of recruiting externally. Six marks: two chains, both about Four Fitness. First, the context. Four Fitness, owned by Gianni, has fourteen gym instructors. It is opening a third gym and needs a Marketing Manager, and its website only gives information. Chain one. External recruitment gives a wider pool of applicants. The instructors are fitness experts, not marketers, so Gianni can choose someone experienced in marketing gyms. The new gym attracts members sooner, and earns revenue sooner. Chain two. An outsider brings new ideas from other businesses, such as letting members book classes online. That gives Four Fitness a competitive edge and more members across all three gyms. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is giving the wrong route's benefits. Being cheaper and already knowing the business are internal benefits, not external ones. Another mistake is forgetting that promotion leaves a gap. When someone is promoted, their old job must be filled too.

Your turn. Now you try. A supermarket needs a new store manager. Its assistant manager has worked there for eight years. Explain one benefit of recruiting internally for this job. Pause and decide.

Answer. The assistant manager already knows the store, its staff and its systems, so little training is needed and they can start straight away. The store keeps running smoothly, and other staff see that promotion is possible, which motivates them. Remember the method: decide internal or external, pick a point that fits this job, then follow it through to the business. Now try the practice questions.
