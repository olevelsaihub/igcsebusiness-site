Idea. Think of a famous burger or coffee chain. Many of its outlets are not run by the big company at all. They are run by local owners who pay to use the brand's name, menu and way of working. This is a franchise. The franchisor owns the brand and sells the right to use it. The franchisee buys that right and runs the outlet. The franchisee pays a set-up fee at the start, and usually ongoing royalties, often a percentage of sales or profit.

Method. Step one. Decide whose side the question takes: the franchisor, who owns the brand, or the franchisee, who buys into it. Step two. Pick one clear feature, such as support and training, an established brand, or fee income. Step three. Chain it to an outcome: a lower risk of failure, more customers, more revenue, or faster growth.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one R in June 2019. Explain one advantage of being a franchisee. It is worth three marks: one advantage and two linked steps. Mark one is the advantage. The franchisee runs a business idea that has already been shown to work. Mark two is the link. The franchisor gives a well-known brand, plus training and advice, so the franchisee does not learn everything by trial and error. Mark three is the consequence. Customers already recognise the brand and costly mistakes are avoided, so the new business is less likely to fail.

Common mistake. Mistake one: mixing up the two words. The franchisor owns the brand. The franchisee buys the right to use it. Mistake two: thinking a franchisee has full control. It must follow the franchisor's rules, buy from approved suppliers, and has no guaranteed wage.

Your turn. Now you try. FreshBowl owns a salad brand with ten outlets. It wants to open fifty more by selling franchises. Explain one advantage to FreshBowl, the franchisor. Pause and decide.

Answer. One advantage is fast growth using other people's money. The franchisees pay to set up the new outlets, so FreshBowl does not need to raise that money itself. It can reach new areas quickly and earn royalty income from every outlet. Remember the method: decide whose side, pick one feature, then chain it to an outcome. Now try the practice questions.
