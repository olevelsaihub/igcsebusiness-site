Economies and diseconomies of scale

Idea. Imagine baking one cake. You heat the whole oven and buy a full bag of flour, just for that one cake. Bake ten cakes at once and those costs are shared, so each cake costs less. The cost of making one item is the average cost, or unit cost. It is total cost divided by the number of units. Two hundred units costing one thousand dollars is five dollars each. One thousand units costing three thousand dollars is three dollars each. Look at these example figures. When a business grows and its average cost per unit falls, that is economies of scale. If it grows too big and average cost starts to rise again, that is diseconomies of scale.

Method. Step one: decide the direction. Average cost falling as the firm grows means economies of scale. Average cost rising means diseconomies. Mixing them up loses the mark. Internal economies come from inside the firm, such as buying materials in bulk at a discount, or borrowing more cheaply. External economies come from outside, such as a whole industry growing in one area, so skilled workers and suppliers are close by. Diseconomies have three common causes. Poor communication across many layers or countries. Slow decisions and paperwork, called bureaucracy. And workers who feel unimportant and lose motivation. For an Explain question, give one cause, then two linked steps that end in higher or lower cost per unit. Do not list several causes.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question from Paper two in June 2023. Explain one reason why diseconomies of scale may occur. It is worth three marks: one reason, then two linked steps. Mark one is the reason. The business becomes so large that each worker feels like a tiny, unimportant part of it. Mark two links it. Managers are far away and rarely notice their work, so the workers lose motivation and stop making extra effort. Mark three gives the result. They work more slowly and make more mistakes, so output per worker falls and the average cost of each unit rises. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The biggest mistake is saying diseconomies mean total costs rise. Every growing firm has higher total costs. The idea is about the average cost of each unit. Another mistake is listing causes, such as communication, motivation and bureaucracy, without explaining any. That earns only one mark.

Your turn. Now you try. A chain of pharmacies grows from five shops to fifty. It now buys medicines in huge orders and gets a discount. Is this an economy or a diseconomy of scale, and is it internal or external? Pause and decide.

Answer. It is an internal economy of scale, called a purchasing economy. The discount comes from the firm's own bigger orders, so each box of medicine costs less, and average cost per unit falls. Remember the method: decide whether cost per unit falls or rises, name the cause, then link it step by step to average cost. Now try the practice questions.
