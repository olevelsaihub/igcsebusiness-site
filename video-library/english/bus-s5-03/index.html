Production methods: job, batch and flow

Read the narration

Think of three things to eat. A wedding cake made to one couple's design. A tray of cheese sandwiches, then a tray of egg sandwiches. And millions of identical chocolate bars. Each is made in a different way. Job production makes one complete item at a time, often to a customer's own design. Batch production makes a set number of identical items, then changes the equipment for the next batch. Flow production moves identical items continuously along a production line, in huge numbers.

Step one: ask two questions. How many items are needed? And how much variety do customers want? Few and unique suggests job. Some variety at medium numbers suggests batch. Huge numbers of the same suggests flow. Each method has a strength and a weakness. Job gives unique products and motivated, skilled workers, but it is slow and costly per item. Batch gives some variety at a moderate cost, but time is lost changing equipment. Flow gives very low cost per unit, but it is expensive to set up, inflexible, and the work can be boring. Step three: use the case. Name the business's product and customers. For an Explain question, give one reason, then two linked steps. Never open with a definition.

Here is a real exam question from Paper two in June 2021. Explain one reason why a business would use batch production. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the reason. The business sells many versions of a product, such as different sizes or colours, but does not need huge numbers of any one version. Mark two links it. After one batch is finished, the equipment is reset to make the next size or colour. Mark three gives the result. Customers get plenty of choice, without the high cost per item of making every product one at a time. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is describing batch as many items made at once. That misses the key idea: switching between different products. Another mistake is thinking job production is fast or cheap. It is slow and costly per item, because each one is made separately.

Now you try. A juice company makes five thousand bottles of orange juice, cleans the machines, then makes five thousand bottles of mango juice. Which production method is this? Give one drawback. Pause and decide.

It is batch production: a set number of one flavour, then a switch to the next. A drawback: no juice is made while the machines are cleaned, so time is lost and the cost of each bottle rises. Remember the method: ask how many and how much variety, choose the method, then link it to one effect for the business in the question. Now try the practice questions.
