Idea. Think about a group project. When everyone cares, the work is done quickly and well. When people don't care, mistakes creep in and some drop out. Motivated employees work harder, make fewer mistakes and give better customer service. They are also more likely to stay. Keeping staff is called retaining them.

Method. Staff turnover is the rate at which employees leave and have to be replaced. High turnover is costly, because every replacement means advertising, interviewing and training again. Staff who stay build up product knowledge and skills they pass on to customers and colleagues. That is why some businesses pay above the minimum wage: to attract better applicants, reward skilled staff, and stop them moving to competitors. To explain, give one reason: higher productivity, better service, lower staff turnover, or attracting skilled staff. Explain how motivation leads to it, then carry the chain to costs, sales or profit.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper one R in June twenty twenty-two. Explain one benefit for a business of motivating its employees. It is worth three marks. Mark one is the benefit. Motivated employees are more likely to stay, so staff turnover is lower. Mark two explains it. The business does not have to keep advertising vacancies, interviewing applicants and training replacements. Mark three is the consequence. It saves time and money that can be used elsewhere, and experienced staff give better service, helping sales. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is explaining how to motivate, such as paying a bonus, when the question asks why motivation matters. Another mistake is writing that staff work harder, then stopping. Carry the chain on to an effect on the business.

Your turn. Now you try. A hair salon loses three stylists every year to rival salons. Explain one benefit to the salon of keeping its stylists motivated. Pause and decide.

Answer. Motivated stylists are more likely to stay, so staff turnover falls. The salon recruits and trains fewer new stylists, and regular customers keep the stylist they trust. So hiring costs fall, and customers keep coming back. Remember the chain: give one reason, explain how motivation causes it, then finish with costs, sales or profit. Now try the practice questions.
