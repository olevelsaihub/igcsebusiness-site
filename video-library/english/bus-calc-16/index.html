Read the narration

Two friends start businesses. Amir invests ten thousand dollars and makes one thousand dollars profit in a year. Bella invests fifty thousand dollars and makes two thousand dollars. Bella made more profit. But who did better with the money they put in? Amir earns one thousand from ten thousand, which is ten per cent. Bella earns two thousand from fifty thousand, which is only four per cent. Amir gets a better return on each dollar invested. The long-term money invested in a business is called capital employed. It is the owners' money in the business, including profit kept back from past years, plus long-term borrowing, such as a five-year bank loan.

Return on capital employed, or ROCE, is operating profit divided by capital employed, times one hundred. The formula is given in the exam. Higher is better. Step one, find operating profit. If it is not given, take operating expenses away from gross profit. Step two, find capital employed, and ignore other lines such as current assets. Step three, divide, multiply by one hundred, and round as asked.

Here is a real question from Paper one R, June twenty twenty-four, about Café Pinch in Berlin. Calculate, to two decimal places, the return on capital employed. It is worth two marks. From the figure, operating profit is eighty-four thousand and fifty-four euros, and capital employed is two hundred and sixty-six thousand and fifty-eight euros. The current assets and current liabilities are not in the formula, so we leave them out. Set it up: eighty-four thousand and fifty-four divided by two hundred and sixty-six thousand and fifty-eight, times one hundred. That gives thirty-one point five nine two and more. To two decimal places, thirty-one point five nine per cent. Café Pinch makes almost thirty-two cents of operating profit for every euro invested.

A common mistake is putting the ratio upside down: capital employed divided by operating profit. That gives over three hundred per cent, which should look wrong. Another mistake is dividing by the wrong line, such as current assets, or using gross profit on top. ROCE always uses operating profit and capital employed.

Now you try. A bike repair business has operating profit of eighteen thousand six hundred dollars, current assets of twenty-two thousand dollars, and capital employed of one hundred and thirty-five thousand dollars. Calculate its ROCE to two decimal places. Pause here and work it out.

Ignore the current assets. Eighteen thousand six hundred divided by one hundred and thirty-five thousand, times one hundred, is thirteen point seven seven seven and more. To two decimal places, thirteen point seven eight per cent. Remember the method: find operating profit, find capital employed, then divide, multiply by one hundred and round.
