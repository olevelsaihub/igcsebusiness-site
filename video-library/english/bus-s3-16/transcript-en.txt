Imagine a juice stall. It sells fifty cups at one dollar each, so it receives fifty dollars. That is revenue: the money from selling, price times the number sold. The fruit, cups and stall cost thirty dollars. Profit is what is left after costs: fifty minus thirty is twenty dollars. If costs were bigger than revenue, the stall would make a loss.

Every way to raise profit works through three levers: the price, the number sold, and the costs. Raise revenue, or cut costs. Each lever has a trade-off. A higher price may lose customers. Cheaper materials may lower quality. Good answers show you know this. To explain a method, name one specific action, show how it changes revenue or costs, then show how that changes profit.

Here is a real question from Paper one in June 2022. Explain one method a small business might use to increase its profit. It is worth three marks: one method, then two linked steps. Mark one is a specific method. The business buys its stock from a cheaper supplier. Mark two is the first link. Each item now costs less, so the gap between the cost of an item and its selling price gets bigger. Mark three is the second link. If it sells the same number at the same price, revenue stays the same while total costs fall, so profit rises.

A common mistake is defining profit as money coming in. Money coming in from sales is revenue. Profit is what is left after all costs. Another mistake is just writing increase sales, with no method. Say how, for example advertising on social media, then show the extra revenue is more than the extra cost, so profit rises.

Now you try. A hairdresser charges twenty dollars a haircut and does thirty haircuts a week. Its weekly costs are four hundred and fifty dollars. Calculate its weekly profit, then suggest one way to raise it. Pause and decide.

Revenue is twenty times thirty, which is six hundred dollars. Profit is six hundred minus four hundred and fifty, which is one hundred and fifty dollars a week. One way: buy shampoo from a cheaper supplier. Costs fall, and if the price and number of haircuts stay the same, profit rises. Remember: method, then revenue or costs, then profit.
