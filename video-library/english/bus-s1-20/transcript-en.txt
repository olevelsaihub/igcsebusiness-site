Idea. Every day you use things the government pays for: the road to school, the school itself, street lights, the police and hospitals. These are public services. Where does the money come from? Mainly from taxes. People and businesses pay taxes, and the government spends that revenue on public services. The government also passes laws, called legislation. Laws often protect people, such as customers, workers or the public, and they can force businesses to change what they do.

Method. Step one. Name the government action: is it spending, a tax, or a law? Step two. Say what it forces or allows a business or the public to do. Step three. Follow it to an outcome, such as costs, prices, demand, health, or government spending.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper two R in June 2022. Analyse why some governments have introduced legislation to reduce the amount of sugar in chocolate. It is worth six marks, so we need two connected points. Point one is health. Too much sugar can make people overweight and cause illnesses such as diabetes and tooth decay. If makers like Cadbury must cut the sugar in Dairy Milk, customers take in less sugar, even without changing their habits. Point two is cost. When fewer people suffer from sugar-related illness, fewer need treatment. This lowers government spending on health care, so money can go to other public services, such as schools. A final link: a law applies to every chocolate maker, so no single firm loses out to rivals by making its bars healthier.

Common mistake. Mistake one: thinking higher taxes are used to raise demand or cut unemployment. Taxes raise revenue, and higher taxes usually reduce spending. Mistake two: calling a private business, such as a shop, a public service. Public services are provided by the government.

Your turn. Now you try. A government passes a law that every bicycle courier must wear a safety helmet. QuickRide employs forty couriers. Explain one effect of this law on QuickRide. Pause and decide.

Answer. One effect is higher costs. QuickRide must buy helmets for forty couriers and check that they wear them. This raises its costs, so its profit may fall unless it raises prices. On the positive side, fewer injuries could mean fewer days lost and a better reputation. Remember the method: name the action, say what it forces or allows, then follow it to an outcome for the business or the public. Now try the practice questions.
